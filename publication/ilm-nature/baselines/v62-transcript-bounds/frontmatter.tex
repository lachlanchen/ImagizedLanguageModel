\newcommand{\PaperTitle}{Learning conditional language\\in continuous image space}
\newcommand{\PaperSummary}{Written language includes historical, handwritten and regional forms that a fixed character inventory cannot faithfully represent. We investigate language modelling with writing images as input and output on one consumer GPU. A continuous image interface supports normalized raster likelihood and independent generation without a deployed character vocabulary. Joint reader--density learning improves target likelihood to \JointNll\ versus \ControlNll\ nats per image, while autonomous streams remain inaccurate. A law over complete observed image answers enables limited Chinese reading: 25 of 256 fixed development answers are exact, versus three with question pixels blanked and 21 after matched additional glyph-answer training. Raster F1 reaches 27.033\%, a 4.893-point gain [0.469, 9.047], while the exact-match difference remains unresolved. Both questions are correct in only four of 393 shared-paragraph pairs. Full adaptation of a 477M-parameter native pixel-language foundation improves pixel likelihood but produces zero exact answers. A matched whole-image mixture further lowers held-out NLL from 0.1542 to 0.1248 nats per pixel, yet still gives zero exact answers and no resolved question-dependent raster gain. Independent runtimes reproduce the raw outputs. Faithful forms and likelihood require separate tests from useful language; a high-quality general image-based language model remains unresolved.}
