\paragraph{Paired supplementary glosses and image probability.}
The starting writer had 2,251,925 parameters and had trained on 55,872 aligned pictured sentence pairs. A local Q8 Qwen3-VL 8B produced alternative modern glosses from typed classical sources in 512 training chapters. One output reached its length cap. We retained the remaining 511 source--archive--teacher triples. Its 9.83-GB weights took 216 seconds of reported model time to create the training targets. Both fine-tuning arms used the same initial weights, optimizer, 12 shuffled passes, 192 updates and minibatches containing both target streams for each of 32 sources. Both adjusted 836,245 parameters. Replaying two archive streams required 4.0 seconds and the supplemented arm 3.0 seconds on the RTX 4090 D, exclusive of the parent training and teacher. The corresponding target exposures per pass were 22,670 and 23,215 cells. At inference, each student read solely the source image and fed its own output image back at each step. Of the supplemented student's 27,774 emitted glyphs, 9,071 used the source pointer, 18,686 an exemplar and seventeen the open drawing route.

We excluded previous evaluation and training chapters and repeated source bitmaps. The same teacher generated evaluation-only glosses after the answers were saved. One capped teacher target was excluded from the second physical comparison. We resampled chapters 2,000 times, paired by source. The eighteen reading cases comprised six per source-marker stratum, each from a distinct chapter. One study-involved model reader judged the complete modern meaning of randomized alternatives alongside the original classical passage, before method assignments were revealed. The follow-up reading of pooled answers reused the same passages and reader. Neither reading constitutes independent human adjudication.

For visible ink $g$, let $p_c,p_e,p_d$ denote the probabilities of copying, choosing an exemplar and drawing. Given attention $a_j$ over source images $x_j$ and probabilities $b_k$ over exemplar images $v_k$, a lower bound is
\[
 L(g)=p_c\sum_{j:x_j=g}a_j+p_e\sum_{k:v_k=g}b_k .
\]
The missing mass on any ink is at most $p_d$. END has its exact route probability. We emit a candidate when its lower bound exceeds every competing upper bound, including that of undrawn ink. Otherwise we retain the original route decision. This rule certified 997 of 1,024 first decisions and changed 222. The independent CPU replay reproduced all original first marks. No reference, teacher, character label or new parameter enters this decoder.
