\paragraph{Reading from teacher probabilities.}
The optical teacher, fitted before the novel study, recovered 207 of 452 Chinese answers and 37 of 452 English answers without supplied candidates. We distilled its distribution over source intervals into a new 64,666-parameter reader and simultaneously trained the reader on human answer images from 6,439 passages. The 32 retained intervals contained a mean of 88.2\% of teacher mass. On the two reserved works the matched student produced 104 exact Chinese and 45 exact English answers, compared with 23 and seven for the earlier small reader. It answered both different questions about the same page eight times in Chinese and never in English (Fig.~\ref{fig:book-span-distillation}). Local text Qwen3-8B completed 121 and 112 such pairs. Each output from the small reader is a strip of original source ink; selecting the wrong strip still produces legible characters.

When we exchanged teacher targets between questions about the same page and kept human targets correct, the control produced 88 Chinese and 59 English answers. The matched-minus-exchanged difference was 3.5 percentage points for Chinese (95\% paired interval $-0.4$ to 7.5) and $-3.1$ points for English ($-6.4$ to 0.2). The larger training set and teaching objective together improved on the earlier small reader. Neither language met the registered criterion for improvement attributable to question-specific teacher distributions. The matched fit required 66.7 seconds and 7.50 kJ of device energy; preparing the teacher probabilities required a further 53.9 seconds and 16.03 kJ. These costs exclude original teacher pretraining.

A subsequent student fitted to the same human answers without teacher targets recovered 81 Chinese and 48 English answers. It answered both questions on four Chinese passages and zero English passages. On 96 separately authored questions with edited narratives, the released matched student correctly returned 12 of 48 Chinese names and three of 48 English names. For example, a passage placed the role of protecting a picture frame with one participant and labelling boxes with another. The reader correctly selected the latter when asked who marked the boxes. The released input and output images reproduce this example on CPU. Most of the remaining questions were still answered incorrectly.
