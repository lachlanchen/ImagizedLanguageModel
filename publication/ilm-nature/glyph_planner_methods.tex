\paragraph{Recurrent planning over completed drawings.}
The passage reader produces a vector $c$. An outer recurrent state starts at $h_0=c$ and advances as $h_{t+1}=\mathrm{GRU}(h_t,[c,R(y_t)])$, where $R$ samples 48 fixed pixel locations on the emitted tile $y_t$. A sigmoid head estimates the stop probability from $h_t$. At each glyph an inner recurrent state starts at $h_t$ and predicts twelve successive groups of four landmark bits. Its inputs include the preceding pattern, group position, earlier landmark bits from the same tile and $h_t$. The frozen renderer maps the 48 selected bits to a binary $32\times32$ tile. Both recurrences receive image measurements, and no expression inventory is queried at inference. The full model has 2,366,148 parameters, of which 950,468 were trained.

We initialized matching parameters from the released reader and writer and fit the two arms for four passes over 142,878 training windows. Both arms used the same examples, ordering, optimizer and 2,980 updates. In the shuffled arm the assignment of the whole passage to the answer changed each epoch. Each target contained 48 four-bit pattern decisions and five binary stop decisions. The summed negative log likelihood was divided by 53. During fitting, past glyphs were supplied through the physical renderer. In free writing the model reread its own emitted ink. Each boundary admitted a learned stop, up to eight glyphs. We compared committing one glyph with retaining four answer histories across boundaries, using the same four internal search paths in both cases. Passing the known history was a separate diagnostic.

For the final comparison we trained the aligned planner for four additional passes in two arms. Both computed the conditional probabilities of the frozen uniform writer on the same target trajectory. The reference arm used only the observed targets. The transfer arm assigned equal weight to the observed-target loss and the teacher's cross-entropy on the 16 physical patterns and the stop decision. Both ran the student alone at inference. All intervals resampled source groups and paired the outputs for each passage. Output length was learned on a corpus of four-character expressions.
