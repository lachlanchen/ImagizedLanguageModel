\paragraph{Writing a changed fact from a pictured passage.}
A causal writer received the original passage and question as glyph images and emitted its own reply. With one training instruction for reporting a participant and a year, it answered both versions of 76/96 single-paragraph date edits under familiar wording. Rephrasing the request reduced the complete count to zero. The year itself was correct in both versions on 96/96 pairs with familiar wording and 11/96 after rephrasing. A model trained from the same weights on the same target images with twelve formulations recovered 80/96 complete pairs under the rephrased request. Across two competing paragraphs, it passed all four required replies on 15/64 boards.

We then presented three new question formulations for each of 64 new single passages and 48 new two-paragraph boards (Fig.~\ref{fig:page-evidence}). The varied writer answered every formulation and year assignment correctly on 34/64 single passages and 8/48 two-paragraph boards. The writer trained with one detail instruction scored zero on both joint tests. In a successful pair the varied writer named the same author and changed only the reported year. In a failed two-paragraph case it kept the correct actor but drew the year from the other paragraph.

The varied model returned both factual word orders on 168/256 DuIE and 9/29 CMRC questions. A matched control supplied with the correct fields scored 245 and 23, respectively. Both page models reproduced 92/128 attested clauses. A whole-page copy with normalized terminal punctuation would match 125/128. With two competing source clauses, the varied model returned six exact quotations on 256 requests and passed zero of 64 boards requiring selection in both paragraph orders. On the broader 359 human questions it produced seven exact stopped replies, compared with 39 from its initial writer on the same page inputs. It reproduced 18/192 historical-form answers with familiar wording and zero of 192 rephrasings. These results support transfer of a pictured factual writing task under controlled changes to the source. Selecting evidence across paragraphs and maintaining broad question answering remain unresolved.
