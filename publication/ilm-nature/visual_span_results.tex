\paragraph{Source order predicts the contents of an inserted phrase.}
We trained two pictured writers on the same sentence edits. One learned a minimum-edit alignment, the other summed the probability of all compatible edit paths. In two fresh cohorts containing 5,376 and 5,413 recorded insertion spans, summed-path training increased the number reproduced exactly in autonomous single-path output from 193 to 257 and from 172 to 227. With the true output history and cursor supplied, exact drawn spans rose from 231 to 279 and from 212 to 251. With the edit action also required to be correct, the corresponding counts were 74 versus 94 and 65 versus 73. This gain came with a substantial loss of stopping accuracy. Only 535 of 1,022 and 528 of 1,017 renderable translations from the marginal writer stopped. Its aggregate glyph error reached 294.97\% and 296.49\% because repeated insertions exceeded the reference lengths.

The learned distribution still distinguished competing meanings and usages. We exchanged inserted phrases between pairs of sentences while preserving their lengths, immediate modern neighbors and source-absent glyphs. The marginal writer preferred the recorded assignment on 111/154 and 102/139 pairs. Reversing the classical source reduced those counts to 81 and 80. The joint preference for the recorded assignment fell by a mean 1.70 and 1.91 nats per pair (95\% paired bootstrap intervals 1.08--2.39 and 1.25--2.65). With the remainder of the modern reference withheld, the original assignments were still preferred in 112 and 98 pairs. The marginal writer also preferred the correct joint assignment in four constructed agent--patient exchanges, but freely produced neither pair of exact answers together in any of the four. These comparisons test the influence of source order on a constrained phrase choice.

Summing over compatible cursors when selecting the next image let both writers stop and quote correctly on all 2,039 renderable cases. The marginal writer's translation errors fell to 54.96\% and 58.43\%, close to the local count rule at 55.27\% and 58.33\%. Its fifteen exact translations were all unchanged sources (Fig.~\ref{fig:visual-span}). Choosing a stopped candidate by estimated edit risk gave 55.13\% and 58.41\% and recovered no complete changed reference. Of 65,248 sampled translations, all stopped. At least one of the 32 draws recovered the complete changed reference on only two discovery inputs and zero replication inputs. A single modern reference permits exact pixel accounting but understates valid alternative phrasings.
