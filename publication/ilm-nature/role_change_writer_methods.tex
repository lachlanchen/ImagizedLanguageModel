\paragraph{Assignment supervision on paired passage images.}
We chose 1,024 DuIE training passages and 128 development passages with separate work titles and two distinct annotated credits for the same work. Each role had one annotated participant and each name occurred once. Exchanging the two names produced a second account with the same character counts. A local Qwen3-4B model proposed pairs of Chinese action phrases and English glosses from 671 training passages. Format and lexical checks accepted 591 pairs. After deduplication, 588 supplied actions for fictional accounts of role assignment, changes of plan and corrected reports. Each of these three categories supplied 588 further paired scenes. Neither the fictional wording nor the edited credits constitute a historical source.

Both fits started from the same 87.1-million-parameter bilingual glyph writer. Their 49,340 presentations included 2,684 book requests and 2,048 historical-form requests. For each edited scene we showed the two page versions, two orders and two question languages twice. Aligned supervision assigned the supported statement on both exposures. Independent supervision showed both possible answers once on each identical input. The input order, per-example target lengths and the answer counts within each scene, language and question order were identical. We updated the upper four transformer blocks, their final normalization and the writer's pointer. At inference, the page and the two pictured questions, separated by a printed bar, condition successive physical output cells and the stop decision.

For a fixed predictor, let $L_{ij}$ denote the negative log probability of stopped answer $j$ on page $i$. The independent-label loss less the aligned loss is $(L_{01}-L_{00})+(L_{10}-L_{11})$. It vanishes for any predictor that assigns the same answer distribution to both pages. For a binary squared-error predictor with label scale $s$ and scores $u,v$ on the two pages, the gain is $2s(u-v)$. Lean~4 verifies these identities in common integer units. They isolate the supervision introduced by the edit. A positive aggregate gain can coexist with an incorrect decision on an individual page.

We evaluated both versions and both question orders in Chinese and English on the 128 held-out credits and twelve separately worded stories, four each about roles, temporal changes and corrections. Earlier pretraining exposure to the credited works is unknown. The stricter contrast metric required all four answers for a scene in one language to stop and contain both names exactly once in question order. For semantic reading we fixed one question order per story before evaluation and kept both page versions and languages. A single model-based reader judged the resulting 48 requests with system identity hidden, accepting supported paraphrases when both clauses answered their respective questions in sequence. The reader had prior familiarity with the study. The text comparison received the passage and questions as text; the visual writers were evaluated on their emitted images.

The score-level diagnostic evaluated both canonical answer images, including their stop, with their own preceding glyphs supplied. We used the same two person names and the same actions in both alternatives. The subsequent wording diagnostic reused each story's annotated actions, names and two assignments, and manually translated the twelve pairs of actions for the English prompts. Each writer answered all three additional input combinations without additional fitting. The rewrites expose the underlying facts by construction and measure sensitivity to presentation.
