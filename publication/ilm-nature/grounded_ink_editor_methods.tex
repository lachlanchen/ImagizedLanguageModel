\paragraph{Recurrent editing of source ink.}
A frozen optical reader located candidate source ink independently for two pictured questions. The editor received the entire page, the two inclusive source ranges in question order, and a third raster strip specifying a short or expanded response. When a retrieved spelling recurred, we used its first occurrence. Each 32 by 32 input cell was average-pooled to eight by eight. A one-dimensional convolution contextualized its visual features and its distances to both boundaries of both regions. A 64-dimensional recurrent decoder selected one of three actions. A copy action scored both endpoints of an arbitrary contiguous span, constraining the end to follow or equal the start. A write action predicted 1,024 Bernoulli pixel logits and emitted their thresholded image. A stop action terminated the stream. Previous output ink supplied recurrent feedback. Denoting the action by $u$, the start and end by $i,j$, and the new bitmap by $I$, we trained with categorical negative log likelihoods for $u$, $i$ and $j$, and a per-pixel logistic loss for $I$. Boundary loss applied only to copies. Bitmap loss applied only to writes, with weight three on ink pixels and weight two on the mean bitmap term. The editor used raw source pixels on a copy and contained no output glyph table.

We formed training pages of two to four fragments drawn from 7,412 annotated clause--answer records (7,066 distinct strings) of the Kyoto treebank and BiPaR novel corpus. Annotated regions supplied supervision during editing practice. The matched control independently exchanged those two regions on half of presentations; the source, instructions, optimizer and target replies were otherwise identical. Each fit used 2,400 AdamW updates of 48 pages. Testing used twelve further excerpts each from \textit{Journey to the West} and \textit{Dream of the Red Chamber}. Both questions, action worlds, orders, writing formats and strictly scored targets were reserved before training. Short targets gave two names in question order, with a conjunction and final punctuation. Longer targets included both subject--predicate clauses, a connecting phrase and final punctuation. At least one pre-specified connector glyph was absent from every passage. An auxiliary condition supplied the known evidence locations. Chinese format instructions were retained for both question languages. A secondary text comparator received the same questions and an explicit punctuation specification. We measured complete four-reply comparisons by resampling the twelve source excerpts together with their edits and orders. The previously studied two-entry historical plate supplied the illustration. A separate transfer test used every eligible development family with distinct oracle and bronze images, the first eligible entry per family and its first archived tile per stage. We paired adjacent families in hash order as distractors, varied both entry and question order, and checked that entries, families, catalogue labels and tile hashes were absent from the historical training plates. The generic control omitted the headword and stage from both questions.
