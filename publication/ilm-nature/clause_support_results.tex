\paragraph{Which translations can the phrase writer express?}
On 1,024 passages from 235 other chapters, exact search recovered the deployed output among the composer's six highest-scoring distinct streams in every case. Even with permission to select from \emph{every} legal phrase combination, only 31 recorded glosses were reachable (Fig.~\ref{fig:clause-support}). Other phrasings could convey the same meaning. We therefore graded all six proposals for 18 passages before revealing the method assignments. The default output was acceptable once. The alternatives rescued none of its twelve failures for which the recorded gloss was judged faithful. In one case a minister finally dispatched an envoy. The classical glyph used for \emph{finally} also expresses death in the previous experiment. Both the default and closest legal outputs distort the dispatch.

\paragraph{Predicting from one's own writing.}
We trained a 2.25-million-parameter causal writer that reads the source and its own preceding images. On 1,024 new passages it made 21,073 edits in 22,655 target positions, compared with 11,799 for the phrase composer. It recognized the first target image in 400 passages. With the correct preceding images it reproduced 8,840 target positions. Free writing matched only 2,502 at their original absolute positions, a measure sensitive to shifts. At the first wrong emission, substituting that single image into an otherwise correct preceding stream reduced correct predictions of the next image from 442 to 133 across 1,019 cases (Fig.~\ref{fig:visual-feedback}a). The initial writer produced one acceptable modern gloss in the 18-passage reading. Reversing its source increased the edit count to 84,055 and reduced learned stops from 999 to 235.

To test whether practice with these mistakes would improve writing, we continued two copies of the same writer for four passes. The experimental arm saw 1,431,375 substitutions of an erroneous preceding image. On a third set of 1,024 passages, edit error rose from 98.85\% under clean-history training to 194.69\% under substituted-history training. The paired chapter-bootstrap interval for the increase was 87.08 to 104.30 percentage points. The latter writer emitted 52,267 cells for 23,507 reference cells. Both writers produced the same faithful translation of a general's defeat. Each passed one of 18 readings, with 12 acceptable corpus glosses in the same packet. Both lost the subject of a prediction about a man's death (Fig.~\ref{fig:visual-feedback}c).
