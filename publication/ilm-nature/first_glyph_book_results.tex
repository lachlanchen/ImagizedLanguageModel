\paragraph{Weighting the first glyph yields one additional exact literary reply.}
On 34 independently sourced passages, ordinary supervision gave 47 correct initial glyphs and 30 exact stopped replies in 136 requests. Extra weight on the first glyph gave 48 and 31. Equal extra weight on a later action gave 42 and 29. All three conditions completed zero passages when each of two questions had to be answered in both languages (Fig.~\ref{fig:first-glyph-book}). The first-action writer answered 24 of 68 Chinese requests and seven of 68 English requests exactly. On Lu Xun's account of buying medicine, it gave the father as the recipient when asked in Chinese. The equivalent English question elicited reed root, an ingredient mentioned later in the passage. Both answers are legible and both occur on the page.

The first-action writer gave four exact answers on three of 374 BiPaR passages and one of 227 regular-print XQuAD passages, equal to both controls. On the separate literary passages, 17 of the 48 replies beginning with an acceptable glyph subsequently failed, including nine that continued past a reference answer. The writer retained 16 of 197 complete CMRC pairs and 99 of 100 familiar historical headwords. On 300 requests requiring absent content ink it gave zero canonical complete replies. Its 2,496 updates on 49,283 examples took 176.8 seconds and 4.50 GiB peak allocated memory on one RTX 4090 D. The later-action control took 175.4 seconds. The current selection rule retains the ordinary bilingual writer.

On the same 136 requests, locally served Qwen3-8B Q8 received text and produced 60 of 68 exact Chinese-request and 59 of 68 exact English-request replies, completing 21 of 34 passages. The visual writer reproduced its archived answers in warm serial inference, taking 5.30 seconds over both languages versus 26.38 seconds for the text model at higher accuracy. In a separate eight-request illustration where two households exchanged dictionary counts, the first-action writer gave six correct replies and the later-action writer gave four. These measurements do not establish an efficiency advantage at matched answer quality.

For the retained ordinary writer, we replayed all 833 observed greedy actions on the literary questions. The teacher-forced maximum at each observed prefix agreed with the archived action. Among 104 wrong stopped replies, the emitted sequence had higher joint probability than every accepted reference in 94 cases. An accepted reference had higher probability in ten. Two further misses reached the length cap. Across all 136 requests, the preferred accepted answer outranked the other question's best reference 97 times. In the 94 strict losses, even exact global maximization of the model's stopped-sequence probability could not select an accepted reference. The discrepancy therefore implicates the learned conditional distribution as well as the search procedure.
