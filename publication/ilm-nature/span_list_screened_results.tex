\subsection*{Recovering answers beyond the first physical span}
Keeping eight spans from each reader made more answers available. To learn which ones to emit, we fitted a question-conditioned scorer to the distinct proposed locations and pooled mass on identical strips (Fig.~\ref{fig:span-list}). A source audit uncovered a split error in the preceding studies. The official DRCD test file had been reserved for later confirmation, yet it supplied 19 human factual questions, 128 broad transfer questions, and 192 and 191 reserved questions in the two subsequent mixed evaluations. We traced at least 530 distinct question IDs to 529 reserved paragraphs. Seven training records also shared a normalized 64-character segment with the reservation. The earlier DRCD results describe exposed material. They cannot establish independent confirmation. We retained the original weights and scores, screened the training records, and trained all three comparison heads again.

On the replacement development sample of 192 CMRC and 167 DRCD questions, widening produced 234/359 exact replies. The frozen generic reader produced 242, the matched depth-one scorer 238 and shuffled supervision 91. Against the generic reader, the wider fit gained 16 correct answers and lost 24 (two-sided paired exact test, $P=0.268$). Among 56 questions whose answers first appeared below the top proposal, it recovered 12. Of the 125 errors, 78 had an accepted answer in the wider pool and 47 had none. One reply correctly supplied both requested game modes. Another correctly named the committee chair but added her professional title. The image comparison counted the extra glyphs as an error.

The writer passed both requested word orders on 15/29 eligible human factual questions and 925/1,200 independent DuIE cases. It passed every question on 70/128 name exchanges and 36/48 reordered boards. On 32 substitutions of historical name images, its induced statements matched 13 references. None of the prespecified performance comparisons for the screened refit passed.

A separate date edit tested an information constraint in the writer. Across 32 pairs of passages with different years, supplying the same correct subject, relation and object gave identical output pixels on every pair and zero correct year pairs. The direct reader given the full pictured passage answered both versions correctly on two pairs. In a different free-writing test, 58/64 replies stopped naturally, 45 contained both annotated names, and none exactly reproduced the full source clause. Exact quotation is a narrow test of source reproduction. The change-of-year test isolates a stronger failure: information omitted from the writer's input cannot control its answer.
