\subsection*{Writing about a participant whose role changes}
Changing the assignment of two participants while retaining both predicates gave a direct supervision signal for writing about one of them. A 694,152-parameter writer learned from 1,035 predicate--complement pairs extracted from original Chinese claims. When asked about a named participant, it had to produce the relevant predicate and complement and place the participant last. Across 101 new predicate--complement pairs with new names, the matched model wrote 368 of 404 complete answers, compared with 186 when participant assignments were randomized and zero for the inherited model (Fig.~\ref{fig:visual-role-writer}). Both answers were right on 179 of 202 passage exchanges versus 54 in the randomized control. For 80 of 101 situations all four combinations of passage and request were correct, compared with zero complete squares for either control. Changing both the layout and the request reduced whole-answer accuracy to 297/404 and complete squares to 40/101.

Transfer to independently worded situations was limited. On the previous role-exchange judgments the matched model answered both versions on five of 48 pairs, versus zero for each control (matched advantage over randomized training, 10.4 percentage points, situation-bootstrap 95\% interval 2.1--20.8). On eight newly authored situations it answered both versions on only one of 32 pairs (interval 0--9.4 points). The joint transfer criterion failed. A follow-up kept the 40 affirmative passage pairs and used the source wording for each claim. The same four pairs still passed. Explicitly negating the other participant's property in the source reduced this to one. In natural inference the exact count fell from 1,610 to 1,533 of 2,947; missing text fell from 524 to 512. Across the fixed 105 triple members the matched model gave 57 correct judgments and Qwen3-8B gave 73. The adaptation yielded zero complete DuReader references and reduced English dictionary writing from 48 to 29 of 96.

When given eight invented facts about printed Qin and Chu, the matched writer produced all 32 reordered descriptions. Replacing both printed names with attested bronze images outside its fixed shape memory yielded 24/32, compared with 16 for randomized training and zero for the parent. Only eight of 16 bronze pairs were correct in both passage orders. In the upper row of panel c, the writer uses the right predicate but copies Chu in place of Qin. In the lower row it writes the correct negative predicate and Qin. Asked to identify a participant from its property without the name in the request, the matched writer gave zero exact names in either script. The two adaptations took 126.5 and 123.4 seconds on one RTX 4090 D after inherited training.
