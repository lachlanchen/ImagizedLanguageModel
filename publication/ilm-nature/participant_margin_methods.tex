\subsection*{Learning a visual span in the presence of a competing name}
For a candidate span $a=(i,j)$, use its predicted start score plus its predicted end score $S(a)$. With target $t$, annotated rival $r$ and admissible spans $A$ of length 1--48 cells, minimize
\[
\log\sum_{a\in A} \exp S(a)-S(t)
\quad +\quad [1+S(r)-S(t)]_+ .
\]
The likelihood is averaged over every question and the second term over questions with a rival. The role-specific correction combines contextual cell features, a five-cell convolution and a learned embedding of the requested role. Its zero-initialized final layer initially reproduces the original span scores. The writing model, previously learned subject and relation heads, and visual backbone were frozen. Training supplied labelled roles. Inference supplied predicted roles. The decoder scores all admissible spans without receiving candidate names or reference boundaries.

Lean 4.12.0 checks that the unit-margin penalty bounds the binary error of preferring the rival or tying. The proof also covers rational scores by multiplication by a common positive denominator. Beating every competing span strictly suffices to select the target. If all valid spans on an edited page are covered by a mapping from the original page, and score differences relative to the target are preserved, the edited maximizer selects the mapped target. These are sufficient conditions on scores. They can fail under real name substitutions, and another span can win even when the annotated rival loses.

Training paired 9,600 replay questions with 9,600 questions derived from 1,200 actual two-role DuIE training passages. Four questions per passage varied assignments and requested roles. For 600 passages, eight additional questions used constructed clauses in both orders. All new development subjects and full pages were disjoint from this adaptation and its original reader training split after script normalization. The larger upstream pretraining corpora were outside this overlap check. We trained 122,882 parameters for 2,000 updates over five epochs per arm. The matched fit took 20.8 seconds on one RTX 4090 D, excluding the shared reader and writer pretraining and feature preparation. Newly contextualizing the 9,600 pictured inputs took 13.0 seconds. The control independently randomized target and rival on paired questions and preserved all replay labels. Its updates, batches and initialization were matched.
