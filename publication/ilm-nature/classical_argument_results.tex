\paragraph{Learning grammatical roles from classical prose.}
We trained the same small reader with person and verb relations from the Kyoto treebank. In the \textit{Zuo Tradition}, the aligned model identified 23 of 64 Chinese-request answers and completed one of 16 four-response contrasts. Neither its equal-label control nor the preceding small model completed a contrast. The English questions yielded 15 exact answers and zero complete contrasts. On the earlier 28 excerpts from \textit{A New Account of Tales of the World}, neither language yielded a complete contrast (Fig.~\ref{fig:classical-argument}). Correct modern-novel replies fell to 33 of 452 in Chinese and 26 in English. A comparison of existing models on the same classical pages identified a stronger reader for adaptation. Before updating its weights, the optical contextual reader completed three Chinese contrasts on the \textit{Zuo Tradition} and seven on \textit{A New Account}. The bilingual generative writer completed none in either work or language.
