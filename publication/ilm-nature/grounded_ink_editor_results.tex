\paragraph{Writing from visual evidence.}
We trained a separate editor from scratch to copy any contiguous span, draw a new glyph and decide when to stop. On \textit{Journey to the West}, it produced 34 of 48 exact two-clause replies to pictured Chinese questions, including the connective absent from the source passage. On \textit{Dream of the Red Chamber}, it produced 42 of 48. Randomizing which evidence location belonged to which question during training gave six and four correct replies on the same images. All four replies were correct in six and ten of the twelve excerpts, against zero for both controls (Fig.~\ref{fig:grounded-ink-editor}). The paired 95\% intervals for the differences in complete two-clause replies were 25--75 and 58--100 percentage points. The shorter format naming both people completed five and ten excerpts. With the required punctuation specified in text, Qwen3-8B returned 23 and 18 exact Chinese two-clause answers but no complete excerpt. Four incomplete English or Chinese runtime responses were counted as misses across the full 384-request text comparison. With the reference source locations supplied, the aligned editor reproduced all 384 full images across both works, orders, languages and formats. English requests with autonomous reading yielded two and ten exact two-clause answers and zero complete excerpts. A conditional editor with 220,035 parameters took 37.5 s and 3.54 kJ of device energy to fit on one RTX 4090 D. These costs exclude the optical reader's pretraining and fitting.

On the earlier historical plate, the adapted reader selected the authentic oracle form but missed the bronze form. Its editor reproduced the pair in either order when supplied with the reference locations. In a secondary comparison selected after this error, the unchanged optical parent retrieved both forms autonomously. The same editor returned both authentic shapes, a generated conjunction and punctuation, and stopped in both orders. The parent also supported 38 and 42 exact Chinese two-clause replies on the two novels, and two and two in English. With four further pictured Chinese instructions, two of the four wordings produced both historical orders exactly. With new wordings for the book format, the Chinese requests yielded 72/96 and 72/96 exact short replies, and 76/96 and 74/96 exact longer replies. Across all 60 eligible historical development families, the frozen parent found the oracle form in 120/120 source layouts and the bronze form in 36/120. The editor wrote 74/240 exact stopped replies and completed all four comparisons for 9/60 families. The adapted reader yielded 46/240 replies and 8/60 complete families. A generic question yielded zero complete replies. With the two reference source locations, the same editor produced all 240 exact images. These plates shared the wording and layout used for the earlier example. The novel experiment measures two specified writing formats and retains the wording of the source clauses.
