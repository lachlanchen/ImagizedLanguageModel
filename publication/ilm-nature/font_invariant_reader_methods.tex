\subsection*{A continuous pixel interface to a Chinese contextual reader}
We used CKIP BERT Base Chinese QA (\href{https://huggingface.co/ckiplab/bert-base-chinese-qa}{model card}, revision \texttt{77b85827}, GPL-3.0). We retained its contextual layers, positional and segment information, two learned delimiters, and span boundary scores. During fitting, 7,918 supported single-character vocabulary entries supplied continuous target vectors. At inference the contextual encoder has no lexical embedding table. Rendered questions and passages enter as streams of $32\times32$ binary images, and the output is the original sequence of image cells maximizing the sum of start and end scores for a legal span of at most 64 cells. Long passages use overlapping windows.

For the first interface, normalized ink at three resolutions gave a feature vector $x$. A fixed Gaussian projection $W$ produced $h(x)=\sqrt{2/D}\,\mathrm{ReLU}(xW)$ with $D=24{,}576$. Given feature rows $H$ and target rows $E$, we solved ridge regression $A=H^{\mathsf T}(HH^{\mathsf T}+\lambda I)^{-1}E$ with $\lambda=0.0003$ and Cholesky factorization. The deployed vector is $h(x)A$. We included regular sans and serif renderings, combined identical bitmaps and averaged their distinct target vectors. The 15,743 distinct images contained 25 ambiguous images. The solve took 1.55 seconds and peaked at 4.52 GiB allocated on one RTX 4090 D, excluding rasterization and inherited training.

For font invariance, a six-convolution encoder produced a normalized 192-dimensional representation. A symmetric contrastive loss paired different renderings of the same glyph against 255 other glyph identities in each batch. Two thousand updates covered training examples drawn from seven faces with random one-pixel translations. Neither the Noto Sans bold test face nor the AR PL UKai family entered training. We then learned a regression on 12,288 rectified random features of the metric representation, accumulating the normal equations across every available training view with $\lambda=0.003$. Both fitted maps compute dense functions of pixels and store no enumerated prototype glyphs in the reader. A serialization failure after the final visual metric was saved required restoring that metric and repeating the analytic solve. No subsequent metric updates were made. The recorded checks bound the original fit below 900 seconds and 15 GiB allocated. The exact original peak and optimizer duration were lost at serialization.

For integrated inference the original action gate saw the actual question and passage images and chose between the new reader and its earlier recurrent composer. No task labels selected the action. The native and visual readers used the same maximum span length. For the separate native text comparison, CKIP used its original tokenizer and Qwen3-8B generated a name at temperature zero. Complete answer images were archived before scoring. A wrong-question control exchanged the queries about the same passage. The new scenes measured transfer across wording and name exchange. Reusing them for the font comparison measures interface robustness on fixed content.
