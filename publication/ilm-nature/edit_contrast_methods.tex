\paragraph{Predictive and contrastive drawing objectives.}
We initialized each fit with the 2,026,237-parameter insertion-limited editor. Four further passes each contained 48,000 translations and 12,000 quotation requests. We updated the 242,901 parameters of the recurrent decoder, action projection and within-glyph predictor with AdamW at 0.0003. The encoder, renderer and insertion predictor were frozen. At up to 256 sampled drawing positions per batch, each target competed with seven cyclically shifted drawings from the shuffled sample. We masked alternatives identical to the positive physical code. For a complete drawing $b$ and state $h$ the score was $s(b,h)=\sum_j\log p(b_j\mid h,b_{<j})$. The additional loss was the cross-entropy for choosing the observed drawing from these eight options. The likelihood-only arm ran the same computations with zero auxiliary weight. Its comparator used weight one. A third treatment subtracted the logarithm of the empirical training-draw frequency from each comparison score. At inference every writer generated from its original conditional probabilities.

Two new sentence rosters were selected by hashing source rows within previously unused chapters and deduplicating physical sources. Neither split constrained the modern reference length. Five and eleven requests exceeded rendering coverage. For the source-order intervention we reversed each true-length source before encoding and reflected each decoder cursor, preserving the consulted source glyph, past actions and previous writing. Natural drawing pairs from distinct chapters shared the current source glyph, preceding output glyph and both current and preceding edit operations. Their target glyphs differed. We scored both assignments of the recorded images by complete conditional log probability and independently counted exact generated bitmaps for both members.
