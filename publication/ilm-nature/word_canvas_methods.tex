\subsection*{Masked prediction of word images}
We reserved the next 200 CoQA stories in the earlier hash ordering before fitting. This yielded 3,155 new turns, including 443 with absent multiword phrasing. Both visual continuations started from the published list-trained visual reader; the two byte continuations started from its byte counterpart. Each received identical batches of 32 over 3,200 AdamW updates at 0.0002. The 102,400 exposures included 44,437 distinct fitting turns. Each batch comprised 16 uniform dialogue draws, eight difficult copy assemblies, four turns requiring additional wording and four labelled lists. With one RTX 4090 D, the masked visual fit took 54.7 seconds and 6.95 kJ for 574,021 parameters. The causal visual fit took 50.4 seconds and 6.65 kJ for 547,125 parameters. The masked and causal byte fits took 52.8 and 47.4 seconds (6.87 and 6.47 kJ). Cold-parent training, previous continuation and preparation costs are additional. All intervals condition on one fitted model per arm.

Let $X$ be the input images, $Y=(y_1,\ldots,y_L)$ the target strip and $M$ the hidden positions. We fitted $p(L\mid X)$ by cross entropy. With probability one half the whole strip was masked. On the other examples we sampled a masking rate uniformly and enforced at least one hidden word. Visible slots received the physical word features. Each hidden slot received a learned mask vector, its position and the common length. Bidirectional attention across answer slots and attention over the pictured fields predicted a copy or spelling action. The copy loss summed probability across all edits rendering the same target. The spelling loss supervised successive glyphs and the end of the word. The original passage endpoint loss remained active. Related conditional masking and language diffusion approaches use discrete symbols (Ghazvininejad et al., \url{https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/D19-1633}; Nie et al., \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09992}).

Inference starts with the most likely length, hides all word images, then commits a cumulative $\lceil rL/4\rceil$ words after pass $r$. Under the chosen route we rank a copy by the summed probability of its identical edits and a spelling by the product of its letter and termination probabilities, including the route probability in each case. A committed slot is immutable. We proved in Lean that a target is feasible under independent per-slot output constraints precisely when every commitment agrees and every unfilled slot admits its reference. This is a necessary condition for a neural writer with coupled slots. Literal bitmap differences may still receive normalized credit.

The alternative search used the eight most probable lengths and four fixed orders (confidence, leftmost, rightmost and centre first). After deduplication we ranked stopped proposals by log length probability plus mean committed-word log probability. This is a proposal score. We scored targets only after the completed drafts were saved in memory. For diagnosis we separately supplied the true word count and then, for each word, every other human answer word. We compared literal images in these two assays as well as normalized complete answers for the length-only condition.
