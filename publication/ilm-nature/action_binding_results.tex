\subsection*{Writing individual clauses isolates a failure to assign actions in new stories}
The earlier writer favoured the correct stopped name image on 52 of 96 single-question story requests. In none of the 24 passage--language comparisons did it favour the correct participant for both actions after the names exchanged roles. The largest term in the four-cell score table was a persistent name preference in 14 comparisons, a page effect in four and a question effect in six. A purely additive page and question score cannot assign both roles across the edit (Methods).

We trained the writer to answer one question with one clause. The aligned and independent-label fits used identical pictured inputs and target frequencies within each scene, language and action. The alignment increased complete four-response comparisons on edited credits from 19 to 175 of 256 (Fig.~\ref{fig:individual-clause-writer}). The paired difference was 60.9 percentage points (95\% scene-bootstrap interval 53.5--68.4). On the twelve new stories both fits completed zero of 24 comparisons. The aligned writer preferred the correct complete clause to a version naming the other person on 975 of 1,024 credit requests and 53 of 96 new-story requests. All alternatives could be written with the available glyphs.

A reader given the pictured passage, the requested order and replies with system identities hidden credited three of 48 aligned responses, five independent-label responses and 38 text Qwen3-8B responses. In the puppet story the aligned writer said the same person controlled and voiced the puppet. It succeeded on a ferry story in which the two workers exchanged jobs, identifying who watched the water level and who collected tickets. On 253 additional novel passages the aligned writer identified both requested excerpts separately in 26 of 506 passage--language pairs, compared with 19 for independent labels and 17 for the parent.

For the black-on-white source card, two Chinese requests supplied with their corresponding catalogue sections elicited all four requested forms of the character for speech in both requested orders. Across eight combinations of question language, source order and request order, source assistance succeeded twice. Giving the complete card to either one or two questions succeeded zero times. With the original inverted raster convention the corresponding counts were two, zero and one. These comparisons measure reproduction of catalogue images.

The aligned fit used 3,848 updates, 140 seconds of optimization and 4.11\,GiB peak allocated GPU memory. On the same 48 warm requests the visual writer consumed 482\,J of device energy and the text control consumed 3,360\,J. Their complete-answer counts differed by more than an order of magnitude. These timings exclude model loading and input rendering.
