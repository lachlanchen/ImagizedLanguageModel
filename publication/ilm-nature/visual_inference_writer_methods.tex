\paragraph{Chinese inference and missing-phrase writing.}
We used the authors' OCNLI release (revision \texttt{b53efdee}) under CC BY-NC 2.0\cite{ocnli}. Each model input contained a sequence of 32-by-32 premise bitmaps and a second sequence comprising the instruction and claim. We excluded records without a consensus label, unsupported glyphs and normalized duplicates or conflicts. Training excluded any normalized premise or claim that appeared in the same field in official development, removing 13,526 records. We reserved 512 training premise families covering all three relations and excluded 30 additional rows sharing a validation claim. This left 33,441 fitting pairs, 3,348 internal validation pairs and 2,947 official pairs. We reconstructed the retained images against their source texts. Each three-relation family supplied a fixed triple, with one row per relation (512 internal and 35 official triples).

Decision responses were full pictured sentences expressing entailment, contradiction or uncertainty. For restoration, we covered one span with a common mask. We sampled its length uniformly among the available lengths one, two and three, then sampled an eligible location. All target glyphs could be drawn from the fixed writing memory. This restriction applies to the restoration trial. Every claim retained at least one visible glyph, and fixed evaluation masks were identical across conditions. Canonical restoration gives credit for the original human wording; other plausible completions can receive zero.

Both arms began at the matched stance checkpoint. The decision loss averaged the first-glyph and complete-sequence negative log likelihoods. We added the mean restoration loss, including stop, and dictionary rehearsal with weight 0.15. The ink encoder and writing keys stayed fixed. Eight epochs used 64 records per batch; the AdamW learning rate decayed from 0.0004 to 0.00008 after 75 warmup steps. The control reassigned premises with the same genre and kept all claims, targets and premise frequencies. Its initial construction stopped before its first update because three records had no genre label and shared a single premise. The amended control retained those three assignments and changed the premise for each of the remaining 33,438 rows every epoch. The completed paired writer remained fixed. We scored complete stopped images and resampled premise families for uncertainty intervals. A two-neighbor count baseline predicted the missing image using conditional frequencies in the training data. A linear relation baseline used hashed unigram, bigram and overlap features of the actual bitmap streams.

\paragraph{Choosing a complete span and comparing a local language model.}
The visual count model stored estimates of the most frequent hidden span for the two immediately visible flanks, either single flank and a marginal distribution. Every training claim contributed equal mass across eligible span lengths and locations. A 15-coefficient logistic selector was calibrated using internal validation cases in which exactly one proposal matched the stopped target. At inference it used proposal lengths, stop, backoff level, passage and claim occurrences and flank agreement at each proposed source location. The additional table and coefficients required 2,308,158 bytes. We checked all 2,947 official outputs against the deployed selector. Separately, the local Qwen3-8B Q8 text model freely generated decisions and completions for the same 105 official claim images reconstructed as source text. Its prompt specified the three full relation sentences or the missing-phrase format. Scoring required a stop and the canonical answer, with premise-level bootstrap intervals. The visual model received supervised task training; the benchmark provides a task-level quality comparison.
