\paragraph{Free visual translation and phrase composition.}
From the pinned NiuTrans classical--modern archive, we selected 1,200 training and 120 held-out chapters, excluding the 64 chapters in the confusable reading. Within 384-row chapter windows we aligned the two streams using a banded monotone dynamic program, allowing skips and scoring cosine overlap of character and adjacent-character counts. A replacement required similarity at least 0.25 and an improvement of at least 0.12. We retained the first 32,768 shuffled training rows. Each free-translation evaluation used 2,048 different rows. The cohorts covered 108 and 114 held-out chapters, of which 105 occurred in both. No reference changed in the first evaluation cohort. The data comparison estimates the effect of a small training perturbation.

For source bitmap $x_i$, a bidirectional gated recurrent network encodes pooled ink and 48 measured pixels into $h_i$. The predicted count $f_i$ ranges from zero to eight. For each slot $j\le f_i$, a copy gate chooses the observed source or a drawing whose 48 landmarks are generated in twelve four-pixel groups. A frozen motor completes the bitmap. This is a visual instance of fertility-based translation \textup{(Gu et al., ICLR 2018)}. Supervision uses monotone minimum-edit alignments, with cross-entropy for the count and drawing terms and binary cross-entropy for the copy gate. The eight-slot bound clipped training targets in 3,027 and 3,024 sentences, respectively. Each optimizer saw eight passes at batch size 96 with identical initial weights and row orders. Of 1,700,698 parameters, 285,018 were trainable. The two fits took 52 and 59 seconds on one RTX 4090 D, reaching 221 MiB peak allocated tensor memory. These figures exclude preparation and previous motor training.

The alternative follows statistical phrase translation \textup{(Koehn, Och and Marcu, HLT--NAACL 2003)}. It counts aligned target fragments for recurrent source spans of one to six complete bitmap cells. Each source span receives one pseudocount on copying. A monotone beam of width four combines log phrase probability, a per-phrase cost of 0.65 and a clipped log association at the join, $\log[p(v\mid u)/p(v)]$, for adjacent output images $u,v$. A 512-passage split by training chapter selected the join weight 0.25 from three choices. The final 2.32-MB file stores 86,180 source spans and 6,619 distinct training images. The runtime can copy unfamiliar ink and compose seen shapes. It receives presegmented cells in reading order. Rendered text and canonical typesetting dominate these experiments.

We measured edit distance between complete sequences of physical bitmaps and resampled chapters 2,000 times for paired intervals. A separate 24-passage reading in each cohort used the first passage from each of 24 different chapters. Identical outputs shared a single grade, and display order was randomized. For reading, a known canonical bitmap was transcribed to its observed character. Grades distinguished an acceptable complete modern rendering, partial modernization, untranslated classical text and an erroneous or unrelated answer. Grades were fixed before scoring method assignments. A correct unchanged date in the second cohort received full credit. Full equality to a recorded bitmap sequence and contextual acceptability measure different properties.

The training-size comparison retained the selected 0.25 join weight and all decoding settings. It used the remaining 1,097 held-out passages after excluding the alignment review. The larger fit used 55,872 training pairs and stored 148,685 spans and 7,280 images in 3.48 MB. The reported 21.5 seconds covers fitting, serialization and restoring from already prepared data on CPU.
