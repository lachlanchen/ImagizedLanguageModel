\subsection*{Supervising the location of answer words}
We evaluated Qwen3-4B in Q4\_K\_M quantization with local llama.cpp, greedy decoding, a 144-subword limit and a JSON grammar requiring an answer and a passage quote. Each prompt received the source, question and previous exchange. On 83 difficult and 128 other development turns we scored the original and two input exchanges. After the first result, we moved the current question after the history and labelled it explicitly. The independent replication used disjoint passages that became printable when typographic quotes, dashes, ellipses and whitespace were normalized. It contained nine turns with absent phrasing across all four references. Teacher admission required F1 of at least 0.35, a drop of at least 0.08 under each exchange, exact matches to human answers and literal passage quotes for at least half the original difficult prompts. Text-teacher inference used 14.19 and 15.36 kJ for the two comparisons. Public CoQA may have been present in teacher pretraining. Prior work studies sequence-level distillation in translation (Kim and Rush, \url{https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/D16-1139}) and learned pointing and generation in summarization (See et al., \url{https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/P17-1099}).

For both representation arms we continued the original cold weights for 1,500 AdamW updates at 0.0002, using the same random batches of 32 human turns. For a target word $w$ and annotated rationale $E$ we added
\[
 -0.35\log\sum_{j\in E\cap M(w)}p(j\mid h_t,\mathrm{point})
\]
whenever at least one matching edit lay inside the passage interval. Each fit included 94,847 such word exposures. The matched continuation retained ordinary pointing, spelling, routing and endpoint supervision. The new visual fit took 26.9 seconds and 3.08 kJ on one RTX 4090 D, compared with 21.9 seconds and 2.70 kJ for its control. The corresponding byte costs were 23.6 seconds and 2.88 kJ versus 21.8 seconds and 2.72 kJ. These figures cover the additional optimizer steps. Fresh evaluation retained complete printed passages and all eligible turns irrespective of target length, excluded canonical overlaps with the earlier trained and evaluated stories, and used all four official answers with 5,000 paired whole-story bootstrap samples. The positive lower visual bound is conditional on a single fitted pair.

Let $a,b$ denote correct and incorrect pointer mass inside the rationale and $c,d$ the corresponding masses outside. For positive total mass and an integer multiplier $k>1$, the favored correct-word probability $(ka+c)/(k(a+b)+c+d)$ exceeds $(a+c)/(a+b+c+d)$ precisely when $ad>bc$. The Lean proof uses exact cross multiplication of nonnegative integer masses, which also covers finite binary probabilities after a common rescaling. We compared this identity with direct normalization of the saved reader's logits on fresh cases with a valid human interval and a copyable target under the correct history. The diagnostic assumes the correct route and preceding words.

For the optional inference rule, let $L_l,R_r$ be the network's unnormalized endpoint weights. Conditional on $l\le r$, the inclusion probability is $q_i=(\sum_{l\le i}L_l)(\sum_{r\ge i}R_r)/\sum_l L_l\sum_{r\ge l}R_r$. Log-prefix and log-suffix sums evaluate all $q_i$ in linear time and space. We multiplied each passage position's pointer weight by $1+3q_i$ while retaining the original routing and feedback. Explicit enumeration agreed within $10^{-14}$ on numerical checks. The longer-story evaluation used remaining eligible official development stories above 448 words, with the same disjointness and scoring rules.

The replication held the initial visual checkpoint fixed and varied the training batches with three additional consecutive sampling seeds. Within each seed, the two arms received identical draws. We resampled four paired seeds and a common set of stories with replacement 100,000 times for each length group, retaining all turns from each selected story. The accuracy--energy certificate evaluates the minimum recorded millijoule cost among checkpoints satisfying the target on both splits. In Lean we prove that the reported minimum is attained and that a factor advantage holds exactly when one qualifying image checkpoint meets the factor against every qualifying comparator. An empty eligible set has no minimum. The check uses the recorded on-device preparation and fit costs and makes no claim about crossings between checkpoints.
