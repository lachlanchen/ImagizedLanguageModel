\subsection*{Learning which question is being asked in English}
With the correct headword visible, the dictionary writer often produced the complete answer to a different question about the same word. Updating 115,713 parameters of the visual question encoder raised exact responses on the standard English frame from 249 to 1,080 of 1,136. The matched control trained with shuffled requests gave 310. On 96 questions written by a separate local model, the corresponding scores were 19 before training, 48 with matching, and 21 with shuffling (Fig.~\ref{fig:english-request-writer}). The matched gain over shuffling was 28.1 percentage points (95\% card-clustered interval 15.6--40.6). A pre-fit review identified 26 requests with an ambiguous or changed intention. Among the remaining 70, matching recovered 32 and shuffling 20.

A different result emerged when both the requested and already known information appeared in one unfamiliar construction. Four requests about the same word used the same glyphs with different field and script assignments. The matched writer gave 267 of 1,136 correct responses, compared with 270 under shuffling. Neither completed all four requests on any headword. The matched writer retained 61 of 96 independent Chinese questions, compared with 60 before training. Natural DuReader answers declined from 15 to 14 of 571. The gain on new English questions depended on their phrasing.
