\subsection*{Reversing a request reverses the returned historical ink}
On a plate showing oracle and bronze forms of two characters, the prior responder returned only the oracle form when asked for both forms of \textit{yan} (U+8A00). We fitted a decoder that can select another source span after its first selection. It answered all 16 individual and compound requests on the same plate of \textit{yan} (U+8A00) and \textit{zheng} (U+5F81), including reverse chronology and reversal of the two entries. Both output cells were exact source images (Fig.~\ref{fig:ordered-page-writer}c). On historical families held out from this fit, the writer gave both requested orders correctly in 94 of 96 contrasts with familiar phrasing. Rewording the request reduced this count to zero. In 178 of the 192 rephrased two-form requests it finished after one cell. Its first cell was correct in 125 cases.

We then trained two copies from the same checkpoint for 1,512 further updates. One repeated the original examples. The other varied the pictured instructions for one or two historical forms and included individual human CMRC questions from the training pairs. Both retained 94/96 complete order reversals with familiar wording. The writer trained on varied requests reached 36/96 on the earlier rephrasing and 16/96 on a further wording with different cards, versus zero and one for replay (Fig.~\ref{fig:ordered-page-writer}a). Neither unfamiliar wording reached the specified 48/96 criterion. The varied writer supplied a single glyph in 80/192 requests on the earlier rephrasing, down from 178. Its next selection remained sensitive to phrasing even when both requested glyphs were visible.

The first ordered writer also answered 22/256 compound CMRC requests exactly, including both orders of seven pairs; the prior responder gave zero. The varied writer achieved 17/256 and both orders of four pairs (Fig.~\ref{fig:ordered-page-writer}b). All ordered decoders gave zero exact name images on the 64 individual role questions, versus 46 for the previous reader. A longer response sometimes expressed the right answer. For example, the varied writer returned the entire clause identifying the mapmaker. It reproduced the whole passage on 23/64 requests and gave identical images to the two different questions about 13/32 passages. Their complete DuReader answers increased from zero to 32--39 of 571. Every exact answer belonged to the 177 single-span cases. None matched a complete reference among 94 distributed-source or 300 absent-content cases. For 235/300 absent-content cases the entire target was representable from the source and the writing memory. The varied writer emitted at least one required source-absent cell in 88 cases, yet included all required source-absent cells in their relative order on none. Exact-reference scoring can miss paraphrases. These results demonstrate ordered composition from original source images and document the loss of ordinary reading accuracy when the recurrent decoder replaces the earlier output selection.
