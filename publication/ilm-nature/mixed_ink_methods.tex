\paragraph{Question-conditioned source boundaries and writing.}
Inputs were ordered 32-by-32 binary tiles. Each tile supplied 64 area means and 48 landmark measurements. A convolutional question encoder provided a pooled representation for a binary whole-answer decision. At each passage position the reader combined local ink, question attention, exact visual matches of up to three consecutive tiles and relative position. Three dilated convolutions produced start and end fields. For an inclusive end $b$ at or after start $a$, the end field received a question-dependent bias indexed by $\min(b-a,48)$. All legal lengths remained available. We fitted the route by binary cross-entropy and the source bounds by the sum of start and conditional-end negative log likelihoods. Decoding retained twelve candidate starts and scored every legal end. The selected source span terminated at its predicted end. For a missing expression the inherited writer received the central 48 tiles, drew successive glyphs and reread its own emitted pixels.

The new module trained for twelve passes on 7,235 natural questions and three requests on each of 2,048 distinct training windows. Two pictured phrasings per request appeared in training. A third phrasing was reserved for evaluation. Questions about opposite sides of the same gap had different answers despite identical passage pixels. The copied intervals have no lexical output inventory. Source images used aligned cells and a common printed typeface. Exact scores measure accepted pictured answers, with multiple human alternatives permitted on the official set. Inference on official questions ran on CPU. Fitting the new module took 42.84\,s on one RTX~4090~D; this excludes earlier writer training, data preparation and confirmation.

For the output-support calculation we split the held-out Wikisource records at Chinese sentence-final punctuation or newlines, discarded empty segments and formed adjacent pairs within each record. We retained the delimiters at sentence ends. A target qualified as potentially reachable if it fit within the writer's eight-cell limit or if its entire sequence of packed bitmaps matched a cell-aligned interval of the preceding segment. This upper bound grants perfect decisions and a writer capable of drawing any short target.
