\paragraph{Changes in stroke thickness improve reading of unfamiliar print.}
With the language writer held fixed, we changed the optical training views of 5,958 glyph identities. A translation-only control and a reader also trained on changes in stroke thickness and aspect ratio received the same 230,400 presentations. On 197 CMRC passages printed in HanaMin, two distinct questions about the same passage both received exact answers on six passages after augmentation and none under the control. Individual correct answers rose from 15 to 43 of 394 (Fig.~\ref{fig:stroke-scale}). On 149 passages covered by FandolSong, a second reserved family, complete pairs increased from one to three and individual answers from 18 to 24 of 298. Its separate development cohort fell from one complete pair to zero. Erasing the question yielded zero exact trial answers in both families. On the first trial passage the writer named the requested game event. Its second answer contained the correct duration and an additional glyph meaning 'within', which the exact-reference metric rejected.

The regular-print trial fell from 14 complete passages to 13, missing the prespecified retention criterion. The augmented reader retained both familiar historical requests on 99 of 100 catalogue cards. In HanaMin, correct glyph identifications rose from 106,216 to 121,915 of 158,717 test occurrences. FandolSong increased from 73,391 to 83,849 of 119,814. An identical visible bitmap appeared with multiple training labels for 16 of 222,404 distinct augmented views and three of 141,354 control views. Such conflicts limit perfect recovery of labels from the observed ink.

Choosing the correspondence from the two input strips recovered all 14 regular-print CMRC pairs and preserved six HanaMin pairs and three FandolSong pairs. On 227 XQuAD passages with Chinese questions in HanaMin, the same route increased individual answers from three to 22 of 454. Neither reader answered both questions on any passage. With Chinese questions in regular print both writers gave 58/454 answers and seven complete passages. With English questions in HanaMin the routed writer gave two complete answers individually. Among the 210 HanaMin passages outside two documented earlier English source selections, the routed result was 18/420 and zero complete passages. On the first 64 source-order pairs, serial image inference gave 8/128 answers in Chinese and 2/128 in English in 8.92 and 14.16 seconds, respectively. Local text Qwen3-8B gave 85 and 74 in 28.03 and 27.47 seconds on the same GPU. These systems operated at substantially different accuracy. The routed candidate missed its additional-dataset selection criterion. Both models gave zero of 300 canonical replies requiring absent content ink.
