\subsection*{Crossed participants in visual judgments and clause writing}
Participant exchange builds on controlled inversion in text-based inference\cite{rolemin}. We retained the paired OCNLI writer, its 32-by-32 input cells and its 7,715-shape writing memory. From the fit claims we extracted a predicate and its complement after a personal pronoun. Twenty prespecified affirmative--negative pairs included knowing, possessing, needing and liking. Single-clause filtering and deduplication yielded 1,035 predicate--complement pairs. We assigned distinct names to two clauses with opposite polarities and varied which polarity appeared first. Exchanging the participants in the passage and asking about each one produced a two-by-two square. One task required a relation sentence; another required the predicate and complement followed by the relevant participant. For example, the position of a participant in the requested answer differed from its position in the source. For validation we removed all complements observed in this training material and used a separate pool of names. The 101 retained predicate--complement pairs were scored twice, with familiar wording and with a new passage layout and request. Their clauses are constructed from human text. Human judgments were used separately on the unmodified OCNLI pairs.

Let $I_t$ be the reference bitmap or stop action at output position $t$ and $p_t$ its conditional probability given the two input streams and the preceding reference bitmaps. For a response of $T$ positions including stop, we minimized $-\tfrac12[\log p_1(I_1)+T^{-1}\sum_t\log p_t(I_t)]$. Each update added the mean losses for natural judgments and crossed examples, and 0.55 times the mean loss for natural masked restoration. The broken-association control used the identical images, queries, answers, sampled masks and optimizer schedule. Independently for each square it exchanged the passages with probability one half while retaining the complete queries and answers. Each arm saw 167,205 natural judgment presentations and 62,760 crossed examples in 2,615 updates. Both final checkpoints were decoded from an empty response through the original write, copy and stop actions. We counted a paired success only when both complete stopped answer images matched. Confidence intervals used 10,000 resamples of complete predicate or situation families.

In a separate source-grounding test we replaced the printed participant cells with the bronze forms B11149 for Qin and B08953 for Chu, verifying both source SVG and raster digests. Eight authored counterfactuals assigned possession of an object to one participant. In one task both the passage and request pictured the same participant; a second task asked for a name from the predicate alone. We recorded the actual returned image, the stopping decision and exact whole-response and name-only matches. The visual samples in Fig.~\ref{fig:visual-role-writer} use the first fixed object and both participant assignments.
