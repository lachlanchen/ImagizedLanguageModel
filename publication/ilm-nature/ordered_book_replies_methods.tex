\paragraph{Writing an ordered answer to two pictured questions.}
We selected two distinct human questions per Chinese BiPaR passage. A local Qwen3-4B model received the original training passage, both questions and the human annotations. It supplied a short Chinese clause for each question. We retained 671 of 1,024 pairs. Each clause had to contain its own annotated fact exactly once, omit the other fact and contain additional Chinese text. The two targets per passage joined the clauses in opposite orders. The writer saw each order with both Chinese and English question images, giving 2,684 training presentations. The 96 evaluation passages came from the previously used BiPaR validation partition. Twelve additional passages and their bilingual questions were written and fixed before either fit.

The reader ranked contiguous intervals of up to 48 glyphs by the sum of their endpoint scores. For each question, we retained up to eight distinct spellings and a total of at most 60 excerpt cells. Both banks followed the intact original page. The control shifted each span to a different available start on the same page, preserving rank, length and all boundary positions. From the same bilingual image writer we updated the final four transformer blocks, final normalization and source pointer for 135 AdamW steps per condition. The model read a pair of separately supplied question strips and emitted one unconstrained stream of up to 48 glyphs with a learned stop. The writer retained its existing printable bitmap bank and pointer to novel source ink.

Let $a$ and $b$ denote the unique positions of the two requested fact spellings in a stopped reply. Credits for the forward and reversed requests require $a<b$ and $b<a$, respectively. An output independent of request order can earn at most one of the two credits per page and language. The finite bound and its extension to a list of pictured pages were checked in Lean~4.12. We also checked the elementary lower bound $A+B-N$ on the number of joint successes when two component checks succeed on $A$ and $B$ of $N$ requests. This bound supplies no estimate of either empirical accuracy. A separate local Qwen3-8B grader assessed the two predications on the twelve new pages, with model identity hidden and the printed facts and page available.

For the second fit we split each accepted clause around its annotated fact. The language target retained the original prefix and suffix and one pictured insertion mark between them. The writer received the original page, the marked fact and a single pictured question. Three passes over 2,684 distinct presentations took 405 updates and 31.4 seconds on one RTX 4090 D, using 4.11 GiB peak allocated GPU memory. The direct ranked fit took 15.6 seconds after preparing its excerpt banks. These costs exclude the inherited encoders and generating the training clauses. At inference the reader selected one excerpt separately per question. We required exactly one aligned insertion mark, a learned stop within 24 frame glyphs, and a combined reply of at most 48 glyphs. Invalid frames and overlong replies were retained for inspection. The same assembled order was used for the bare-excerpt control. In the complete-statement assessment, one reader inspected all 48 four-way comparisons, with shared replies coalesced and system labels hidden. Each credit required both relationships in the requested order with coherent wording and no material contradiction. Correct isolated names earned only the separate fact-inclusion credit.
