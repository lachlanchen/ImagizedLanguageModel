\subsection*{Learning to credit a pictured fact in either word order}
Separating the subject, relation and object of a question raised the number of complete correct statements from three to 33 on 48 original human questions (Fig.~\ref{fig:role-binding}). An equal-update control with disrupted labels gave five. The new reader identified 42 subjects, 42 relations and 43 objects. Replacing its object with the human reference raised the complete count from 33 to 37. Thus both attribution and selection of the participant continued to limit accuracy. On 1,200 separate DuIE passages with held-out question wording, the reader and its induced sentence patterns produced 841 exact statements, compared with 48 for disrupted supervision. The 48 human questions had already been used in prior failure analysis. The 1,200 questions were generated from independent annotated passages.

The improvement on independently written programme notes was smaller. Sixteen of 32 statements matched, compared with 14 for the preceding planned writer. Only two of eight programmes received both correct credits on both source assignments. The first film note now gives the right director and screenwriter. After exchanging the two names, its director answer includes intervening prose and its screenwriter answer includes an extra character. Replacing the names by unencoded, two-cell aliases assembled from historical glyphs gave 13 correct statements out of 32, zero complete programmes and only 14 outputs that followed the same substitution as their input. The aliases are a test of visual substitution and carry no asserted historical reading. On 256 further, broadly sampled human questions, the specialized object predictor recovered 147 original direct answers. Its frozen visual reading baseline recovered 187. Adaptation to the twelve relations transferred unevenly to other questions.

An autoregressive writer then received the three selected fields and a pictured instruction to lead with the subject or the object. It wrote \emph{both} stopped, pixel-exact sentences for 31 of the 48 human questions, 16 of the 32 programme questions and 810 of the 1,200 DuIE passages. The unchanged starting writer completed zero pairs in all three cohorts. Supplying the correct fields raised the paired scores to 44, 32 and 1,124, respectively. In less constrained prose the model sometimes added a date or biographical detail absent from its three input fields, or repeated a name up to the output limit. Including both reference names in such a reply is insufficient evidence for a supported statement.
