\paragraph{Cross-script question matching and reading a passage.}
We adapted a visual reader on Chinese novel passages where human annotators had asked the same question in Chinese and English. On the previously used BiPaR development split, matched supervision yielded 92 exact source excerpts for 748 English requests. Exchanging the English questions between facts within each training page gave 67, and the parent gave 27. Both adaptations answered 329 Chinese requests exactly. The matched reader answered both questions in both languages on three of 374 passages. On XQuAD it completed two of 227 passages (Fig.~\ref{fig:bilingual-book-intervals}a).

On 12 new classical passages, the matched reader answered 11 of 24 Chinese requests and three English requests exactly. The parent answered 12 and one. On 11 modern passages, the matched reader answered 17 of 22 Chinese requests and zero English requests, compared with 17 and two for the parent. No reader completed all four requests on a new passage. The one-pass adaptation took 35.3 seconds and 2.24\,GiB peak allocated GPU memory on one RTX 4090 D, excluding the parent model's pretraining. The new reader missed the prespecified criteria for replacing the parent. Feeding its predicted excerpt and the original question to the released causal writer yielded 19 exact stopped replies to the 92 requests. Twelve correct selections were lost and no incorrect selection was repaired. The same writer with the full page also gave 19 exact replies, with a different distribution across languages and works. Text-input Qwen3-8B Q8 gave 85 exact replies and completed 18 of 23 passages.

We then asked whether the question images alone distinguished the requested facts across languages. A separate visual encoder trained from fresh weights matched the English and Chinese questions correctly on 364 of 374 BiPaR passages. A control trained on exchanged questions matched 248, and a length comparison matched 257 (Fig.~\ref{fig:bilingual-book-intervals}b). On XQuAD the corresponding counts were 198, 117 and 174 of 227. Among all 748 Chinese candidate questions in the BiPaR test, the aligned encoder retrieved a matching Chinese question at rank one for 89 English requests and within the first five for 288. This is a measurable cross-script relation under a two-question choice. An unrestricted search for the translation and the production of a correct answer remain much harder tasks.
