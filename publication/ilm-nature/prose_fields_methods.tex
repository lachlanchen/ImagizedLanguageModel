We used the official CrossWOZ splits. A single-domain assistant utterance of at most 160 cells was eligible when a venue could be uniquely resolved and at least one distinct scalar catalogue value occurred verbatim. We excluded text conflicting with an explicit informed value. The first unambiguous occurrence supplied the labelled half-open interval. Test passages were screened against training utterances, earlier human answer targets and the assembled training texts. The question and supporting utterance came from different conversations and could share a venue with training. Only two of the 184 natural question--evidence pairs contained a training-unseen venue; 119 had unseen wording. Truth was consulted in selecting answerable and missing-evidence cohorts. The reader received a pictured question, known domain, venue name and continuous paragraph, wrapped at fixed width. It received no property row boundaries, catalogue values or lists of available properties.

For each field the visual convolutional reader scores an opening $a_i$, interior $h_t$, closing $b_j$, and a null option. The score of a nonempty source interval is
\[
 a_i+b_j+\sum_{t=i}^{j}h_t=(a_i-H_{i-1})+(b_j+H_j),\qquad H_j=\sum_{t\le j}h_t .
\]
A cumulative log sum over possible starts gives the exact normalizer per end in linear time. A cumulative maximum supplies the best interval. Cross-entropy includes nulls; a null wins a decoding tie.
