\subsection*{Writing an answer from several phrases}
Adding questions with multiple annotated answer spans to the 547,125-parameter writer's training increased complete visual answers from 24 to 49 on 501 held list questions. On the 396 questions whose full answer wording was absent from the inputs, the list-trained writer produced 15 complete answers. Ten lost credit independently when we exchanged the question or the passage. Nine of these ten answer strings were unseen in fitting. Both earlier visual curricula scored zero on this subset. For a question about the singing in \emph{What's Love Got to Do with It}, the writer assembled five word images, \emph{Tina Turner and Laurence Fishburne}. The uniform control wrote \emph{Angela Bassett} (Fig.~\ref{fig:compose-dialogue}). The matched byte writer produced 19 complete answers on the difficult list subset, 14 sensitive to both exchanges.

On 3,004 conversation turns from 200 previously unused stories, the uniform and list-trained visual writers returned 797 and 764 exact answers. On the 407 turns requiring absent multiword wording, all six writers returned zero exact answers. The visual list curriculum changed mean word F1 on these turns by +1.08 percentage points (95\% paired story interval $-0.44$ to $+2.63$), compared with +22.93 points on the list task ($+20.05$ to $+25.86$). Each comparison used one training seed. For a question about \emph{My Left Foot}, both visual writers answered \emph{David Helfgott}. The reference was \emph{Christy Brown}.

Could the writer recognize a plausible answer that it failed to propose? Given two human answers and two pictured questions about the same page, the list-trained visual writer chose the correct joint assignment on 139 of 158 pairs with empty history. Both questions individually preferred their own answer on only 37 pairs. Exchanging the page while retaining both questions and candidate images reduced correct assignments to 117 (difference +13.9 percentage points, 95\% paired story interval +5.4 to +22.2). With the actual previous exchange the writer assigned 118 pairs correctly. These measurements show question--answer compatibility and a contribution from the pictured page under supplied-answer scoring.

Grouping positions that rendered the same word at inference increased complete list replies from 49 to 52. The visual writer still returned zero exact difficult conversational replies. A separate search proposed up to 32 stopped answers using only visible word images. On difficult lists, 24 of 396 top proposals matched, 17 lost the match under both exchanges, and 68 references occurred among the first 32 proposals. On the 239 difficult conversational turns whose normalized answer words were available, no top proposal matched. Just one reference occurred among the first 32. The search also found one reference among 32 after passage exchange. With this search budget, a selector of these proposals has little scope to recover the missing answers.
