\subsection*{Following a name when two people exchange roles}
The question reader recovered the requested role while sometimes copying the other person's name. We therefore conditioned the scores of both name boundaries on the predicted role. On 128 new DuIE passages containing two credited people, each passage supplied two questions before and after exchanging the people. In every question the writer had to produce the correct fact in both word orders and stop. The previous system passed all eight generated answers on 29 passages. The new scorer passed 70. It gained 44 passages and lost three. Randomly assigning the names during paired supervision gave 11. On 48 additional constructed credits we also reversed the clause order. Each credit now required sixteen stopped answers. The new scorer passed 36 credits, compared with 13 previously and two with random assignment (Fig.~\ref{fig:participant-margin}).

On the exchanged song that motivated the experiment, the writer now correctly credits Su Tian as composer. On the separate 512 DuIE questions, the reference name scored higher than the annotated competing person on 472. In 42 of those cases some other span still won, as permitted by the formal result. The new scorer answered 924 of 1,200 earlier DuIE questions in both requested orders, up from 892. On 48 human questions it fell from 34 to 30, and on broadly sampled direct questions it fell from 147 to 121 of 256. The first lost human answer still gives the author's name but prefixes it with the dynasty. Exact-span scoring counts the extra glyph as an error. On 32 statements containing two-cell historical aliases the new scorer recovered 14 against the earlier 13. The human-question prerequisite for replacing the default failed.

In a secondary fit, removing the rival penalty while retaining matched labels, initialization and batches yielded 79 of 128 fully correct exchange passages and 37 of 48 complete clause-order comparisons. It generated both word orders on 28 of the 48 human questions and 926 of the 1,200 earlier DuIE passages. These single-seed scores distinguish the contribution of the penalty from that of paired supervision.
