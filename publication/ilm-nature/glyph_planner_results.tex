\paragraph{Planning the next glyph from the passage.}
A 2.37-million-parameter writer kept a recurrent passage state and started a fresh drawing state for every glyph (Fig.~\ref{fig:glyph-planner}). On 17,686 new Chinese idiom gaps it reproduced the first glyph 1,283 times, compared with 1,221 for the previous uniform writer. Only 136 complete answers matched, versus 547 for the uniform writer, zero for shuffled passages and 960 for local counts. The planner emitted 70,705 familiar bitmaps in 70,744 cells and recovered 106 exact expressions with all four characters absent from the visible passage. Across 6,735 matched pairs it never answered both passages correctly. The first newly correct answer relative to the uniform writer appears in Fig.~\ref{fig:glyph-planner}c.

Keeping four complete-answer histories raised the count from 136 to 176, with 49 gains and nine losses (paired 95\% interval for the difference, 0.15--0.31 percentage points). Every original output, length and stop was reproduced by retaining one history. When given the correct preceding glyphs, the aligned planner reproduced 1,283, 1,699, 2,799 and 3,172 reference glyphs at the four successive positions. In free writing with one history the counts were 1,283, 510, 1,004 and 274. Earlier errors therefore account for some of the later failures. Accurate supplied history alone seldom identified the correct next drawing.

On a separate 17,723 gaps the same four-history planner recovered 191 complete answers before further training. Four additional passes over the original examples yielded 435. At equal exposure, training on both real answers and the stronger writer's physical pattern probabilities yielded 383, gaining 40 answers and losing 92 against continued reference training (paired 95\% interval, $-0.43$ to $-0.17$ percentage points). The teacher wrote 540 exact answers and local counts recovered 977. All three student conditions generated their answers independently.

The fixed four-character task also conceals limitations in transferring the system to ordinary questions. Among 1,788 held-out CMRC answers, only 680 fit within the eight-glyph output cap. Given the true landmarks of every source glyph, the idiom renderer exactly reconstructed 219 complete answers across all lengths. Just 183 answers both fit within the cap and were canonically reconstructible. This calculation supplied the answer and measured the physical interface alone.
