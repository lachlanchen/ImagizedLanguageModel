\subsection*{The right historical stage can belong to the wrong word}
The same stream writer learned to give structured answers containing a fact and a historical form. It answered 284 of 1,136 standard questions exactly on new entry families, compared with 38 after within-card question reassignment (difference 21.65 percentage points, card-clustered 95\% interval 19.37--23.94). On 96 independently phrased questions the corresponding scores were 19 and two. On English requests for the same pictured answers they were 74 and 46 of 1,136. The matched writer opened every standard answer with the correct headword. It then used the correct stage with the requested headword in 561 cases and with the other headword in 568 cases. Seven outputs ran to the length limit. No card had all eight answers correct (Fig.~\ref{fig:general-entry-writer}).

Swapping the two complete records changed 277 output streams and gave 282 exact answers. Supplying the requested record alone, with the original question and frozen writer, raised complete answers from 284 to 1,104. Supplying only the distractor gave zero. This intervention uses the known record identity. It isolates a substantial failure to select evidence when a second plausible record is present.

We therefore used the visible headword to locate an entry before writing. If exactly one bracketed headword appeared in the question, the writer received that record. Ambiguous questions retained the full card. This fixed image-matching rule raised exact standard responses to 1,103 of 1,136, gained 819 and lost none. It raised independently worded responses from 19 to 60 of 96 and English responses from 74 to 249 of 1,136. All 1,136 English queries located the correct entry, isolating a further difficulty in interpreting the request. This rule relies on visible matching ink and marked record boundaries. It changed none of the 1,383 DRCD or 571 DuReader input passages.

On a new single-entry card for \textit{y\'an} (speech), two Chinese requests elicited the recorded gloss \textit{talk} with bronze form B02975 and the pronunciation \textit{y\'an} with oracle form J04903. Both historical bitmaps were copied exactly from the pictured evidence. The headword and lexical facts occurred in entry training with different exemplars. On the broader assays, correct DRCD pairs fell from 21 to 12 of 1,042, and exact DuReader answers fell from 16 to 15 of 571. All three writers still gave zero exact responses on the 300 natural references requiring a new content glyph.
