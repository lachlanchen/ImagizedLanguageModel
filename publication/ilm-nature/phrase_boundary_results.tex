\paragraph{A learned phrase boundary repairs the reading of distance.}
For two camps nine li apart, a single-cell writer retained the classical prefix meaning mutually and inserted the modern equivalent of distance. A writer trained to choose the neighbouring source cells and the observed modern expression together produced an idiomatic statement that the camps were nine li apart (Fig.~\ref{fig:phrase-boundary}). It repaired all three instances of the same error in development and two newly authored cases. Of eight authored answers, three were faithful with joint selection and one with single-cell replacement. Both writers produced three faithful answers on twelve paired archival clauses, with both sides correct on one of six pairs. Both received seven credits on seventeen further archival clauses and zero on twelve diverse passages.

Only 37 of 1,024 diverse sources contained any of the four trained centre images. The joint rule changed one output relative to its matched control and reduced total reference edits by one, from 15,561 to 15,560. Even a perfect choice of one arbitrary replacement strip could reproduce only 60 of the 1,024 reference translations when it covered at most three source cells. Permitting six cells raised the count to 182. Alternative faithful paraphrases can differ from these references. Widening one local edit has limited reach when the rest of the sentence also requires translation.
