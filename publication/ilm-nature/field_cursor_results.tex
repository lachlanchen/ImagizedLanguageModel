\subsection*{Binding two requested facts to their surrounding words}
A guest asked for the price and address of a Beijing hotel. An image writer returned the number 1,354 and the correct street, yet called the number an address and the street a price. Requiring a decoder to emit both source spans secured their appearance while leaving the error in their surrounding words untouched (Fig.~\ref{fig:two-fact-figure}a). A smaller writer that received the current field at each step produced a sentence assigning the price and address correctly.

Among 37 held natural turns labelled with two requests, an unrestricted optical writer trained on human examples returned both catalogue values cleanly on 14. Adding joined training questions increased this to 24. Constraining the writer to realize its own predicted field sequence increased it to 30. A reader that aligned two fields to distinct positions in the question, combined with the same constrained writer, returned both clean values on 36. Only 24 also passed a local check that each value was attached to a suitable property cue. Independently writing and concatenating clauses from that same plan gave 36 clean pairs and 23 explicit attachments. The compact current-field writer gave 36 clean pairs and 36 explicit attachments. A matched byte reader with the shared compact writer gave 37 on both checks (Fig.~\ref{fig:two-fact-figure}b). Only four of the optical writer's 37 replies exactly reproduced the human sentence. The optical model gave a clean value on 515 of 515 eligible single-field questions. After freezing the model we admitted fourteen additional turns from the same held corpus without requiring the human answer to repeat both annotated values. It passed twelve. One of its two misses asked only for a rating in the visible question although the annotations also requested an address. In the other it omitted visit duration.

The sole original two-field miss was a guest who actually requested three facts. The model wrote the rating and hours and omitted expenditure per person. Qwen3-8B answered all three and also correctly expanded a municipal address on a different turn. Both answers failed the literal two-field test, on which Qwen scored 35. Thus its lower literal count cannot establish superior answer quality for the image model. The aligned reader followed both directions of eighteen field exchanges under a list-order instruction, but none when asked to leave the first-mentioned field until second (Fig.~\ref{fig:two-fact-figure}c). These are eighteen field pairs per wording with shared syntax.

The compact model contains 90,345 parameters for reading and 243,505 for writing. Training these components from scratch required 27.2 seconds and 2.58 kJ of measured device energy on one RTX 4090 D. The matching byte alternative required 26.3 seconds and 2.51 kJ including the shared writer. In three blocks of 32 warmed sweeps of the 37 questions, the image model took 0.393--0.492 seconds and 35.3--44.5 J per sweep. A local quantized Qwen3-8B text system took 18.66--18.71 seconds and 4.17--4.23 kJ per sweep in two runs. The specialized writer receives a resolved, aligned record and generates short replies within eight possible fields. The comparison includes local HTTP for Qwen, excludes host energy and corpus preparation, and establishes no general-purpose training advantage.
