\noindent\textbf{A visual language model must identify the content of a request and write an answer from it. We tested both abilities with independent Chinese--English writers on one consumer graphics card. Predicting which strips of glyphs belonged in a translation increased literal predicate retention from 17 to 43 of 336 controlled answers. Neither writer preserved both named participants in any answer. On 192 requests drawn from four unseen books, the guided writer conveyed one complete meaning, its unchanged parent two, and an 8B text model 166. A pictured reader presented with two possible answers improved from 21 to 77 of 96 book pairs after correcting its preference for particular target strings. Choosing which words to propose remains the limiting step in free visual translation.}\par

\section*{Free writing and independent choice}
A source-only model judge read the outputs in two hidden orders. The aligned head scored 1/192, the shuffled head 2/192 and source exchange 0/192. The aligned-minus-parent difference was $-0.52$ percentage points (four-book interval $-1.56$ to $0.00$). All 768 book answers replayed on CPU. On 256 OPUS requests the same three writers scored one, two and two for aligned, shuffled and parent.

Ranking two supplied answers jointly across two sources can conceal a strong preference for one answer on both. The parent correctly matched 91/96 book pairs jointly. It chose correctly on both individual sources in 21 pairs. Subtracting each candidate's mean log score on 16 training images selected by the current source length raised that count to 77. With 84 role pairs having identical target strip multisets, both independent choices improved from 57 to 76. Among the free proposals, even perfect selection could credit only two book answers.
