\noindent\textbf{In the sentence \emph{It was Mary who helped Tom}, an answer about the helper must connect the printed word \emph{who} to Mary. We trained two 312,244-parameter readers on the same pictured English clauses. Changing the target from the local grammatical head to its annotated antecedent raised exact recovery of both participants from 0 to 69 of 126 linked test clauses. On 27 linked clauses from an external treebank it raised recovery from 0 to 9. With a frozen bilingual writer the resolved participants yielded exact Chinese answers for 180 of 236 controlled subject clefts. None of eight answers to naturally occurring events conveyed the complete proposition.}\par
\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{Reading past the pronoun}
A verb mark identifies the event. Four endpoint scores locate two disjoint words in the glyph stream. We initialize each reader from the same English locator and train on 8,966 EWT and GUM clauses, including 891 with visible relative links. Both fits use the same 102,400 sampled examples on one RTX 4090 D. At test time only the pictured clause and the predicate location are supplied. On ordinary clauses the resolved reader recovered 822/1,069 pairs against 829 for its control. In the external GENTLE corpus the counts were 227/303 and 237/303.
\end{multicols}

\section*{What reaches the writer}
We place the chosen actor, marked verb and undergoer in that order, convert their cells to the writer's print face and allow it to write until stop. On 118 role-reversed pairs of subject clefts, both answers matched the canonical Chinese images on 69 pairs. The local-head reader scored zero pairs. Across object clefts the resolved reader scored 31 pairs. These are controlled rewritings of previously evaluated events. On eight external clauses the reader found both participants three times. All eight Chinese outputs lost an essential part of the event on an unblinded bilingual reading.
\section*{What the three spans discard}
Across 236 affirmative--negative contrasts, the annotated three-span inputs were identical in every pair. The learned reader produced identical inputs in 194 pairs. A deterministic writer must make at least one error on each collision when the target meanings differ. The Lean proof formalizes this bound for a finite panel. It motivates retaining the complete source for tense, negation and modifiers. \repo{references/visual_referent_learning_result.md}{Model, raw images and proof}.
