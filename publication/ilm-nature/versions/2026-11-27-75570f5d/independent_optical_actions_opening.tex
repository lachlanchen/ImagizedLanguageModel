\noindent\textbf{Can a model read and write language as images on a single graphics card? We trained two independent bilingual writers from the same 268,342 parallel pairs. One writing decision produced either a single glyph or a short strip learned from adjacent images. Given two sources and two translations, the writers assigned 95 and 93 of 96 book pairs correctly. Free generation from the same books preserved the complete meaning on zero and one of 192 requests, respectively. A quantized 8B text model earned 166 credits. Changing the writing unit reduced the number of decisions by 57\% and increased training time by 14\%. A separate historical-dictionary reader learned to order supplied glosses and specimens on 332 of 608 paired instructions. These results locate a gap between recognising an appropriate translation and proposing its content during visual writing.}\par

\section*{Recognition under controlled changes}
We assigned two complete targets to two sources of similar length from the same work by comparing summed conditional log likelihoods. Matching longer answers to longer sources earned 57/96 with half credit for ties. The single-glyph and strip writers reached 95/96 and 93/96. With exactly equal source lengths they reached 22/23 and 23/23.

Reversing who acted on whom kept every input and output glyph count fixed. The models correctly assigned 167/168 and 164/168 pairs among six transitive predicates. When asked to produce the sentences freely, neither reproduced a canonical answer among 336 requests. The single-glyph writer included both prescribed names in seven outputs. The strip writer included both in zero. Alternative names and paraphrases remain possible under this literal check.
