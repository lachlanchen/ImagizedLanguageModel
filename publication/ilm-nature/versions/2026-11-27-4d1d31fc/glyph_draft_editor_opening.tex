\noindent\textbf{Can a visual writer translate a passage by editing an image of a rough translation? We trained two English--Chinese writers on the same 39,008 bilingual pairs. The writer that could copy from a phrase draft preserved both polarities in 225 of 236 controlled event pairs, compared with 222 for matched training and 195 before training. On 96 requests from four unseen books, the editor earned one complete-meaning credit, the matched writer zero and a quantized text model 89. The editor's sole credited answer reproduced its draft exactly. The model could distinguish two supplied translations far more reliably than it could compose one.}\par
\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{Learning to use a draft}
The source encoder reads windows of four adjacent glyph images. The decoder can generate any glyph in the learned print inventory, copy one from a phrase draft or stop. A learned gate controls copying.\cite{pointer} Both writers received 32,768 parallel passage pairs and 6,240 controlled event pairs in identical order, with four passes on one RTX 4090 D. The draft editor took 58 seconds of additional training over previously fitted models and tables.
\end{multicols}

\section*{What the pictured source contributes}
With one unrelated draft held fixed, the editor favoured the correct assignment of supplied translations on 48 of 48 book pairs and all 236 controlled role swaps. Its free answers preserved both roles and polarities in 107 of 118 controlled sets, against 104 for matched training. All five visual conditions stopped on every request.\par
\section*{Where free writing fails}
The source-only automated judge credited 72 of 96 supplied reference translations, three phrase drafts and zero unrelated foils. The editor assigned the reference a higher mean log probability per glyph than its own answer only once. Even ideal selection among all five visual outputs could earn only four credits. The limiting step on these passages is generating a faithful proposition. \repo{references/glyph_draft_editor_result.md}{Scores, raw answers and reproducible code}.
