\noindent\textbf{Can a system that recognizes a translated passage also write one? We trained a bilingual visual marker and two sentence writers on one RTX 4090 D. Each writer read the complete pictured source. On 236 held pairs of English object clefts, the marked writer produced both the affirmative and negative Chinese answers exactly 195 times, compared with 193 for the full-source control. Both conveyed one complete meaning in 96 free book translations. A separate alignment model learned correspondences between visual strips from 497,775 parallel sentence pairs. It identified the correct source among 48 passages for 93 of 96 pictured translations. Incorporating its probabilities into the writer gave one credited answer in a second 96-request translation test, equal to its neural baseline.}\par
\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{Retaining the source}
A three-head reader had collapsed 194/236 polarity contrasts into identical writer inputs. We kept every source cell and supplied three optional fields for predicate, actor and undergoer. The marker learned from English EWT/GUM and Chinese GSD annotations. Each writer trained for two passes over 237,577 pairs, with the same targets and update order. Generation used the source image alone. On 96 requests from four new books, moving the fields changed 24 output images and left complete-meaning credit unchanged.
\end{multicols}

\section*{A bilingual correspondence in ink}
We estimated a conditional distribution over target strips by marginalizing their alignments to source strips, following IBM Model~1.\cite{brown} After excluding strips without letters or Chinese characters, the translated query ranked its source first in 93/96 cases. Physical glyph overlap alone did so in 6/96. Each search included all 48 candidates. The alignment model can identify content even when it cannot determine the order of an event.
\section*{Free composition on new passages}
On a second panel from the same works, the lexicon preferred the genuine source over an unrelated source for every reference. We used its conditional probabilities to adjust next-glyph scores over every compatible strip segmentation. Both writers earned 1/96 complete meanings, with different credited answers. The text comparator earned 81 and the supplied references 68 under the same automated assessment. These counts depend on the judge and the four selected works. \repo{references/full_image_roles_result.md}{Models, raw outputs and evaluation}.
