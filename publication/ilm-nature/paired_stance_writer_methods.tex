\paragraph{Opposed natural evidence.} We selected pairs of human-annotated DuReader answers to identical questions that gave opposite positions in two different selected paragraphs. Each pictured window comprised at most 192 glyphs and contained the original human answer literally. The opposing answer was absent. This reference-assisted window selection makes whole-window and first-sentence copying informative controls. Training, validation and official cohorts comprised 805, 144 and 18 question pairs, respectively. We separated normalized questions, entire evidence windows and shared 32-character runs across these cohorts. The 694,152-parameter ordered writer learned from each training pair 16 times, using 578,696 trainable parameters. We held the ink encoder and writing keys fixed. The loss equally weighted the three-glyph position prefix and the remaining quotation with stop, each averaged over its own positions. Dictionary rehearsal contributed one quarter of its mean loss. The reassigned control reversed stance labels for a random subset of pairs and complemented that subset on the next epoch, pairing each passage equally often with either position while preserving its quotation. Each fit took 544 updates and less than 16 seconds and 0.41\,GiB of peak allocated memory on one RTX 4090 D after inherited training. The additional crossed-question test used 12 independently constructed situations, two subjects and opposing assignments per situation. All outputs were generated from an empty answer and scored from their saved bitmaps.
