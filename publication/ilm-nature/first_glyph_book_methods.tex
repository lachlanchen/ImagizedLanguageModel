\paragraph{Action weighting on pictured book questions.}
For observed answer image $y_t$ let $p_t(y_t)$ sum the inventory probability and the copy probabilities at every source address bearing those pixels. We retained the mean next-image log loss and the auxiliary first-address loss. In each batch containing novel questions, we added the mean of $-\log p_j(y_j)$ for those examples with unit coefficient. The candidate used $j=1$. The control chose one subsequent action, including the stop, by hashing the question identifier, with the same position for both question languages. All three training conditions used the same initialization, images, assignments, seed and update schedule. The reported optimization time excludes inherited pretraining and corpus preparation.

We reserved 34 passages from Lu Xun's preface to \emph{Call to Arms}, Faxian's \emph{Record of Buddhist Kingdoms}, Xu Xiake's travel journals and \emph{Dream of the Red Chamber}. Source text was converted to simplified Chinese for rendering. We composed two questions per passage with disjoint accepted spans, then expressed both questions in Chinese and English. Font coverage, source offsets and disjoint acceptable images were checked before inference. A physical overlap screen compared every 32-cell window in the selected passages with every sixteenth starting position in 16,529 distinct supervised pages. This detects any identical aligned run of at least 47 cells. The test covers four works and one training seed. Upstream pretraining exposure is unknown.

Let $F$ count replies beginning with an accepted glyph, $D$ the subset of those that subsequently fail, and $E$ the exact stopped replies. Then $E=F-D$, so an improvement in exact answers requires the gain in $F$ to exceed the increase in $D$. For nonnegative integer action masses an incorrect greedy first choice also implies that at least half the total mass lies outside the intended glyph. Lean checks the finite-count identity and the mass inequality. The experiments test how supervised weighting changes these quantities.

For a separate probability diagnosis after selection, we summed the log probability of every glyph and the stop along each accepted reply, the other question's accepted replies and the archived emission. We compared the best accepted joint score with that of the emitted stopped answer using a log-score tolerance of $10^{-5}$. We excluded capped emissions from this comparison and independently reproduced the greedy choices along the observed prefixes. Supplied alternatives were used only after autonomous generation.
