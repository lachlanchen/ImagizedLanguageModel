\paragraph{Reading a nearby phrase and writing its missing expression.}
We gave the writer a separate view of the four pictured glyphs on each side of a gap. On 26,213 new passages it wrote 801 complete source expressions, compared with 797 for the original reader, 805 after training with shuffled local views and 1,504 for local counts (Fig.~\ref{fig:ordered-neighborhood}a,b). The aligned reader gained 136 and lost 132 answers against the original (paired 95\% interval for the difference, $-0.11$ to $0.14$ percentage points). Among 256 passages sharing both an eight-glyph window and its recorded answer with a training passage, it solved 37 with the held-out passage and 39 with the actual training passage.

How many expressions could this writer draw if given the answer during fitting? Of 3,845 different training expressions, it drew 98 from the mean of the passage states for each expression. Optimizing one state per known expression raised the count to 3,649. In 182 of the 196 failures the emitted physical path had higher probability than the source path. Refreshing the state after the second glyph recovered another 108 expressions with no losses. It drew 313 of the 360 rare forms, below the prespecified 90\% criterion.

A shared controller then received each successive target glyph as an image. It copied 2,759 training expressions, including 110 rare forms. On 3,845 new combinations of the same glyphs it drew the first glyph exactly in 3,514 cases. Only 159 prefixes survived the second glyph, six survived the third and none survived the fourth (Fig.~\ref{fig:ordered-neighborhood}c). The fixed renderer could reproduce all four glyphs under direct landmark input in 3,833 of these cases. This localizes the loss of compositional copying to generation across glyph boundaries in this system. It leaves the reader's ability to infer the intended glyph sequence as a separate question.

We tested this boundary directly with the same trained controller and writer. Both arms committed the best glyph after each twelve physical decisions and received the four output positions externally. Carrying drawing history produced 2,484 exact training expressions and zero complete unfamiliar strings. Clearing that history at every boundary reduced the training count to 1,564 but recovered 1,450 of the 3,845 unfamiliar strings (Fig.~\ref{fig:ordered-neighborhood}d). First-position pixels agreed in every case. The intervention changed the contribution of writing history while keeping the supplied images, model weights and within-glyph search fixed. It did not reach the 90\% copying criterion.
