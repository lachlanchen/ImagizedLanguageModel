\paragraph{Optical reading of classical roles.}
On 14 prospectively reserved excerpts from the \textit{Discourses of the States}, the adapted reader returned 38 of 56 exact name images for Chinese questions. It answered both questions on both versions of four excerpts. The original optical reader returned 35 correct images and completed three excerpts. A matched fit with equal classical name weights returned 31 correct images and completed two excerpts. Paired 95\% bootstrap intervals for the advantage in complete contrasts spanned $-14$ to 29 percentage points against the parent and 0 to 36 against the control. A generic Chinese question completed none. For English questions, the aligned reader gave 16 of 56 correct answers and completed no excerpt. Text Qwen3-8B completed eight excerpts in each language. The earlier classical works showed different effects: the adapted reader completed nine Chinese contrasts in \textit{A New Account} against seven for the parent, but two against three in the \textit{Zuo Tradition}. On the reserved modern novels it returned 230 of 452 Chinese answers and 81 English answers, below the equal-label arm's 242 and 84 (Fig.~\ref{fig:optical-role-adapter}). The aligned update trained 50,690 of 86,000,354 parameters in 24.6 s on one RTX 4090 D, with 1.33 GiB of peak allocated device memory. The reader also carries 11.8 million buffer elements. These marginal gains on Chinese excerpts leave substantial uncertainty about transfer across books.
