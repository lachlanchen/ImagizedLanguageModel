\paragraph{Copying and drawing inside a sentence.}
We tested whether a writer could keep part of a pictured classical Chinese passage and generate the missing modern writing within the same answer. A recurrent editor trained on 48,000 classical--modern sentence pairs. The initial editor reproduced 2,044 of 2,044 renderable passages on request, but 1,291 of its translations reached the output limit during repeated insertion at one source position. Fitting the renderer until it reproduced all 7,326 training shapes left the translation result at 30 complete answers on 2,048 requests. None was an exact longer sentence requiring a new Han glyph.

Learning an insertion limit for each source position restored a learned stop on every pictured request. On the first set of chapters the model returned 32 complete modern sentences and one of 1,955 longer mixed references. In 248 separate confirmation chapters the corresponding counts were 30 of 2,048 and three of 1,964 (Fig.~\ref{fig:visual-edit}a,b). None of the three complete reference sequences occurred in training. One changed the classical honorific for death into its two-glyph modern equivalent. All 2,045 renderable quotations on this set were exact. The writer's translation edit rate was 56.82\%, compared with 57.28\% for copying and 56.39\% for edits predicted from local counts. Scoring the edit action and its proposed drawing jointly gave 38 complete answers and an edit rate of 56.36\%. The paired 95\% interval for improvement over local counts included zero. Translation wording admits alternatives, and these scores measure agreement with the recorded reference.

We isolated the choice of the next drawing by supplying the correct previous ink, actions and source cursor. At 37,451 recorded insertion or replacement positions, feeding the true 48 ink measurements into the renderer reproduced 37,427 glyphs. Predicting those measurements from the language state reproduced 3,816 (Fig.~\ref{fig:visual-edit}d). The difference localizes a major error before rendering even when the preceding history is correct. The experiment establishes exact local revisions in previously unseen sentences. General translation and book continuation remain open.
