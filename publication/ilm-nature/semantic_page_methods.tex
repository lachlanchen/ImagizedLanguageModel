\subsection*{Reading back the generated image}
We used the cleaned E2E corpus (\href{https://github.com/tuetschek/e2e-cleaning}{Du\v{s}ek et al.}). Each record supplies typed field values and one or more human descriptions. We rendered bilingual fields in aligned 32-pixel cells and varied their row order in training. Shared cell projections and a convolution produced one vector per visible row. Three causal self- and cross-attention layers conditioned blocks of four letter images on the visible facts. Within each block a predicted cell depended on its earlier glyphs. The output was a choice among 73 distinct training bitmaps and a learned stop. Each reader saw 432,000 sampled descriptions over 9,000 updates. The byte control observed a fixed projection of UTF-8 bytes in each aligned input cell and used the same physical output writer. No textual transcript entered optical inference.

The writer proposes complete sentences with width-eight beam search, retaining the eight highest length-normalized completed paths. A separate 223,200-parameter convolutional reader inferred the present facts from each candidate's image sequence. It used 4,000 batches of 96 human descriptions. The input encoder estimated the requested facts from its pictured rows. We selected the completed candidate maximizing mean glyph log probability plus 0.7 times its Bernoulli readback log likelihood per requested fact. The likelihood-only control returned the highest length-normalized beam. Its length exponent was 0.7. Thus the full selector changes both the likelihood normalization and the readback term. We therefore replayed the same eight candidates and separately scored mean log likelihood with the listener weight fixed at zero. Previously saved selected strings had to reproduce exactly. Training used one RTX 4090 D with no language teacher.

The scorer used the original corpus realization patterns, kept maximal spans without supplying the source to resolve ambiguity, and compared sets of recognized facts. The literal criterion counts alternative matches emitted by the patterns. A second criterion merges low, average and high customer ratings with their numeric counterparts and merges three qualitative price bands with their corresponding numeric bands. These assumptions can obscure differences in precision. Both criteria can miss malformed clauses. We report their values on human reference descriptions, and inspect twenty systematically spaced generated answers. The test records are disjoint from training. The same test records exposed the greedy failure before the search intervention; a separate mixed-script development check used the frozen settings.
