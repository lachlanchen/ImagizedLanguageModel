\paragraph{A single writer can alternate between factual writing, natural answers and historical forms.}
The failure on natural questions persisted with the correct evidence. We therefore adapted the date writer for one more epoch, using human reading questions, historical-form cards and replay of factual writing (Fig.~\ref{fig:mixed-visual-answers}). On the original human questions, exact stopped replies rose from 7 to 114 of 359. The general span reader still recovered 242. On the 302 questions eligible for the paragraph test, the mixed writer gave 115 exact replies from the annotated section and 111 from learned selection; its parent gave zero in both cases. The full-page mixed writer answered 87 of those questions.

On 128 additional CMRC passages, each with two distinct human questions and disjoint accepted answers, the mixed writer returned 79/256 individual answers and answered both questions on 15 passages. Its date-trained parent answered five questions and passed one pair. The span reader recovered 156 answers and passed 49 pairs. With the question replaced by one blank cell, the mixed writer answered one question and passed zero pairs. Its response changed on 127/128 switches with the actual question. The first exact answer identified the music society that the pictured question asked about. No further DRCD pair survived the source and eligibility filters for this comparison.

The same weights recovered 190/192 historical-form replies with familiar wording and 54/192 after rephrasing, up from 18 and zero. The known catalogue card for \textit{yan} yielded both attested forms in both requested orders. With learned paragraph selection, all twelve replies were correct on 23/48 distinct-subject boards and 14/34 shared-subject boards, up from 13 and eight. Some comparisons regressed. Whole-page shared-subject boards fell from two to zero and earlier routed date boards from 40/64 to 39/64. Most natural question pairs still contain an incorrect answer.
