\subsection*{Measuring transfer with the writer held fixed}
The broader training distribution added 24 manually authored question pairs to 16 existing pairs. We refitted the question and passage focus network from the same initialization. The writer and its cached complete-answer log likelihoods were frozen. Each of twelve passes sampled two questions per clause from 19,067 training clauses. Training took 26.1 seconds on one RTX 4090 D, with 235 MiB peak allocated device memory during this cached fit. These costs exclude the preceding writer fit and cache construction. An otherwise identical control randomly exchanged the roles of the two answer images within clauses.

We reserved two English classroom questions from the Austin Peay and San Jos\'e State University writing centres and adapted two Chinese prompts from Thinka's lesson on sentence constituents. These four wordings were fixed before fitting. We selected 32 classical excerpts for syntactic coverage, froze acceptable one-word English glosses and checked the excerpts against their source editions. Two shared a target window with writer training and one duplicated an earlier passage. All 116 fresh classroom responses were regenerated on CPU from the complete page and question bitmaps. A pass requires both the correct source position and a stopped image spelling a registered gloss.

For passage $i$, let $W_i$ indicate that the writer can produce both accepted glosses at the supplied positions and $F_i$ indicate that all four questions locate the intended characters. The observed score is $\sum_i W_i F_i$, bounded by $\sum_i W_i$. With identical writing weights, the difference between two readers equals the count of repaired focus decisions minus lost focus decisions on writable passages. We verified both finite counting statements in Lean~4. The bound concerns the fixed gloss lists and decoder. A model can produce a sensible synonym outside those lists.
