\paragraph{Learning from freely emitted book answers.}
We ran the released bilingual writer on all 22,902 pictured questions in its novel-training assignments. A rival had to be a stopped nonempty image with a unique spelling on the source page. We excluded substrings and superstrings of every accepted answer. We sampled 192 proposals in each combination of question language and whether the rival was the annotated answer to another question on the page. A local Qwen3-8B Q8 read the passage and the original question as text and compared the annotated reply and the rival in both presentation orders. It could choose either, both or neither. We retained a pair only if both orders uniquely favoured the annotated answer. A separate model-assisted reading of 48 fixed, stratified examples checked the original passages. Four uncertain or ambiguous examples were removed. This screen samples label quality and does not establish the correctness of every retained pair.

For a sequence of glyph images $y$ ending in stop, write $s(y)=\sum_t\log p(y_t\mid x,q,y_{<t})$, where $x$ and $q$ are the imaged passage and question. Each probability sums all writing and source-copy routes to the identical bitmap. Both fits started from the released writer and used the same seed, batches, standard likelihood and auxiliary address supervision. On batches containing preference pairs, we added the mean of $\log(1+\exp[s(w)-s(y)])$ for a generated wrong reply $w$ and the first annotated answer $y$. Both sequences included the stop. The positive-only control detached $s(w)$ from differentiation. At identical parameters the gold gradient and forward loss therefore agree. The experimental condition also differentiates the rival score. The summed log probability preserves the cost of completing longer replies.

Among these two alternatives, let $p$ and $r$ be the masses of the accepted and rival replies. A reversed or tied comparison requires $r/(p+r)\geq 1/2$. Lean verifies the resulting bound on the number of such comparisons for normalized masses in a common integer unit. It also verifies that a reply which beats every inspected rival and outweighs the total uninspected mass beats every member of a finite candidate set. The experiment estimates pair order on named rivals. It separately measures what the unconstrained writer emits.

Before evaluation we reserved six passages each from \emph{Mencius}, \emph{Han Feizi}, \emph{The Classic of Mountains and Seas}, \emph{Discourses of the States}, \emph{Journey to the West} and \emph{A Record of Buddhist Monasteries in Luoyang}. Each passage has two distinct questions with disjoint accepted spans, expressed in both languages. Source and physical-overlap checks followed the preceding literary experiment. Four exact stopped replies were required per passage. The selection rule also required retention of 14 complete CMRC question pairs and 99 familiar historical cards. For a separate diagnostic we scored each question's first annotated answer against both questions. For log scores $s_{ij}$ of answer $j$ given question $i$, the assignment margin $s_{11}+s_{22}-s_{12}-s_{21}$ cancels any additive question-only or answer-only score. Lean verifies the cancellation. Positive assignment and correct individual preferences are reported separately from free reading.
