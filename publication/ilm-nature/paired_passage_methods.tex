\subsection*{Visual question segmentation and ordered span composition}
The internal split contains 1,702 training passages and 425 held-out passages. Each original question and answer is a stream of binary $32\times32$ cell images. A convolutional planner reads pooled ink intensities, 48 landmark pixels and clipped distances from the ends of the entire prompt. At each cell it selects one of four actions: skip, read, copy a prompt cell, or read and stop. Supervision gives question boundaries during training. During inference the planner sees only the complete prompt image and its occupied extent. For each predicted question run, a shared image-conditioned reader selects a passage span. The writer copies these spans and the selected prompt punctuation in predicted order. The resulting image can contain disjoint passage spans; its glyphs must already appear in the input.

For passage $x$, predicted question region $q$ and answer image $y$, the refined span reader minimizes
\[
-\log \sum_{(i,j):\,x_{i:j}=y} p(i\mid x,q)\,p(j\mid i,x,q).
\]
This marginal assigns credit to every cell-aligned occurrence of the answer. The planner trains for six epochs on 24,846 ordered pairs and 7,235 singles per epoch. The refined reader trains for eight epochs on the unique questions with AdamW. The 101,144-parameter system uses the earlier visual reader as its initialization. Data loading, fitting and checkpoint writing took 27.1 seconds on one RTX 4090 D, with 174.3 MiB peak PyTorch allocation through training. This measurement excludes the preceding reader training, corpus rendering and evaluation. Exact scoring requires the full physical output and termination. The interval resamples all 425 internal development passages with replacement 10,000 times.

For the official development comparison we excluded 12 of 848 passages, containing 49 questions, because the source font lacked a required glyph. The remaining 836 passages shared no complete passage image with the internal corpus. We accepted every annotated answer present verbatim in its passage. Each of the 9,600 ordered requests has disjoint forward and reverse reference sets. The two new planners reused the original passage reader, identical initialization, question order, learning rate and 1,506 optimizer steps each. Every ordered training request received each of six prefixes once in the varied arm; the replay arm repeated its prefix six times. Prefix lengths and supervised action counts therefore vary. We reread and scored the archived images in each condition. The English instruction is a formatting transfer test with Chinese passage evidence.

The constrained decoder maximizes the sum of the learned cell-action logits over a five-state reading process. The initial state can skip instruction ink. Once a question starts, it can continue, emit one pictured separator to start the next region, or choose a learned stop. A terminal state can skip trailing input. An unfinished or empty output remains possible. Viterbi decoding takes linear time in prompt length for this fixed state graph. It uses neither an expected number of answers nor a prescribed separator glyph. Exhaustive enumeration of short action sequences verifies the maximum-score recurrence. The same weights, passage reader and source-copy operator were used for both decoding rules. Three official requests were independently replayed from their PNG images on CPU.
