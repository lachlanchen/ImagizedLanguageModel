\paragraph{Reading participant expressions across held-out works.}
From BiPaR we selected passages with two human questions in Chinese and English asking who performed or received an action. Both answers had to be distinct short expressions present in the pictured passage. Of the 394 training pages, 355 were from one work. We reserved all eligible pages from the Harry Potter and Three-Body collections (177 and 49 pages). Both training and validation pages of these works belonged exclusively to the reserved set for the fresh reader. Earlier writers had seen material from both works.

A shared 48-dimensional encoder projects pooled glyph pixels and contextualizes them with dilated convolutions. Each page location attends to the pictured question. Additional evidence channels record rare matching glyphs, adjacent matches and their pooled neighborhoods. The reader scores both endpoints and each of eight span widths. For a written answer $y$, its probability is the sum over all source locations that would emit the same bitmap. We minimized the mean of two negative log likelihoods, one over all valid spans and one restricted to the two annotated answers. Two exposures to each target were averaged within the objective. The aligned reader always receives the correct participant; in the independent-label reader each participant receives half the weight on the same input. Both fits used identical initialization, all four questions per training passage, 40 passes and 2,000 AdamW updates. The final aligned run took 19.9 seconds of optimization and 118 MiB of peak allocated device memory on one RTX 4090 D. A training-free comparator scores nearby literal glyph matches with extra weight for contiguous matches and discounts glyphs that repeat in the passage.

For unrestricted person proposals, we trained a convolutional detector independently on 24,000 distinct renderable DuIE training snippets with explicitly person-typed relational arguments; exact duplicates of the book pages and identifying book titles were excluded. Person annotations can be incomplete. We balanced logistic loss over labelled and unmarked spans within each page and used six passes. The detector ranks distinct strips by their best occurrence. The question reader ranks the top 16 by summed occurrence mass. This follow-up reuses the 226 reserved pages after observing the unrestricted reading failure. Uncertainty was estimated with 10,000 paired resamples of pages stratified by work. Every response bitmap was checked against the reference and the passage. The comparison uses exact source wording, which can exclude valid aliases. Warm inference used pre-rendered inputs for the visual reader and local Qwen3-8B with greedy short answers for the text comparison.
