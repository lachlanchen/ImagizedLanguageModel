\paragraph{Drawing lexical senses from running prose.}
We used single-character words bearing lowercase English glosses of at most 24 letters or hyphens from the Kyoto Classical Chinese Universal Dependencies treebank, release 2.16. We reserved the \textit{Analects} (KR1h0004) and \textit{Chu ci} (KR4a0001) in their entirety within that release's training file. We removed conflicting and repeated marked windows and any identical regular-face image window shared across splits. A passage supplied at most eight Han cells on either side of the annotated cell. Whitespace filled the remaining cells. Paired tests required two different attested readings of the same headword in a work, each previously observed three or more times in training. Only the glosses were targets. Physical image identity and the stopping decision determined exactness. We also checked a frozen list of synonymous glosses. The untrained comparison with the previous compositor used supplied central locations on 64 cases per work. Its 128 replies all missed the gloss.

The input path pooled each $32\times32$ bitmap into $16\times16$ ink densities, projected the cell to 160 dimensions, and applied ordered convolutions of widths three and five. The centre and its local and pooled context initialized a recurrent writer. Seven training-selected pixel coordinates separated all 27 Latin letter and hyphen images. At each step the decoder assigned probabilities to the $2^7$ possible joint ink patterns and an ending. A learned drawing function expanded the predicted bits to 1,024 logits. A training-only head predicted the equality class of the entire reference image sequence. If $c_{1:T}$ are the physical codes and $c_{T+1}$ is the ending, the batch objective was the mean next-step negative log likelihood over occupied steps plus one half of the mean whole-gloss classification loss. The drawing function first learned all 27 physical images exactly. Both contextual fits shared that initialization and used 7,736 AdamW steps, alternating regular and bold source fonts. In the control we independently permuted entire responses within each central-bitmap group each epoch. All fits used the same source images and response counts. The deployed writer contains 1,180,977 parameters. The auxiliary 353,073 parameters are discarded at inference. A reader restricted to a headword image gives the same deterministic reply in both contexts, so it cannot earn both exact scores for distinct targets. The formal proof and original streams accompany the implementation.

Optical transfer used a fixed bank of regular and bold source cells observed during fitting. Nonempty ink was cropped, centred and scaled with preserved aspect ratio. The comparison distance was the mean of the two directed nearest-stroke distances, clipped at four pixels and scaled to the unit interval, plus cosine distance of pooled ink. The selected source image replaced the novel source cell. The writer and all output pixels used the original output font. The optical bank contains no glosses. The formal language task therefore assumes a marked cell and a finite visual field. A full printed page needs a separate segmentation and focus-selection step. The Qwen comparison presented each same-headword pair as two independent binary questions with the focal character marked. For the visual writer we scored the sum of log probabilities of the complete pictured candidate and its ending. Neither model received the other passage in a pair.
