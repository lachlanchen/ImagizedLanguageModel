\paragraph{Does identifying the source improve the generated phrase?}
We paired passages from different chapters whose nearby writing and insertion lengths matched but whose recorded phrases differed. Training an additional sentence-conditioned transformation on 120,628 such comparisons raised exact stopped insertions from 2,143 to 2,529 of 24,048 sites across five cohorts (Fig.~\ref{fig:sentence-choice}). An equally trained likelihood model recovered 2,516. Across individual cohorts the 95\% chapter-bootstrap intervals for the paired model's advantage over likelihood included zero. A whole-phrase frequency rule estimated on the expanded corpus recovered 3,554. Of 837 sites in chapters untouched by the previous evaluations, each new writer solved 38, the frozen writer 26 and whole-phrase counts 59. A model reader accepted 10 of 48 anonymous insertions from each new writer, eight from the frozen writer, 14 from the count rule and 47 from the recorded translations. Both new writers missed all 7,504 instances whose complete reference phrase was absent from the enlarged training inventory.

The learned transformation primarily altered endings. Across 13,041 multi-glyph targets, the paired writer produced 60 complete correct phrases compared with 138 for its parent. It ended after a strictly shorter correct prefix in 2,143 cases, up from 1,197. Its first glyph was already wrong in 10,660 cases. In a separate free-generation control, enabling the learned transformation only when drawing yielded 2,160 exact answers. Enabling it only when deciding whether to stop yielded 2,529, the same aggregate total as the full transformation. One corrected answer in Figure~\ref{fig:sentence-choice} ends immediately before the following translation supplies the next glyph. The second fails on the first character of a causal expression. The paired writer assigned higher probability to the authentic sources in 453 of 797 matched evaluation pairs. It generated 199 correct phrases with authentic sources and 193 with the sources exchanged. Reference-conditioned discrimination therefore gave little improvement in freely generated content.

Among the 679 exchange pairs with different initial glyph signatures, the recorded first glyph outranked the competing reference at 688 of 1,358 authentic sites. The writer emitted the recorded image at 258 sites. Of the 1,100 wrong first emissions, only seven assigned a higher probability to the recorded first signature than to the emitted one; the other 1,093 favored the emitted signature. Searching the same distribution more thoroughly cannot make the lower-scoring recorded signature its maximum at those 1,093 sites.
