\paragraph{Learning from local visual context.}
We corrected the neural probability of each four-pixel group using the frequencies seen beside nearby classical and modern glyphs (Fig.~\ref{fig:local-ink}). Applying the correction to the first glyph raised exact stopped phrases from 337 to 599 and from 268 to 538. Applying it also to subsequent drawing and ending decisions gave 618 and 564. The 95\% chapter-bootstrap interval for the latter gain included zero on discovery. We then examined 1,024 newly reserved sentences from 549 chapters outside training and the two preceding phrase-test pools. At 5,987 aligned sites, correcting the full stream recovered 405 exact phrases, including 27 multi-glyph phrases. The original writer recovered 187, the onset correction 354, a source-ablated full-stream model 268, and local whole-phrase counts 651. Against the original the interval for the gain was 3.04--4.28 percentage points. The local whole-phrase rule led the full-stream writer by 3.32--4.89 points. The visual writer alone solved 104 sites missed by this local rule. It recovered none of the 2,433 fresh instances of unseen insertion types.

The fresh writer stopped on 5,188/5,987 cases. Of its 35,290 emitted glyphs, 35,279 exactly matched a single-font bitmap in the scoring collection. Supplying the correct first code raised whole phrases to 1,917. With all drawings autonomous, a perfect length controller could recover at most 590. Corresponding perfect-length ceilings on the earlier cohorts were 822 and 732, also below local whole-phrase counts. In a blinded, model-read sample of 48 fresh sites, eight visual answers, eleven local-count answers and 45 references were judged acceptable in context. Five acceptable visual answers differed from the recorded wording. The reader marked four visual answers uncertain. Fluent glyph shapes, correct phrase choice and coherent independent sentences require different evidence.
