\paragraph{Training on the image that reaches the reader.}
Pooling helped a frozen decoder, so we compared credit for a single training path with credit for every copying position and exemplar that would write the target glyph. Each arm saw all 55,872 pictured training passages in the same order. Both used the pooled decoder. On 1,024 further passages from 423 unseen chapters, the all-path writer made 17,940 glyph edits against 18,276 for the single-path arm and 20,461 for their unmodified parent (Fig.~\ref{fig:visible-likelihood}). The paired improvement over single-path training was 1.31 percentage points (95\% chapter-bootstrap interval 0.03--2.56). The two trained systems each reproduced nine entire archive glosses exactly, including four requiring a change. Copying the input still made fewer edits (16,660), and the phrase composer made 15,787. A masked reading accepted none of eighteen diverse answers from either neural arm, four from the composer and fifteen from the archive. In a diplomatic account the neural writer retained the states providing and receiving military assistance but replaced an envoy's journey to Zhao with a journey to his army.

One exact output translated a short classical refusal attributed to Confucius. We kept the speech frame, varied the speaker among four alternatives, and crossed permission with refusal. None of the eight complete source bitmaps or the eight neural answers occurred on their respective side of the training corpus. Both systems preserved the pictured speaker and the polarity of the predicate in all eight cases. Seven neural answers and six phrase-composed answers were acceptable complete utterances; each answered both signs acceptably for three of four speakers. The neural writer added an unwarranted first-person plural in the affirmative utterance attributed to Confucius. This is evidence for productive use of negation within one familiar construction. The pair containing the motivating success and the shared grammatical frame limit generalization beyond these prompts.
