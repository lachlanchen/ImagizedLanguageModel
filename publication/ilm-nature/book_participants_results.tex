\paragraph{Identifying people in novels with a fresh visual reader.}
We trained a 64,666-parameter reader on 394 passages from four works. Each contained two human questions whose answers were different people or descriptions of people. No passage from either reserved work was used for this fit. The reader assigned a probability to each source image of one to eight consecutive glyphs. We first asked which of two supplied participant images it preferred. It answered both Chinese questions on 62 of 226 reserved passages, compared with ten for an independent-label control and 38 for literal image matching (Fig.~\ref{fig:book-participant-reader}). Its advantage over literal matching was 10.6 percentage points (95\% passage-bootstrap interval 4.0--17.3). Among Chinese question pairs that changed its choice, it assigned both correctly 62 times and exchanged them incorrectly 12 times. In English these counts were 27 and 36. Although the aligned reader completed more English pairs than the controls, it made only 217 correct individual choices in 452 binary English questions. This comparison supports reading the Chinese questions more strongly than the English ones.

In unrestricted reading the same model could return any short strip from the page. It recovered only 23 of 452 Chinese answers and seven of 452 English answers despite answering every training question correctly. We therefore trained a separate 35,952-parameter detector on 24,000 DuIE passages with annotated person expressions. Its 16 proposals contained the source answer in 222 of 452 cases. The aligned reader selected 50 correct Chinese answers and 34 correct English answers from these proposals, compared with 51 and 48 for the independent-label reader. The detector's first choice alone was correct 49 times in each language. The two readers together returned both Chinese answers on four pages and both English answers on one. Text Qwen3-8B exactly answered 330 Chinese and 316 English questions without supplied candidates. Answering a question among two known people transferred in Chinese, but selecting the intended person freely among many plausible source images remained difficult.
