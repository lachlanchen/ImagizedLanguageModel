\paragraph{Changing the passage while keeping the questions fixed.}
The same person can occupy a different role after a single edit to a passage. We trained a causal image writer to follow such edits, using two possible assignments for every pictured scene. A control received both answers equally often on each version. The models saw the same pictures, including the same printed questions, and the same marginal distribution of complete answer images. Only the association between the pictured assignment and its answer differed. Each fit updated 28.6 million parameters on one RTX~4090~D in approximately 100 seconds, excluding data preparation, earlier training and evaluation.

On 128 held-out credits the aligned writer placed both names in the requested order and stopped on 756 of 1,024 requests. On 755 it reproduced the entire target image exactly. A complete contrast required both question orders on both versions of a credit within one language. The aligned writer passed 165 of 256 contrasts, compared with five for the independent-label control and zero for the earlier writer (Fig.~\ref{fig:role-change-writer}a,b). The paired difference was 62.5 percentage points (95\% bootstrap interval, 54.3--70.3; resampled by work). On 77 works all eight requests passed. The successful pair in Fig.~\ref{fig:role-change-writer}c shows two stopped answers identifying the lyricist and singer after their names exchange positions in the source.

The improvement did not extend to independently worded stories about events, later changes of plan and corrected reports. The aligned writer passed zero of 24 contrasts and produced four supported complete statements in 48 readings. The independent-label control produced seven and local Qwen3-8B from text produced 39. In the illustrated story the writer named the same person for both actions even though the two questions concerned different people. Of 96 story requests, the aligned writer assigned higher probability to the supported canonical answer than to its reversed-assignment alternative on 49, compared with 998 of 1,024 on the credits. Both alternatives were physically writable in every case. On historical requests it retained all 192 familiar instructions and all four measured forms of the character for speech, but missed all 192 rephrased instructions.

In a diagnostic using the story annotations, we rewrote either the passages or the questions in the training style while retaining the assignments. The aligned writer passed two of 24 contrasts after rewriting the passages, none after rewriting only the questions, and four after rewriting both. All four joint-rewrite successes used Chinese questions. The independent-label control passed none in any condition. Providing familiar wording recovered some bindings. Simply rendering both sides in that wording was insufficient for most cases.
