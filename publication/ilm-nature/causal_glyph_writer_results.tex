\subsection*{A generative reader often copies at the first missing glyph}
The ordered writer could copy two separated forms but recovered no complete open answer requiring new content ink. We tested whether transferring a causal Chinese language prior could support writing beyond a source span (Fig.~\ref{fig:causal-glyph-writer}). A continuous map from printed cells to pretrained GPT-2 embeddings gave a mean centred cosine similarity of 0.9998 on its 7,902 calibration bitmaps. Under correct answer history, the transferred and upstream embedding rows gave nearly identical losses on writable development targets before answer training (1.724 and 1.715 nats). Both gave two complete answers in 571. This comparison shares the same printed alphabet, segmentation and pretrained causal body.

After training on genuine question--passage and answer pairs, the writer returned 16 exact stopped answers, including two whose reference joined dispersed source ink. Equal training with permuted source--question pairs gave zero. Replacing the passage at inference while retaining the original question likewise gave zero. With all 1,366 distinct renderable human answers, mean best glyph overlap was 0.423 for genuine pairs, 0.170 after shuffled training and 0.144 with a different passage. Overlap counts common glyphs regardless of order. Among the first twelve absent-content cases a descriptive reading found two responsive answers, three partial answers and seven incorrect ones. The first two role questions illustrate the distinction between legibility and answering: the reader correctly identified the mapmaker in a sentence, then repeated the mapmaking fact when asked who had lost the way.

All 1,525 required occurrences of glyphs absent from the displayed question and passage were writable. Given the correct preceding answer, the writer recovered 446 of these and only 18 of the 300 first such positions. At 258 of those first decisions it selected a glyph already on the page. Giving the first missing glyph eight times the ordinary training weight reduced source selections from 238 to 226 relative to further replay, but both recovered the correct next glyph in only 21 cases. Neither completed an absent-content reference. Some targets also depended on passage selection. For 51 cases the original documents contained the entire reference elsewhere. A question-only lexical ranker displayed it in five and recovered zero complete answers. Placing a window around the known reference displayed it in all 51 and recovered seven. This last condition uses the answer to locate the evidence. It isolates an input limitation while showing that displaying the answer still often fails to elicit it.
