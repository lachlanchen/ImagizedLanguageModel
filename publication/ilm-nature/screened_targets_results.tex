\paragraph{Learning a translation from its pictured target.}
We changed the supervision for 113 passages while holding the starting writer, source images, order and optimizer schedule fixed (Fig.~\ref{fig:screened-targets}). With the book targets, the writer reproduced 54 original translations and none of the reviewed translations exactly. With reviewed targets, it reproduced one original and 47 reviewed translations. Thus the supplied wording measurably determines the free-running image sequence.

Would the writer still read the input after learning that wording? In a subsequent probe, we changed one pictured numeral in each of 18 eligible sentences. The reviewed writer returned 13 exact revised translations, including all 11 whose unchanged translation it had reproduced exactly. All 13 answers differed from the input and from every complete target in either training set. For example, moving a famine-relief event from the third month to the fifth changed the date in the modern account while retaining the translated verb and location. The book-trained control also adapted an acquired wording: it reproduced the revised book wording in nine of 16 cases where the same numeral and unit could be aligned to that version. In a separate displacement probe, shifting one shared input glyph one pixel horizontally preserved 31 of the 47 previously exact reviewed answers.

As a mechanistic control, a rule compared input images with the 113 source strips, found a unique one-cell substitution and replaced the matching image in its stored translation. It reproduced all 18 revised quantities and abstained on all 118 archive passages and all 24 authored prompts. A novel complete answer on the numeral probe is therefore consistent with local analogy.

Transfer to unfamiliar language was weaker. Neither continuation nor the starting writer produced a faithful whole translation on the 16 new archival passages read under masked method identities. On twelve authored pairs that changed a participant, quantity or negation, the reviewed writer conveyed both complete meanings on one pair. The starting writer succeeded on three and the phrase composer on seven. An offline Qwen3-8B text comparison received ten of the sixteen archival credits and eleven of the twelve whole-pair credits. The small writer learned target wording and reused it under a local change in the depicted quantity. These experiments leave open how to learn a construction that applies across unrelated passages.
