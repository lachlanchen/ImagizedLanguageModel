\subsection*{Ordered prediction of missing glyph pairs}
We continued each Tang writer fitted with visible-glyph supervision in two conditions. Within each poet attribution and metre we paired distinct source lines with distinct endings by a seeded cyclic permutation. Each training batch comprised 32 sampled pairs. The two members shared a gap position and a source typeface. All four fits retained masked writing and visible-glyph identification. For target images $y_1,y_2$ at two consecutive gaps, source $x$ and surviving second-line ink $v$, we computed
\[
 s(x,y;v)=\log p(y_1\mid x,v)+\log p(y_2\mid x,v,y_1).
\]
Both conditions added the average negative ordered score with weight $1/4$. In the contrastive condition the sum $m$ of the two authentic scores minus both exchanged scores contributed $\frac12\log(1+\exp(-m/2))$ to the loss. Source-only and ending-only additive terms cancel in $m$. Each fit used 4,000 AdamW updates of 64 examples at a peak learning rate of $3\times10^{-4}$. The paired control saw identical batches, masks and typefaces.

From eight Song shards in the pinned corpus we reserved up to 512 paired lines per shard. We excluded Tang poet names, either line occurring in the retained Tang partitions, and either line already selected for the Song sample. Period and exact author-string separation cannot resolve anonymous or disputed attributions. New printable test shapes remained eligible as input and reference. The output bank retained its 8,414 Tang bitmaps. At inference we expanded the eight most probable first shapes at each two-cell gap, selected the best second shape for each and returned the pair of greatest ordered probability. The largest omitted first-shape probability bounds every completion below that branch because conditional probabilities are at most one. Lean verifies this certificate for common-scale log scores. It establishes optimality under the fitted distribution whenever the retained best score reaches the bound. We used a paired percentile bootstrap over 192 poet names for the difference in complete repairs.
