\paragraph{Learning from complete wrong book answers.}
The released writer returned 15,118 stopped, incorrect replies on 22,902 training questions. Of 768 stratified proposals, 646 passed the teacher comparison in both answer orders. Separate inspection supported 44 of 48 sampled pairs. Excluding the four uncertain cases left 642 comparisons for training. The released writer assigned higher probability to the annotated reply in 55 of those comparisons. Positive-only replay raised this to 523, and two-sided preference supervision gave 582 (Fig.~\ref{fig:stopped-book-preference}a).

On six additional Chinese works, the preference writer produced 31 exact stopped image replies to 144 Chinese and English requests, compared with 32 under positive-only replay and 32 from the release. It answered 20 of 72 Chinese requests and 11 of 72 English requests. Of 36 passages, 0 received all four correct replies, compared with 0 for replay and 0 for the release. The paired difference in individual reply accuracy relative to replay was -0.7 percentage points (passage-bootstrap 95\% interval -4.9 to 3.5 within the six works). The writer completed 9 of 197 CMRC pairs and 98 of 100 familiar historical cards. None of its 300 open requests requiring absent content ink matched the complete canonical answer. The fixed selection criteria retained the released bilingual writer.

On the six works, providing the two candidate answer images during a separate diagnostic yielded a positive assignment margin on 34 of 36 Chinese and 23 of 36 English passage pairs. Both questions individually preferred their own candidate on 25 and 6 passages, respectively. A previously trained bidirectional reader, restricted to extracting one pictured span, answered 45 Chinese and 1 English requests exactly and completed 0 passages. The causal preference writer's positive pairwise signal therefore has to be interpreted alongside its free replies (Fig.~\ref{fig:stopped-book-preference}b,c). A model-assisted reading of 24 exact misses against their passages found 4 acceptable alternatives, 0 incomplete responses, 20 incorrect responses and 0 unclear responses.

The two-sided fit updated 28.6 million parameters of an 87.1-million-parameter writer for 2,496 steps. Training on the 49,283 replay records took 179 seconds and 4.59\,GiB peak allocated GPU memory on one RTX 4090 D. Mining the original replies took 330 seconds, and the offline teacher spent 297 seconds adjudicating pairs. These adaptation costs exclude the inherited pretraining. The writer gave 31 exact replies in 6.22 seconds of warm serial inference on the 144 requests. Text-input Qwen3-8B Q8 gave 137 exact replies and completed 32 passages in 28.91 seconds. The systems differ in input representation, output interface and accuracy.

On the four earlier literary works we tested whether removing a question-independent page preference would reverse the released writer's strict sequence-score losses. We subtracted the score under a blank question from twice the actual score, keeping both the page and the supplied reply fixed. This reversed 21 of 94 losses. Among 30 originally exact replies, 30 outranked the other question's best annotation under this correction. This was a score diagnostic on previously observed outputs.
