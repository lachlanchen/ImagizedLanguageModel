\subsection*{Two actions within one Chinese line}
A question-independent object pointer cannot recover two different objects from the same selected line. We allowed the question to select a verb and its object jointly. The image writer and its Chinese context encoder remained fixed. Across 971 reserved classical clauses containing multiple annotated events, the trained reader located every requested verb and object in both question frames on 307 clauses. It also wrote every registered English object meaning on 247 clauses, compared with 51 for a frozen conditional decoder and 11 when the training objects were exchanged within each multi-event clause (Fig.~\ref{fig:questioned-events}). Of these 247 clauses, 122 contained at least one object outside the next cell. Selecting the next cell after the learned verb succeeded on 201 clauses overall. This rule outperformed learned object selection on the 336 clauses from held works (81 versus 70), whereas the learned selector performed better on 635 clauses from held sections (177 versus 120). With every correct object position supplied, the writer completed 741 of the 971 clauses. In a previously seen verse it now writes \textit{cup} for the action of raising and \textit{moon} for inviting. The earlier reader had selected only the cup from this line.

The literal-name reader completed no pages when both actions were described by dictionary definitions (118 eligible pages). We trained a second head on a literal question and a description of the same action at each sampled event. Its literal score increased to 257 of 971. On the dictionary questions this reader and its exchanged-description control each completed two pages. On 112 pages eligible for two newly composed natural questions per event, the literal, description-trained and exchanged-description readers completed two, five and one page. Sixteen of the 106 complete question strings had occurred in the inherited training prompts. Restricting evaluation to the 21 pages for which every question was absent from those prompts left no complete written success for any of the three readers. Supplying every object position enabled the writer to complete 16 of these pages. On ten screened Pu Songling extracts no model completed all written answers with literal action names. Only one extract was writable throughout at the supplied positions. Thus the model can change its object within a line in response to a pictured action name, while generalization to new questions and literary prose remains limited.
