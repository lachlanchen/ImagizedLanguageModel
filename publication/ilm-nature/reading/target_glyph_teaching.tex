\spread{What can a text teacher tell a writer about the next glyph?}
\noindent A translation model can favour several words whose first visible mark is identical. At each aligned target-piece boundary we asked which marks the teacher's predictions actually support. The independent writer learned from those probabilities during training and read only the source image and its own ink during generation.

\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{Teaching what the source model resolves}
At a genuine target boundary we decoded each of the 32 highest-probability teacher pieces after its actual prefix. Pieces that began with the same visible glyph contributed to its lower probability $l_g$. End of sequence supplied a separate stop event. The residue included the omitted tail and every incompatible or unwritable option. For the writer's probabilities $q_g$, we added the following loss to the human next-glyph likelihood,
\[
 \sum_{g:q_g<l_g}\!\big[l_g\log(l_g/q_g)-l_g+q_g\big].
\]
Each active term lower-bounds the corresponding full generalized KL contribution because its derivative in the unknown teacher mass $p_g\ge l_g$ is $\log(p_g/q_g)>0$. For masses at common finite precision we also proved in Lean that $\sum_g(l_g-q_g)_+$ is the smallest total variation over all normalized completions of the teacher. This identifies the part of the probability discrepancy that assigning the missing mass cannot erase.
\par The teacher supplied 364,385 aligned English and 318,323 Chinese decisions per pass. Of the total English teacher mass, 49.7\% mapped to whitespace and 14.3\% to letters. In Chinese the corresponding fractions were 0.9\% and 59.0\% for whitespace and letters or ideographs. The two systems saw the same 7,343,256 human glyph or stop positions in four passes.
\section*{Do the freely written answers convey the meaning?}
The primary source-only assessment credited five guided answers, four likelihood-only answers and three from the starting writer in 192 requests. Pairing the guided writer with exchanged source pictures gave zero. The difference from matched training was $+0.52$ percentage points (paired four-work bootstrap 95\% interval, $-1.56$ to $+3.12$). The reference-informed assessment credited both fitted systems on two requests. Giving the correct preceding glyphs at development time reduced the guided writer's loss relative to the matched fit in both languages, from 1.086 to 1.076 nats per English decision and 3.688 to 3.642 per Chinese decision.
\par Both fits took about 24 seconds on one RTX 4090~D after the teachers' probabilities had been prepared. Earlier training, teacher inference, transport to optical targets and grading add cost. The text comparator earned 173/192 in the primary assessment. Matched-quality computational efficiency remains open.
\end{multicols}

\section*{A single numerical choice can change a sentence}
Teacher guidance changed 168 of the 192 outputs relative to the matched fit. Every output stopped. Across all four experimental conditions a CPU replay reproduced 767/768 original GPU PNGs, lengths and stop decisions. On one likelihood-only control the first 57 glyph choices agreed. At choice 58 the two leading unscaled scores differed by 0.00026 on GPU and their order reversed on CPU. The GPU stopped after 79 cells and the CPU after 62. Both variants reproduced at batch size one. The primary evaluation uses the original GPU answer. No independently written answer matched the entire paired reference image on this panel.
\section*{Preserving a longer unit of target history}
We supplied a pretrained bilingual decoder with the actual preceding visual spans, using known piece boundaries. Each distinct training span predicted the mean of the teacher input vectors observed for that ink. For unseen spans we used the training mean at the same length where available. The decoder received the same learned optical source field throughout. Its next-piece choice agreed with its genuine target-embedding path on 2,866/2,951 noninitial English decisions (97.1\%) and 2,808/2,957 Chinese decisions (95.0\%). Exchanging training-span vectors within length gave 3.6\% and 10.8\%. The Chinese sample included 74 zero-cell spans.
\par This experiment supplies true boundaries, true preceding ink and the inherited output head. It establishes that a visual span can carry much of the previous target-state information. Learning the boundaries and writing the next span autonomously are separate requirements. Byte-level retrofitting has also used an inherited global language network and new local modules.\,\href{https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-11111-4}{[Minixhofer et al., 2026]}
