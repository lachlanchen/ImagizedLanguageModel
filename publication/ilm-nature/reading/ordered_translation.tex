\spread{A shorter visual sequence can cost meaning}
\noindent Mandarin and English express similar propositions with substantially different marks. Translation therefore tests whether a model can use a pictured source to write a new sentence in another script.
\platehere{Figure_187_translation_quality}{Prediction loss and semantic transfer across scripts}{\textbf{a}, Both systems have 1,688,480 parameters and saw the same 31,387 bilingual training pairs in both directions for twelve passes. The lowest development loss selected pass eight in both conditions. \textbf{b}, A local Qwen3-VL 8B Q4 judge assessed anonymized stopped translations against human references in two opposite orders. A local Qwen3 8B Q4 model received the corresponding text. Each direction contributed 32 held families.}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{What an ordered block preserves}
Let $X$ be an ordered sequence of pictured cells. A four-cell packing $P$ has a left inverse $U$ that distinguishes absent padding from pictured whitespace. For every decision rule $f$, the rule $f\circ U$ returns the same answer on $P(X)$. Thus optimal decision loss is unchanged, and, for finite variables, $H(Y\mid P(X))=H(Y\mid X)$. Lean checks the inverse and the pointwise decision-loss identity.
\par The network then pools each bitmap and projects a concatenation to 160 dimensions. These operations can lose relevant distinctions. For a deterministic representation $Z$, the increase in optimal expected logarithmic loss is $I(Y;X\mid Z)$. When positive and negative examples of total weights $p,n$ collide at one representation, the minimum deterministic error mass on them is $\min(p,n)$. Lean checks the latter finite bound.
\section*{What a shorter source costs}
At length $4n$, a source head visits $16n^2$ ordered pairs before packing and $n^2$ afterwards. Per-target cross-attention falls fourfold. In the real length distribution, the combined valid source and cross-attention counts per training pass fell from 154.59 to 19.63 million per head with layer multiplicity. Device energy during training and validation fell from 11.33 to 10.33 kJ. Wall time fell from 101.06 to 97.06 s.
\par Finer spacing translated better. Mean sentence chrF2 fell from 30.09 to 22.55 into English and from 15.77 to 11.41 into Chinese when the stride increased. Exchanging source sentences dropped the finer model to 10.62 and 2.75. Both visual writers failed all sixteen additional contrasts probing roles, actions, negation and number. Qwen preserved all sixteen. Its text pretraining and possible public benchmark exposure differ from the optical writers' supervised training.
\end{multicols}

\spread{One sentence plan can carry an unseen mark}
\noindent An unfamiliar name can be quoted exactly without knowing its spelling. We asked whether an optical translator could learn where a name belongs while keeping its original drawing.

These are the first incorrect and first exact English-to-Chinese results in fixed held order with the first specimen assignment. \textbf{a}, The writer preserves specimen J17339 and loses the occupation of dolphin trainer. \textbf{b}, It draws J10928 and reproduces the supplied Chinese reference. The reference conveys looking for work and omits the source adjective \emph{good}. The marks replace aligned personal names experimentally.}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{A symmetry with a physical payload}
A pointer selects a location in the source. Its addition expands the physical output support to the union of trained glyphs and source images. In a separate preservation mode, an unfamiliar bitmap receives a slot in order of first appearance. The semantic network sees the same descriptor for each such slot. The output action addresses the exact unpooled image. A repeated occurrence retains its slot.
\par Let $a$ be a learned sequence of fixed-glyph and slot actions and let $R_s(a)$ render it under a payload assignment $s$. If distinct slots hold distinct images outside the ordinary palette, $R_s$ has a left inverse. Consequently $R_s(a)=R_s(b)$ exactly when $a=b$. Lean checks this equivalence and its substitution law. The stop decision is part of the cached execution. Distinguishing historical periods by their strokes requires a shape-sensitive reader.
\section*{Testing what can be shared}
We replaced one aligned personal name in real human translations by either of two held specimens. With unchanged weights, both assignments stopped, reproduced the appropriate specimen once and agreed on every other glyph in 5/70 pairs using the original features and 56/70 using slots. The corresponding development counts were 3/80 and 66/80. Memoization needed 66 neural generations for 140 test requests. Comparison with uncached execution verified every output byte. Only two of the 140 complete translations matched a reference, both assignments of the same sentence.
\par The learning advantage has a precise scope. For arbitrary binary labels on $M$ contexts and $K$ payloads, worst-case exact identification needs a label on every context--payload pair. If labels depend only on context, one example per context is necessary and sufficient. Lean proves both coverage criteria. This result concerns noiseless identification. An erroneous sentence plan remains erroneous under every valid substitution.
\end{multicols}

\spread{Does more bilingual supervision improve meaning?}
\noindent The conversational translator often drew a plausible sentence about the wrong event. We tested whether more diverse paired sentences would improve free translations on previously unseen articles and conversations.
\platehere{Figure_189_translation_transfer}{Adding editorial prose improves stopping more than meaning}{Sixteen new families in each domain were translated in both directions. Grey bars count complete, stopped outputs. Coloured bars count stopped translations whose meanings were accepted in both grading orders. The base, replay and mixed writers use the same 1.69-million-parameter optical architecture. The comparator is the locally served Qwen3-8B Q4 text model. The independent evaluation set is small and its paired directions share a source family.}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{An article-level held set}
Of 363,802 bilingual News Commentary v18 rows, 256,297 exceeded the existing 96-cell limit. A further 26,293 used shapes outside the training inventories and 2,007 were empty after normalization or duplicated earlier sentences. We retained 78,251 short training pairs from 9,327 article groups and 954 pairs from 128 held groups. Article titles set the grouping. Normalized repeats in either language were excluded, including sentences from the original parallel corpus. Lexical similarity between different articles remains possible.
\par Each continued writer saw 400,000 directed sentences starting at the same visual checkpoint. One received conversational replay. The other received equal numbers of conversational and news pairs, including 61,448 distinct new pairs across four passes. Because news sentences were longer, the writers saw 8.48 and 12.54 million target positions, respectively. Fit time was 52.9 and 52.1 seconds, with 5.91 and 6.41 kJ of device energy. The exposure match is in sentences.
\section*{How much changed in the replies?}
On 32 conversation requests the starting, replay and news-mixed writers received four, two and five credits for meaning. Three paired gains over replay account for the difference. The 8B comparator received 29. On 32 news requests the mixed writer stopped every time and increased mean sentence chrF2 from 9.55 for replay to 13.25. All three visual writers received zero meaning credits. The 8B comparator received 26. Every human reference and unrelated foil met its calibration criterion in both orders across the two domains.
\par The additional data taught the writer more about the shape, overlap and length of editorial language. This comparison does not establish a gain in transferring a proposition across scripts. A stronger test of the semantic representation would teach contrasts in which fluent alternatives differ in precisely who did what, when, or to whom, and require the reader to generate the changed proposition on independently authored pages.
\end{multicols}
