\spread{Can a writer learn who did what to whom?}
\noindent A subject, predicate and object locate different parts of an event. We asked whether encouraging a writer to attend to the corresponding region of a bilingual source helps it translate the whole event. The three writers started from identical weights and read the same 21 books.

The correct target prefix is supplied. The permutation expectation preserves each example's role counts and labelled locations. \textbf{b}, Complete stopped translations on 384 pictured requests from four further works. Each answer needed acceptance in both presentation orders. The separate assessments either supplied or withheld the corpus reference.}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{Learning a location}
For a target glyph annotated with role $r$, let $a_{hi}$ be the attention probability of head $h$ at source window $i$, and $f_{ir}$ the fraction of that window's occupied glyphs with role $r$. The matching mass is $m=H^{-1}\sum_{hi}a_{hi}f_{ir}$. At eligible positions we added $-0.10\log m$ to the summed glyph and stop loss before normalizing by the total number of positions. Transferring mass between two equally labelled windows leaves this objective unchanged, even if the words differ. Lean verifies the identity. On the real development positions the maximum absolute discrepancy between the analytic and automatic logit derivatives was $2.1\times10^{-16}$ in double precision.
\section*{One card and three equal fits}
Every fit received 8,753,476 glyph and stop positions over 2,768 updates. The two supervised conditions each received 3,094,812 annotated targets. Shuffling moved 442,383 of the 878,416 tagged source positions while retaining all within-sentence counts. Each fit took 34--40 seconds from the existing checkpoint. The true-role writer directed 29.78\% of its attention to matching cells, 5.91 percentage points above its permutation expectation. Shuffled supervision also increased attention to tagged positions, reaching 29.27\% and an excess of 5.32 points. The likelihood control's excess was 4.97 points.
\end{multicols}

\spread{The verb in a new book}
\noindent We reserved 48 sentence pairs each from \emph{The Great Gatsby}, \emph{The Republic}, \emph{Water Margin} and a bilingual Bible. The reader received each source as an image, and the writer chose successive physical glyphs until its stop. All four independent visual writers stopped on every request.

Each writer scores the next glyph image and stop. The winning image comes from its fixed output repertoire. The PNGs preserve each chosen bitmap. Each illustrated output reproduced independently from the source image on CPU.}
\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{Whole sentences}
The starting, likelihood, shuffled-role and true-role writers received 10, 10, 9 and 10 source-only credits. With the corpus reference identified, the counts were 5, 7, 6 and 7. The true-role writer earned seven credits in both readings and reproduced one reference exactly. Its source-only difference from the likelihood control was zero in each work. Against shuffled roles the work-resampled 95\% interval ranged from zero to 0.78 percentage points. The four works provide limited independent replication.
\section*{Reading the scale}
Quantized Qwen3-8B, supplied the same sentences as text, earned 315/384 source-only and 198/384 reference-informed credits. Corpus references earned 219 and 383, and unrelated foils earned zero in both. An automated bilingual reader made these assessments. The role-trained writer retained 110/118 controlled families, each requiring eight exact answers. It learned the location objective while the registered improvement in whole meaning failed. The full set of 1,536 original answer PNGs accompanies the article.
\end{multicols}
