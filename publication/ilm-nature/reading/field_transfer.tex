\spread{Can better visual reading improve independent writing?}
\noindent An optical reader trained to reproduce the predictions of a bilingual translator improved translation with that translator's decoder. We asked whether its source information would also help a smaller writer choose the next glyph from its own previous ink.

\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{Two readers, one writing mechanism}
Each system adds a projected 512-dimensional optical feature at each source cell to the original writer's cross-attention memories. Both started with the same zero projection, decoder and motor. We fitted 802,240 weights in each system for four passes over 28,342 English--Chinese pairs from 21 works. Training order and terminal checkpoint selection were identical. At inference the model receives a picture, its occupied length and the language to write. A neural motor predicts the 1,024 pixels of each selected visual code and the decoder reads those pixels before choosing again.
\par In the primary assessment the prediction-trained reader yielded 4/192 complete meanings against 5 for feature regression and 5 for the unchanged writer. The difference from regression was $-0.52$ percentage points (paired four-work bootstrap 95\% interval, $-1.56$ to $0.00$). The exchanged source earned zero. The judge credited the text comparator on 171 requests and the corpus references on 106. Giving the judge the reference changed the corresponding independent counts to three, four and two. The judge and the text comparator share a model family.
\section*{Where the source enters}
Write $L_{ij}$ for the log probability of the correct next glyph or stop. A one in the first or second position supplies the true base image path or learned field respectively. The field's order-averaged contribution is
\[
  \tfrac12[(L_{01}-L_{00})+(L_{11}-L_{10})].
\]
Adding the analogous base contribution recovers $L_{11}-L_{00}$, including when the paths interact. We checked this identity in Lean. On 100 equal-length development pairs into English and 83 into Chinese, the prediction-trained field contributed 0.056 and 0.231 nats per decision. The original path contributed 0.618 and 1.497. Thus the independent writer uses the field, even on the comparison where its free answers lost a credit.
\end{multicols}
\smallskip\noindent{\small\textbf{Aligning the teacher with the pictured answer.} At exact target-piece boundaries, the 32 most likely teacher pieces certified the correct next glyph on 2,447 of 2,941 aligned English and 540 of 1,344 aligned Chinese glyph decisions. The independent writer chose it on 2,202 and 410. Normalizing full-width punctuation obscured subsequent literal Chinese prefixes. Original surface offsets increased Chinese development coverage from 1,422 to 2,878 of 3,197 boundaries, including stop. These are supervised-prefix measurements.}

\section*{A different decoder on the same passages}
A second source-only assessment compared both independent writers with a pretrained translation decoder receiving features from the same optical readers. The prediction-trained field produced 28/192 credited translations with the pretrained decoder and 4/192 with the independent pixel writer. The paired four-work interval for the difference was 4.17 to 24.48 percentage points. The regression field yielded 13 and four respectively. Both systems read the pictured passage. The pretrained decoder also receives learned boundary slots and attention weights, while the independent writer has a second image encoder. The result localizes an opportunity to improve target-side generation, with these interface differences still in play.
\section*{What clean strokes establish}
The two independent systems made different answer PNGs on 109/192 requests. Every emitted cell could be transcribed by exact bitmap match to the known drawing repertoire after generation. A sharp word can therefore be the wrong word. The compact motor learned to reproduce 3,717 drawings and cannot generate an arbitrary new historical form through these codes. Testing language requires the complete, freely written message.
\par Each independent system has 10.48 million parameters and 604,480 stored optical-code scalars. The four final training passes took 24.5 and 21.6 seconds on one RTX 4090~D. These timings exclude the earlier writer, optical readers and teacher. The large quality gap to the text comparator precludes a matched-quality efficiency estimate.
\end{multicols}
