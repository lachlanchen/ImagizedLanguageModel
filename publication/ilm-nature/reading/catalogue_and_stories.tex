\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{A training shortcut}
We paired two different historical forms under each of 2,615 training families. Each entry displayed the headword and two bilingual labels with their source images. Initially the first request was always in Chinese and the second in English. A writer could therefore succeed without reading the requested period. On 64 development families it matched 512 of 1,024 requests, with no family correct under every intervention.
\section*{A matched change to the examples}
We continued from the same page weights twice. Both writers saw every period four times, with the same modern-language replay, optimizer and batch order. In one condition each requested period occurred in both languages and both row orders. Labels stayed attached to the attested shapes in training. We drew fresh evidence strips from the actual presented page in both conditions. Source rasters store a set bit for white. Both continuation fits convert them to the deployed foreground-bit convention. The original development result used the legacy contrast.
\section*{An explanation remains harder}
Asked in English to explain the development of the character on this four-form page, the balanced writer emitted one glyph and stopped. It could identify the requested specimen in eight individual requests but supplied no historical narrative. The underlying library contains 68,750 entry-to-form links and 65,769 different SVG images. Eleven books contribute a separate index of 9,755 pages for future grounded reading.
\end{multicols}

\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{Transfer to new passages}
We selected 32 MCTest and 32 Chinese C\textsuperscript{3} development documents by a fixed hash order with overlap checks against the earlier supervised pools. The visual writer received a pictured question and the aligned page. The released 63.91-million-parameter MiniMind-3 full-SFT model received the same transcribed material and an instruction to answer briefly. Both generated freely. This free-response adaptation has a different scoring task from the original multiple-choice benchmarks.
\section*{A short answer can miss the question}
In English the visual writer correctly named the city as Todd's destination. On the same story it wrote a phrase about going to the city when asked what Todd said after returning. In Chinese it supplied the correct paper used to print banknotes. When asked why each sheet was inspected, it repeated much of the question. Across the two new corpora neither the whole-page visual writer nor MiniMind matched both human wordings on any of the 64 pages. Longer correct paraphrases require semantic grading.
\section*{What MiniMind contributes}
The released text model uses eight causal layers of width 768. Its assistant-only training mask and explicit stop event give a concrete comparison for learning where an answer begins and ends. Using its native loader on one published conversation, 414 of the 549 nonpadding tokens contributed shifted prediction targets. The authors estimate 2.31 hours for one pass each through their small pretraining and instruction datasets on a 3090. Our comparison uses released weights and does not measure their training cost.

\section*{How much did the page teach?}
In the independent sealed split, the original, fixed and balanced writers matched 281, 512 and 1,020 of 1,024 source images at print contrast. Complete families rose from zero in both controls to 63 of 64. These are 64 independent family units. Each yields sixteen related measurements. The formula for the visual writing distribution at one step is
\[
 p(y)=g\,s(y)+(1-g)\!\sum_{i:x_i=y}\!a_i ,
\]
where $s$ is the learned spelling distribution, $a_i$ indexes visible positions and $g$ is the probability of using the spelling branch. Extending the visible area can add physical support for a new glyph. A Lean-checked support identity predicted the change from 5,230 unreachable historical targets per training pass to zero. A second checked bound shows that 1,020 credits on 512 two-target pairs require at least 508 pairs with both answers correct. A separate recount found exactly 508. The fixed continuation never changed its output when only the requested period changed.
\section*{Where the effect stops}
On another 64 sealed families, familiar requests gave 512/512 exact forms; independently worded Chinese and English paraphrases gave 134/256 and 127/256. A rule selecting the printed label with the longest shared glyph sequence solved all 512 paraphrases. Including two period names in an English question changed accuracy to 203/256 with the requested name first and 102/256 with it last. The page writer received 13 of 64 modern semantic credits and MiniMind received five under the same stopping and length rule. The judge accepted 61 of 64 human references twice and rejected 63 of 64 same-page foils twice. In English MCTest the page writer earned one credit to MiniMind's three. On Chinese C\textsuperscript{3} the judge missed two human references and credited a foil. Exact human wordings on 192 modern Chinese exam questions fell from 59 for the parent to 47 for the page writer and 24 after balanced continuation. We retain the latter for historical selection.
\section*{A measured compute boundary}
The whole-page writer and its two reader modules contain 1,179,882 learned parameters. On 416 batch-one requests its timed generation took 29.7 seconds and 2.56 kJ on one RTX 4090 D. MiniMind took 365.1 seconds and 36.77 kJ. Output lengths, decoding units, stopping rates and quality differ. These figures apply to the warmed generation loops and device energy. They do not measure equivalent-quality training or inference.
