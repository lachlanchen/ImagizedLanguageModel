\subsection*{Source-conditioned phrase scoring}
Two continuations of the broad visual writer replayed all 55,872 book pairs once. Each of 873 updates included 64 book pairs and four contrasts sampled in shuffled cycles from 39 training pairs. Each contrast paired two pictured sources with a common pictured answer prefix and two different source-supported completions. We selected the training pairs by editing a deterministic sample of 96 candidates. Both arms trained the writer on the correct completions. Given the observed prefix and preceding cells of a candidate, we scored each next cell by its log softmax over similarities to visual exemplars. The score $s_{ij}$ averages these values for source $i$ and candidate $j$. A match to the next planned source anchor advances the reading position. The crossed writer also minimised $\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{mean}_i\log(1+\exp(s_{i,3-i}-s_{ii}))$ for $i\in\{1,2\}$. This score measures the compatibility of an offered phrase under observed history. Independent generation uses the writer's usual copy, retrieval, drawing and stopping decisions.

The evaluation used a common exemplar bank for all three neural checkpoints. It included any additional glyph shapes in the supplied candidates. We reserved 192 passages from 177 further chapters, selected eighteen whole translations before fitting and compared six archive contrasts with nine authored changes in roles, polarity or quantity. A pair earned choice credit only when each source strictly preferred its designated phrase. Each writer also translated every passage directly from its image. One model reader assessed whole translations and the specified details on shuffled, deduplicated answers with method identities withheld. For quantity questions we recorded explicit comparison or computation separately from translation of the stated facts. The advance criterion required an increase in both correct paired choices and complete faithful archive translations relative to rehearsal.
