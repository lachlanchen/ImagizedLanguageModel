\subsection*{Training and scoring multi-phrase visual replies}
From 73,714 CoQA fitting turns we identified 5,054 with absent multiword phrasing whose normalized words could be assembled from the source, question or previous exchange, and 3,761 requiring at least one additional normalized word. The four allowed physical edits retained a word or removed its first cell, last cell or both. A Lean induction over ordered copy, spell and stop actions shows that the minimum number of spelled words equals the count of unavailable target occurrences when the remaining spellings are writable. The theorem assumes a specified target and sufficient action budget.

We continued each cold parent for 1,500 updates with 32 turns per update. A targeted batch retained 16 uniform draws and added eight turns from each of the two groups. The list curriculum replaced four draws in each targeted group with eight human-annotated MultiSpanQA examples (Li et al., \url{https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2022.naacl-main.90}). It used 3,169 training examples after input, output and page-overlap checks. We joined annotated spans with the word \emph{and} in passage order. Our complete-answer score evaluates this ordering. All 501 input-eligible examples on 497 validation pages entered evaluation. Of these, 417 reference answers met the sixteen-word length bound. On a single RTX 4090 D the visual targeted and list-training continuations took 21.9 and 27.1 seconds (2.70 and 3.34 kJ). The matched byte fits took 23.3 and 24.1 seconds (2.79 and 3.16 kJ). These measurements cover optimizer updates only.

We separately reserved 200 CoQA training-source stories containing 321--448 words, excluded during initial fitting. We ordered stories by a fixed salted hash after excluding canonical matches with earlier dialogue material, official development and both list partitions. All eligible questions entered the denominator, regardless of answer length. These stories carry one human answer per turn. The 407 difficult turns included 239 normalized copy assemblies and 168 requiring additional wording. We measured complete, stopped normalized answers under the original input, question-and-history exchange and passage exchange. Intervals resampled whole stories with replacement.

For a supplied word image $w_t$, we summed the probability of every copy edit yielding that image and the probability of spelling and terminating that word, weighted by the respective route probabilities. We multiplied these word probabilities along the candidate and included the final stop. For two questions and two supplied answers, let $s_{ij}$ be the stopped log likelihood of answer $j$ given question $i$. A correct joint assignment requires $s_{11}+s_{22}>s_{12}+s_{21}$. An additive preference for either candidate cancels in this comparison. We used 158 disjoint question pairs from 108 stories. Candidate-free search retained eight live copy prefixes at each depth through sixteen words. It ranked completed sequences by mean log probability per decision, including stop. We used reference strings to score the proposals after generation.
