\subsection*{Locating evidence for two questions}
We asked whether a writer could choose different facts on the same pictured page. On a Chinese account of a damselfish, the model wrote its common name in response to the first question and the taxonomic classification in response to the second. Both matched the references. A question about a fossil discovery on another page elicited a two-character fragment instead of the location. Figure~\ref{fig:paired-evidence}a preserves the question and reply images.

On 256 newly reserved passages, the preceding optical reader gave 25 of 512 complete answers and answered both questions correctly on zero passages. We then trained a reader that assigns probability to whole intervals of source ink as well as freely generated answers. It learned from different human questions about the same training passages. It answered 190 questions and passed 43 paired passages. Providing human-marked evidence locations during fitting gave 195 answers and 44 paired passages. Nineteen passages passed only with evidence supervision and eighteen only without it (exact two-sided paired $P=1$). The comparison supports the combined change in reader and training protocol. Its narrower evidence-supervision contrast remains inconclusive. All four corpora, including the two English corpora, contributed paired successes (Fig.~\ref{fig:paired-evidence}b).

Changing a unique, explicitly stated numeral while holding the pictured question fixed gave correct responses on both versions for 20 of 28 items. Substituting unseen digit-shaped bitmaps gave both original and physically correct edited output on seven. Erasing the question eliminated all 44 paired successes. A model receiving bytes and writing the same glyphs scored 213/512 individually and 52/256 in pairs. Its measured training energy, including the preceding writer stages, was 38.0\,kJ, compared with 37.5\,kJ for the optical system (Fig.~\ref{fig:paired-evidence}c). The optical reader wrote only two correct answers to 32 English dialogue questions for which all four human wordings were absent from the visible passage. It also failed to write both opposite answers on any of 120 external Winograd pairs. Free book continuations still repeated short phrases.
