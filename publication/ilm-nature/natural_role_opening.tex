\noindent\textbf{Can a small model read the marks on a page and recover who did what? Given a pictured English sentence and the location of its verb, a 312,244-parameter reader located and returned both participant head words in 901 of 1,196 test clauses from two treebanks. A matched position control recovered 48 pairs. In a third treebank the reader recovered 347 of 478 pairs. Generalization depended on the construction. It found both heads in 890/1,164 active clauses and 11/32 passives on the two official tests. In subject clefts it returned a relative pronoun where the question asked for the named person. A separate writer attempting free Chinese--English translation conveyed only one complete book meaning in 192 requests. These experiments distinguish interpreting a relation in pictured text from composing a new sentence.}\par
\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{A question with two pictured answers}
We used 8,973 training clauses from the \href{https://universaldependencies.org/treebanks/en_ewt/}{English Web Treebank} and \href{https://universaldependencies.org/treebanks/en_gum/}{GUM}. In active clauses the answers are the subject and object heads. In explicit-agent passives they are the by-agent and passive subject. The marked predicate identifies the event. The reader scores four glyph boundaries and selects two disjoint ink spans with maximum total score. It emits their original pixels. The position control sees the same feasible spans, predicate location and sentence length.

\begin{multicols}{2}
\section*{What the participant marks conceal}
With both candidate words marked, a separate 202,001-parameter reader chose their order correctly on all 1,196 official clauses. Independent checking reproduced every gold label and found zero test sentence images in training. Extra blank cells broke the marked reader on 84/84 constructed actives. Compacting runs of blank cells repaired every case. When \emph{Dr} and \emph{by} occupied the same positions in active and passive pictures, the full reader chose both correctly in all 84 pairs. The layout control chose both correctly in none.
\section*{From a head word to its referent}
Without candidate marks the locator found both names in 83/84 active and 77/84 passive controlled sentences. In all 84 subject clefts of the form \emph{It is Anna who helps David}, it returned \emph{who} as the actor. The annotated-clause training objective rewards grammatical heads. Recovering their referents requires another relation. Both image models ran on one RTX 4090 D. The open locator's 2,400 updates took 27 seconds. \repo{references/natural_pictured_roles_result.md}{Source images, evaluation and model}.
