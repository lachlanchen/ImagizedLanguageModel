\subsection*{Looking up a character from a pictured question}
The supplied-plate test gave the reader the relevant page. We next withheld its location. A reference collection held 6,971 distinct pictured headwords, each with up to six fields spanning recorded meanings, character structure and historical exemplars. A 257,577-parameter reader located the headword in the pictured question and selected fields in mention order. The system then returned their stored ink (Fig.~\ref{fig:glyph-atlas}). For a question about the evolution of the training headword \textit{yan} (speech), it returned the recorded meaning and four attested forms. For the held headword \textit{ma} (horse), it reproduced the bronze and oracle forms in the requested reverse chronological order.

Among 795 held headwords, the first reader completed all 6,360 two-field requests in familiar frames. New wording reduced complete replies to 3,726. Keeping its field predictions and supplying the correct entry raised the count to 6,262. We therefore retained the original fitting budget and added 20 training frames. Across another 6,360 questions in fresh frames, complete replies rose from 2,979 to 4,268 (20.3 percentage points; exploratory focal-entry bootstrap interval 18.7--21.9). The broader reader completed all four crossed headword and field-order requests in 942 of 1,590 groups. It returned 3,368 exact replies on 5,056 questions with at least one available field. Its byte counterpart completed 2,349 two-field requests. Giving the image reader the correct entry raised the count to 6,070. Headword families were held out during fitting; the fresh sentences recombined the same test families. Both training sets used authored question frames.

On a 96-page volume assembled from existing four-row plates with unique headwords, the previous reader returned 1,536 exact stopped answers when supplied with the correct page. Adding the learned headword lookup recovered 895 pages and 895 exact answers. A different rule forced the question pointer to select the most likely glyph appearing in the book. This raised correct replies from 3,941 to 5,676 on 6,360 new requests. After removing each requested record, the number of wrong record selections rose from 410 to 4,770. We retained the reader with unconstrained attention followed by exact lookup. The results establish access to pictured evidence under a fixed field schema. Writing an original historical explanation or inferring an unattested form requires different evidence.
