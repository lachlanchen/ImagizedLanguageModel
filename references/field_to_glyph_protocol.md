# Can a prediction-trained optical reader teach an independent glyph writer?

An optical source reader trained to match a frozen translator's predictions earned 22/192 complete meanings against ten for continued feature regression. Both used a pretrained piece decoder at evaluation. On the same passages the independent neural pixel writer earned two. Test the source-field difference in a deployed system that reads images, predicts ink, feeds back its own raster and stops.

Attach a zero-initialized linear projection of the frozen 512-dimensional visual source field at each occupied source cell to the two cross-attention memories of the independent decoder. Start each arm at the released pixel writer. Train the projection, causal decoder and its final normalization on four full passes over the 28,342 paired book passages in both directions. Freeze the source encoder, optical readers, choice vectors, choice head and pixel motor. Use the same shuffled length-bucketed minibatches, initial parameters, target loss, AdamW optimizer, gradient limit of one and terminal checkpoint. The only arm difference is whether the fixed field came from regression or prediction supervision. Training may cache exact-position visual fields. Deployed inference must compute them from the source PNG alone. Limit each fit to 12 GiB allocated and one hour on GPU 0.

Choose the next 24 disjoint passage pairs per work from the same four previously assessed titles. Exclude the prior training, assessment and optical-duplicate pools, including both recent literary panels. The primary outcome is a stopped, whole-meaning answer accepted in both presentation orders of the source-only model judge. Assess the two learned writers, untouched motor, within-work length-matched source exchange for the prediction arm, Qwen3-8B Q4 text translations, human references and foils on the same 192 requests. Compare both directions and report a paired four-work bootstrap with exact arithmetic at the interval endpoints. To qualify the change, prediction must outperform both the unchanged motor and the matched regression reader, with a strictly positive lower 95% bound against regression, exceed exchanged-source credits, and stop on at least 95% of inputs. Retain every original PNG, failed answer and cap. Compare gold-prefix held next-glyph likelihood on the 240 development pairs, including after source exchange, to distinguish changes in reading from changes in fluency. Publish the first differing outcome and the first fixed-order failure. If the semantic test fails, use these interventions to choose the next experiment.
