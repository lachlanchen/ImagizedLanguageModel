# The same natural question with two different viewpoints

A useful reader must distinguish what two real passages say about the same question. We prepared paired DuReader examples in which human annotators supplied both an affirmative and a negative answer to the same question, with each answer traceable to a different passage. The intended output gives the position expressed in the supplied passage and quotes the human answer as evidence. The paired answers can both be scored without treating either stance as an objectively true assertion.

We surveyed the full available archive. Of 24,460 training and 797 official development yes/no records, 5,544 and 218 distinct eligible questions had opposed annotations. Requiring both answers to appear in distinct, human-selected paragraphs left 2,344 training candidates and 36 development candidates under normalized matching. Requiring both original answer strings to occur literally in their own 192-cell window, excluding the opposing answer from that window, limiting the final image to 64 cells, checking the rendered font and separating normalized questions and development passage runs yielded **949 training pairs and 18 official development pairs**. These windows were selected offline using the references. The task measures interpretation given evidence. Retrieval from an unlabelled corpus requires a separate test. [Corpus screening](evidence/english_request_writer_20261119/natural_stance_feasibility.json) · [Preparation hashes](evidence/english_request_writer_20261119/natural_stance_preparation.json)

Inputs and targets are local 32-by-32 glyph streams. The stance prefix is 肯定。 or 否定。 followed by the unaltered human answer. For 35 of 36 development responses at least one stance character is physically absent from both passage and instructed question. Both explanations in a pair are real human text supplied in their respective evidence. Producing a missing stance character would demonstrate a fixed decision expressed in writing, while quotation measures extraction. Neither alone establishes free composition.

An independent pass reread the raw research-only archive and reconstructed all 1,934 input and answer images. The preceding 694,152-parameter ordered writer, given the pictured instruction and question, generated zero exact stance prefixes and zero full answers on the 36 responses. It stopped on 34. A fixed printed-marker rule got 24 stances right and both stances right on six of 18 pairs. A reader ignoring the passage can get at most one of the opposed labels right per pair. Eighteen official test pairs allow only a coarse estimate. Reserve a larger training-only validation split by question and passage before designing the next fit, and keep all official questions intact.
