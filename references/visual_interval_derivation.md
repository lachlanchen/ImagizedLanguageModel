# From an answer interval to a sequence of glyph decisions

Give every valid interval [i,j] of at most 48 cells the unnormalized mass exp(aᵢ+bⱼ), where the reading model scores the two physical endpoints. For a prefix u, let M(u) sum the interval weights whose glyph streams begin with u. Let E(u) sum those whose streams equal u. Then the probability of stopping at u is E(u)/M(u), and the probability of continuing with a specific image x is M(ux)/M(u). In the product of all continuation probabilities followed by stopping, every intermediate M cancels. The resulting complete-answer probability is E(u)/M(empty). Equal streams from repeated source occurrences contribute their combined mass. This calculation also applies to an inscription without a character encoding.

At prefix length k, each continuing occurrence starting at i points to source position i+k. We compute its weight by summing all admissible right endpoints beyond that position. A stopped occurrence has right endpoint i+k−1. Suffix log-sum-exp computes all these masses within a width-48 window. A teacher can disagree with an annotation, so we compare mixtures against both hard source alignment and ordinary replay. All empirical losses are measured on annotated histories. The student's physical next-image distribution receives probability from its fixed image inventory and its source pointer. Training the joint copy position charges the student for using the correct route as well as emitting the correct ink. Summing over possible routes can only increase the probability of the observed image.

The Lean proofs establish exact integer-mass identities for conditioning and stopping. The floating-point implementation is checked against explicit enumeration of intervals, including repeated bitmaps, padding and maximum-length replies. These identities establish the training target, while the held-out tests establish how much of it a finite writer learns.
