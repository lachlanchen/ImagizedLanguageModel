# Reading two different facts on one pictured page

The short-answer model read the pictured question and page without a text transcription or an external model at inference. Its English sentence writer read the emitted fact image together with the question. On a new set of 256 English and Chinese passages, the contrast-trained reader answered both questions on 34 passages. Its visual parent solved 32 and a likelihood-only refit solved 33. The contrast reader gained 14 against the parent and lost 12 (two-sided exact McNemar P = 0.845). It solved eight English and 26 Chinese pairs; likelihood training solved six and 27. The recorded adoption criterion failed. The original remains the default. ## How the model was trained

Each of three fits drew 128,000 questions from the same 32,527 training pages, including 16,630 SQuAD, 5,760 CoQA, 2,233 CMRC and 7,904 DRCD passages. Each training pair had visibly distinct answers, with all exact physical occurrences located. A visual likelihood fit maximized the total probability of the correct surface. Another visual fit and a matched byte fit also penalized the second question's answer under the first question. The frozen book model, free writing parameters and English statement writer were retained. In the 2,442,424-parameter optical short-answer pipeline, 236,052 parameters received gradients. Adding the English statement writer brings the independent pipeline to 2,787,303 parameters. The evaluation reserved 64 additional page families per corpus and imposed no visible-answer requirement. Content was capped at 600 aligned glyphs per passage and 160 per question including conversation history. For a two-answer comparison, let the rounded correct-minus-competing log masses be d₁ and d₂ on the respective questions. The optimal question-independent bias for one answer gives a worst margin of (d₁ + d₂)/2. Lean checks necessity, sufficiency and attainment on signed integer scores with half-unit offsets. Log masses were rounded in units of 0.0001 nats. For 204/207 contrast-reader pairs some pair-specific oracle bias can yield two positive margins. The ordinary reader must choose the right surface among all intervals, including stopping and free generation. On 449 questions with an accepted contiguous surface of at most 48 cells, it wrote 175 correct answers. Its best individual interval was exact on 181, rising to 324 given a correct start or 319 given a correct end. In 53 cases neither oracle alone sufficed. A second Lean identity shows that the model's length-corrected additive score cannot express a residual preference reversal between the same two ends at two starts. These statements describe the score family and inform a subsequent architectural experiment. ## Same questions for quantized local models

On the same 512 questions, Qwen3-8B Q4_K_M answered 298 correctly from text and solved both questions on 101/256 pages. On the first 16 reserved pages per corpus, Qwen3-VL-8B Q4_K_M saw identical question and page PNGs to the ILM. It answered 63/128 correctly and solved 21/64 pairs, versus 43/128 and 6/64 for contrast ILM. The same Qwen3-VL weights reading the underlying text gave 83/128 and 28/64 pairs. Temperature was zero and comparisons used normalized stopped answers. Pretraining exposure to the public datasets is unknown for both general models. Wider semantic scoring could credit alternative phrasings. [All counts](evidence/competing_facts_v1/benchmark.json) · [Saved Qwen visual answers](evidence/competing_facts_v1/qwen8vl-image-outputs.json.gz)

The full optical inference took 4.37 s and 517 J on GPU0 with batches of 16; every bitmap and stop matched the recorded result. Qwen3-8B text inference took 87.72 s and 14,842 J with four concurrent requests. The 128-question image subset took 1.00 s and 136 J for the optical model and 152.89 s and 29,390 J for Qwen3-VL. Qwen image timing includes PNG encoding and serving; optical timing uses already prepared glyph grids, model setup and generation checks. The Qwen3-VL text run followed its image run with resident weights. These workload costs accompany different accuracy. The contrast refit consumed 45.79 s and 6.23 kJ on top of its retained training stages. ## Reading the original output

Asked what NSFNET promoted, the base reader wrote “no”. The contrast reader selected “advanced research and education networking”; its English writer produced “NSFNET promoted research and education networking.” It also changed 愛德華 to 愛德華一世 on a question about a monarch. On a question about the period when Xiushui was called Fenning, it changed the correct 北宋 to the incorrect 1914年. We selected the first passages in each prespecified change category. Across 22 held questions, CPU reproduction from saved PNGs matched 66 fact images and 30 English statement images from the GPU. Across 24 numeral edits the contrast reader answered both versions on 18, versus 17 for the original. [Original images](../models/competing_facts/examples) · [Individual replay records](evidence/competing_facts_v1/physical-replays.json)

The fixed English sentence writer gave 9/96 exact QA2D statements with the new reader, up from eight. On 96 additional SQuAD families independently reserved for the earlier study its short-fact accuracy declined from 25 to 22 and the count of stopped sentences containing an accepted fact rose from 27 to 30. Among the first 24, one project model reviewer credited six complete grounded statements versus four before. Reviewers of model outputs had project familiarity. In a separate review of all 26 flipped pairs, counting unambiguous extra wording gave 17 fully answered pages for the contrast reader and 15 for the original. These selected comparisons do not measure general semantic accuracy. Ordinary book continuation, scan segmentation, historical origin explanations and parity with general models remain open.
