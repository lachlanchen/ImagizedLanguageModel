# Two questions in one prompt image

A single contiguous passage span can reproduce two of 6,086 ordered development targets. The earlier reader given both question boundaries and an external composer reached 440, including 220 of 3,043 exchanged pairs in both orders. It answered 434 of 1,788 individual questions exactly. These are development passages split from the original CMRC training release. The wrapper and separator are shared across sets. [Corpus and split](paired_passage_questions_preparation.md)

Train a four-action convolutional planner on the complete prompt image. It marks instruction material, the question to read, punctuation to reproduce, and the last question cell that requests termination. Use preparation boundaries for planner supervision. For each predicted question run, the shared passage reader selects a span by image-conditioned start and end probabilities. The writer concatenates the selected physical spans and any punctuation selected in the prompt. It can emit several spans or fail to stop. This mechanism is restricted to source and prompt ink. It receives ordered 32-pixel cells of known extent and has a single wrapper in training.

Freeze the original image features and train the planner six epochs on every ordered training pair and single question. Then train the original span reader eight epochs on the 7,235 unique single-question records. For source image x, question image q and target image y, minimize the negative log of the sum of p(start | x,q) p(end | start,x,q) over all cell-aligned spans whose pixels equal y. Deduplicating repeated clause supervision weights each original question equally. Use AdamW at 0.001 for the planner and 0.00025 for the reader, seed 19303, final weights only. Cap this serial job at 30 minutes and 86% of GPU 0 memory. The registration binds the corpus, source and initialization. Warm-start costs belong to the preceding reader.

Compare the frozen original reader plus the same trained planner, the original externally split composer, the final planner and reader, and an inference perturbation that puts each pair into canonical order by comparing the two intact image sequences. The perturbation alone has access to the original split. Record every output and stop, single retention, both-orders accuracy, and exact segmentation. Use the first successful swapped pair and the first failed request in original order for figures. Measure candidate-minus-original uncertainty by resampling development passages together. Each exchanged pair with unequal targets has an exact upper bound of one of two for any deterministic order-invariant system.

Require more than 440 complete pairs, more than 220 both-order successes, and at least 434 exact singles to advance the new reader. On failure, retain the original reader and localize error to the planner or evidence span. On success, test a new, source-disjoint set and a second prompt construction before advancing the general language claim. Preserve free writing, dictionary reading, handwriting and total compute as separate measures.
