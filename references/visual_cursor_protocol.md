# Writing through a latent source position

The 2.12M-parameter bilingual writer learned to predict held passages, yet its first free English instruction reply repeated parts of the question and it completed zero external Winograd pairs. A single optical cell provides weak evidence for where a multi-glyph name starts and ends. Test whether carrying a distribution over the source position through writing improves complete replies. After any printed glyph the model can continue at the next source position, switch to any visible position, or draw from a free visual writer. Every transition, including stopping, is inferred. Train by summing over hidden positions compatible with the answer image. Supervision consists solely of pictured questions, passages and answers.

Keep the preceding optical writer frozen. Compare two identical new networks with and without movement from one source position to the next. Use 2,400 matched updates of 24 pages per arm. Alternate six human QA batches with two general writing batches in every eight. For human supervision cycle through English CoQA, simplified CMRC2018 and traditional DRCD, with separate official development passages. Show the query first, the previous question and answer for CoQA, and the beginning of the passage up to a total of 768 visible cells. Choose the crop without consulting the answer. Limit each training output window to 48 cells, supervising a stop only at a genuine end. Keep the preceding instruction, reference and book sources in the replay. Evaluate on human questions grouped by unseen passage, two different questions and answers per document, up to 64 documents per source in fixed hash order. Report incomplete or clipped evidence, complete answers, source-absent targets, both answers on the same page, and swapped-question sensitivity. Preserve the original Winograd pairs, free English and Chinese fiction and instruction responses. A copying gain with poor general writing warrants a narrower claim. Use one 4090 D, fixed final weights, a 20 GiB device ceiling, and retain terminal outputs.
