# Transfer the stronger pictured Chinese reader to English book questions

On 36 reserved passages the bidirectional visual reader gave 45/72 exact Chinese-request replies, compared with 25 for the released causal writer. The reader gave only 1/72 on equivalent English questions. Sequence preference supervision taught the writer 582/642 training comparisons but gave 31/144 exact free replies. Retire that fit and the blank-question score correction. Use the stronger Chinese-reading signal directly for a bounded bilingual evidence experiment. [Measured findings](stopped_book_preference_result.md)

Keep the reader's pixel encoder and full passage. Train the final contextual layers to assign probability to the correct bounded interval among all physically possible source intervals up to 48 glyphs, summing mass over repeated occurrences and human alternatives. Compare matching Chinese and English questions with English questions exchanged between facts on the same page. Keep source, target intervals, optical representation, initial weights, batch order, exposures and resource limits matched. At inference only pictured passage and question are supplied. An extracted span is one source of evidence for a future writer. The ability to compose absent material still requires a separately evaluated image decoder.

The prepared training split has 11,439 parallel question pairs on 2,909 pages and 2,536 pairs with multiple acceptable intervals. Twelve of the original pairs exceeded the reader's 512-position budget including the exchanged question. Use the 36 and 34 previously evaluated literary passages for development. Before promotion reserve questions on additional works and include a separate modern source. Require English-request gains over the parent and matched exchange, preservation of Chinese evidence reading, and more complete four-request passages than both comparisons. Retain the released writer for historical forms and causally generated replies. If bilingual transfer fails, isolate weak English question representations before attempting to attach a writer. If it succeeds, test whether the same learned evidence improves the writer's freely stopped answers under both question languages. Record the whole-system cost of any additional inference model.
