# Can a visual reader assemble a complete answer?

A visual writer given a pictured passage and question returned **Tina Turner and Laurence Fishburne**. The uniform-training control wrote **Angela Bassett**. Both checkpoints rendered these replies independently from the same two PNGs. This is the first difficult list question in corpus order for which list training gained an exact answer that lost credit under each of two input exchanges. All five emitted words came from visible word images. A complete reply must stop and match a normalized human answer. For sensitivity, it must lose that match under both a question/history exchange and a separate passage exchange. On lists, target spans follow passage order with **and** between them. This custom score measures writing the full list in order. Of 396 difficult list questions, 370 required complete wording unseen in fitted targets. Nine of the ten exchange-sensitive visual matches occurred in those 370. The single-seed change in word F1 across all list questions was +22.93 percentage points (95% story-bootstrap interval +20.05 to +25.86). On difficult dialogue it was +1.08 points (−0.44 to +2.63). The conversation test used one human answer per turn from 200 previously unused CoQA training-source passages. All 3,004 input-eligible turns entered evaluation. ## What the writer can recognize and propose

We held two natural candidate answers fixed and scored their complete, stopped likelihoods for two different questions about one story. The visual list writer chose the correct joint assignment for 139/158 pairs with empty history (95% story interval 82.2–93.0%). Only 37 pairs had both independent choices correct. Swapping the passage reduced the joint score to 117/158, a difference of 13.9 points (5.4–22.2). Even with the wrong passage, many assignments remained possible. Across the 72 pairs with zero normalized overlap between either question and either candidate, the original and exchanged scores were 65 and 51. Supplying actual history yielded 118/158 joint assignments. [Four log likelihoods per pair](evidence/compose_dialogue_v1/responses/pairs) · [Source-exchange results](evidence/compose_dialogue_v1/exchange.json)

Summing pointer probability for identical output images at inference raised list exactness to 52/501 and the difficult two-exchange count to 11. The visual writers still scored zero exact on 407 difficult dialogue turns. A separate reference-free copy search held eight live prefixes and returned up to 32 stopped proposals. On 396 difficult lists it reached 24 top-one matches, 17 sensitive to both exchanges, and 68 matches anywhere in the first 32. On the 239 difficult conversational turns with copyable normalized words it reached **0 top-one and 1 top-32**. One reference also appeared in the first 32 after passage exchange. On the single original dialogue hit, the reference ranked eleventh and the top proposal was a close paraphrase. The earlier failure on *My Left Foot* was more substantial: the greedy writer named David Helfgott for Christy Brown. ## Mathematics and cost

The Lean development proves a lower bound on the number of spelling actions for any successful copy/spell path. A constructive proof attains it if every unavailable word is writable. It also proves invariance of the paired log-score inequality under additive question and answer biases for integer scores. The proofs describe representability and how to interpret paired assignments. Which answers the network proposes is an empirical question. [Checked Lean source](../formal/span_list/RequiredSpelling.lean)

Each continuation trained 547,125 parameters for 1,500 AdamW steps on one RTX 4090 D with matched image/byte batch indices. The visual assembly and list fits took 21.9 and 27.1 seconds and 2.70 and 3.34 kJ. The byte fits took 23.3 and 24.1 seconds and 2.79 and 3.16 kJ. These are incremental optimizer costs; the model records also give preparation and parent hashes. No matched-quality energy advantage is established. Aligned printed English and a training letter palette define the scope of this comparison. [Released weights and instructions](../models/compose_dialogue/README.md) · [Cold-parent energy comparison](dialogue_efficiency_result.md)

The candidate-supplied measurements suggest the reader has a question-dependent signal. The search experiment locates a bottleneck before final answer selection. The next comparison will train a whole-answer draft from the pictured question and passage, measure reference-free proposal coverage and both-exchange exact replies on newly reserved stories, and retain matched byte and unchanged decoder controls. A gain on the list task alone would replicate the limited result above.
