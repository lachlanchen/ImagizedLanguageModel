# Compose modern glosses from observed image fragments

The block writer learned a dependence on word order, but its source-only free writing overproduces common function glyphs. A majority transfer using a five-cell neighborhood improved bitmap distance and gave two acceptable translations in the frozen 24-passage reading. Test whether a writer which retains observed multi-cell correspondences can transfer coherent phrases to another passage.

Use the same first 32,768 aligned training rows. Estimate source-phrase to output-phrase counts from monotone edit alignments. Both sides are sequences of exact 32-by-32 images. Use contiguous spans of one through six cells, at least two observations, source copying for unseen ink and a smoothed target bigram transition across phrase boundaries. Combine observed translation probabilities and a boundary cost with monotone beam search. The output alphabet for translated content consists of observed training images. Preserve source-only out-of-alphabet glyphs on the copy path. This method cannot synthesize new letter shapes. Fixed controls are direct copying and the immediately preceding local majority model.

Set beam width to four. Choose the language weight from 0, 0.25, 0.75 on the first 512 passages from a separately hashed subset of training chapters, minimizing total bitmap edits with deterministic ties. Refit once with all training rows. Evaluate the previously unused held-out rows 2,048 through 4,095 in preparation order. Report overlap with the earlier evaluation chapters and training targets, distance to copying and local counts, changed complete exact translations and a contextual reading blind to method on 24 chapter-distinct passages. Retain original streams and labels. Stop after the comparison and characterize remaining errors before spending more compute.
