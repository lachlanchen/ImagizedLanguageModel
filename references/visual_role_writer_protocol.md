# Learning which participant a pictured statement describes

A visual writer gave identical judgments in 46 of 48 paired changes to the subject of a pictured statement. It recovered 524 missing Chinese phrases and scored 1,610/2,947 on natural inference. Test whether teaching the association between a participant and a predicate can change the freely written answer. Subject exchange is a documented diagnostic of inference shortcuts. [Previous observations](visual_inference_writer_result.md) · [McCoy et al., 2019](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1334/) · [Min et al., 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.212/)

Train both arms from the same final paired OCNLI writer, retaining its learned visual descriptors and writing keys. Each update includes up to 64 unmodified natural judgments, up to 16 natural restorations and six crossed two-person situations. Draw predicates and complements from the original Chinese fit claims containing a first- or third-person predicate (second person is also eligible). Generate an affirmative and negative form with the same complement. Assign separate names to the two clauses, randomize which sign comes first, exchange the people, and ask about each person. Half of the situations request a judgment. The other half request a full reordered clause placing the participant after 的是. The output must include both evidence and participant. Both predicates, names and model emissions consist of glyph images. The program uses text for preparing stimuli and scoring known responses. No author-created diagnostic sentence is a training example.

The matched condition preserves all four input–answer associations. The control independently swaps the two pictured premises for the entire square with probability one half, preserving exact marginal exposures, names, queries and written targets. Training and sampling seeds, architecture, natural replay, five epochs, initial weights and optimizer schedule are identical. Train one arm at a time on GPU0, with 1,200 seconds and 8 GiB allocated per fit. Use the last update without model selection.

Score all 48 earlier role-exchange decisions and 32 additional pairs from eight newly worded situations, written before fitting. Score all filtered validation predicates with new names and no training-complement overlap, in familiar and unseen formatting. Record both orders, both queries, all four correct, identical responses, exact arbitrary-length stopped answers and complete bitmaps. The familiar and unusual layouts share test content. The natural check uses all 2,947 official development claims, all 35 complete triples, the fixed 105-row Qwen comparison, internal validation, and the identical masks. Preserve independent English and Chinese dictionary writing, DRCD and DuReader tests. The former official split has already been examined; this is a follow-up using fixed comparisons.

Require a positive matched–randomized cluster-bootstrap lower bound on both the existing and new independent role-switch sets for a transfer claim. Require exact new-name, new-complement full responses above the randomized control to credit productive binding. Report natural inference against the parent and the earlier 1,689/2,947 sparse visual baseline. Any losses in complete writing or lexical generalization remain part of the outcome. If role changes transfer poorly, identify which part of the reversal the learner follows before adding more training. No result from these constrained tasks establishes open language, historical writing, or total-compute parity.
