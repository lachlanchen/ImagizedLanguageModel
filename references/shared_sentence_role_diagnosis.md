# Why a legible pictured answer can say the wrong thing

The watch repairer 江映 returned a watch to its owner 余青. Asked who returned it, the visual writer produced 江映. Asked who originally owned it, the writer produced 修表铺收工时 (“at closing time at the watch shop”). Exchanging the names changed its first answer to 余青, correctly, while its answer about the owner became the single character 修. These are the first four outputs in evaluation order. [Original wording and expected names](../scripts/explicit_participant_cases.py)

We restored the calibrated checkpoint on CPU and reproduced all 96 stored answer images and stop decisions. The writer selected the copy action 89 times. Twenty-one answer images contained exactly the requested name, 14 contained exactly the other name, and 61 matched neither. In 14 of the 48 pairs of opposite questions about identical evidence, it returned exactly the same pixels. [Frozen scores and per-question results](evidence/shared_sentence_writer_20261119/explicit_error_diagnosis.json)

We then gave the scorer the locations of **both** complete names in each sentence and compared their existing scores. Where a name occurred twice, its higher score counted. This retrospective calculation uses known name boundaries that the deployed system must discover. The requested name scored higher on 53/96 questions, with zero ties. A fixed choice of the first person mentioned scores 48/96 by construction. For 39/48 pairs the model preferred the same person for both questions, although the correct answers differ. It preferred the right person under both questions and both name orders in zero of the 24 scenes. No parameter or archived answer changed. [Reproduce the diagnosis](../scripts/diagnose_explicit_role_answers.py)

The model makes two distinct errors in this test. It often chooses material outside either name, and supplying the exact names still leaves it weak at connecting a question to the requested participant. A sharper renderer would draw the same wrong phrase more clearly. The next training comparison should hold the participants fixed while varying who performed or received the action, then measure transfer under unseen predicates, phrasing and name order. The 24 inspected scenes should remain available for regression testing. A fresh unseen evaluation set is needed to claim generalization after further development.
