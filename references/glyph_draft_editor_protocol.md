# Learning to edit a pictured phrase draft

The fixed phrase inventory raises content retention but excludes all 96 exact new-book reference images. Draft-only Qwen editing earned four complete meanings. Source-assisted editing earned 73 while the source-only teacher earned 86 in the same assessment. Learn an independent reader/writer with direct access to the original source and optional access to the fallible phrase draft. Every printed target glyph and the learned stop remain available at every step.

Before fitting, reserve twelve eligible pairs per work from four further books, excluding earlier panels and training images. Use the previously trained visual reader/writer as initialization. Train two identically initialized writers, one with the draft and one with it masked. Give each the same source, target, minibatch order, optimizer schedule and opportunity to learn. Use a uniform seeded sample of 32,768 real pairs from the optical corpus plus earlier controlled training events after explicit held-source checks. Produce drafts with the exact published phrase decoder, without access to the training target during draft generation. The phrase table was previously estimated on the real training corpus, so training drafts are in-sample for that table. Use four passes and a single 4090-class GPU. Cap the fit at 30 minutes and 15 GiB allocated per arm. Bound CPU draft preparation to six workers and two hours. Preserve hashes for resumable shards.

Compare new full-draft and masked writers with correct and exchanged drafts, the unmodified visual writer, and the hard phrase writer. Test unrestricted outputs on fresh books and on the previously frozen 236 paired role/negation prompts. Grade book meaning in both candidate orders alongside supplied references, text-input Qwen and unrelated answers. Require more complete book meanings than both the matched source-only arm and original writer, with no loss against the original on paired polarity and exchanged roles. Report controls, uncertainty over four works, original pixels, draft dependence, stopping, and full deployment footprint. If scores fail, determine whether the draft altered proposed content or merely changed local wording.
