# Can paired interval boundaries improve visual reading?

The held boundary intervention and the length-corrected rectangle identity motivate a question-conditioned interaction between the start and end of a printed fact. Compare two new rank-eight readouts initialized at the released **original** optical reader. Both use the same left and right projections of its contextual, question-conditioned ink, including the same number of trainables. One adds the difference-free sum of the two ends' independently transformed coordinates. The other adds their inner product. Both use the same complete interval likelihood, 4,000 batches of 32, and identical sample and learning rate schedules as the optical likelihood refit. Keep the same encoder architecture, shared writer and greedy stop. Only the endpoint readout differs between the two arms. Initialize both as zero corrections. Evaluate final weights only. Reserve 32 further passage families per corpus with both questions, excluding all training, older tests and the complete competing-facts test. Compare original, parameter-matched additive and interacting models. The primary outcome is both accepted stopped answers on the same new page. Require more completely solved pairs than both controls in each language to select the interacting model for further work. Report gains and losses, matching individual answers and supplied-versus-inferred limits. Failure retains the existing default and leaves the broader reader and composition hypotheses open. Cap each fit at 12 minutes and 12 GiB on GPU0. Use a reproducible per-step sampler, optimizer checkpoint and cursor for resumption.
