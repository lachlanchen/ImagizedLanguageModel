# Contextual reading of freely generated phrases

A modern translator can choose a different sound phrasing from the recorded one. Review a stratified sample of actual fresh outputs in complete surrounding sentences to examine this gap. Use 24 single-glyph and 24 multi-glyph reference sites picked by SHA256 priority, one per chapter, excluding the two first-in-order illustrations. The two strata each have equal weight in this descriptive sample. At each site show the original classical sentence, the surrounding modern reference outside the gap, and the three fillings from the local ink stream, local whole-phrase counts and the recorded translation. Score only the fit of the bracketed material. Deduplicate identical fillings at a site and shuffle distinct versions by a hash of their pixels. Display the original pixels if a generated shape lacks a transcription. Freeze the packet and all decisions before joining labels to the sources. Mark each filling acceptable, incompatible or uncertain. Accept an alternative if it yields a grammatical, meaning-preserving rendering at that location. Keep uncertain readings and disclose that the reader is a model involved in the study, with no independent human adjudication. A matched reference can also be uncertain or flawed. These judgments use supplied modern text on both sides and cannot validate independent sentence translation. They do not alter the exact physical scores or admission criteria.
