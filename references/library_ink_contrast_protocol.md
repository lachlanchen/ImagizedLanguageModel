# How much does the current source change the next glyph?

The broad writer's first literary outputs include repeated family words and misplaced actions. Use its frozen four-pass weights. For each direction and occupied source length, choose four sentences from four different training works, nearest in length with a fixed source-independent seeded ordering. They establish what the writer expects to say at the same answer history when it sees other pages. The real pictured source and every comparison share the same generated history. Include stop as an ordinary decision.

Let p be the conditional distribution under the true source, q the arithmetic mixture under the four backgrounds, and h the log ratio log(p/q) clipped to ±log 4. Decode greedily from the normalized weights p exp(a h). This finite Gibbs distribution uses a bounded change in log preference. A proposal gaining relative to background receives more mass. The change in a pair's log odds is exactly a times the difference of their h values. Calibrate one global gain from 0, 0.25, 0.5, 1 and 2 by minimum cross entropy over the complete dev splits (both directions, including stops). Evaluate the limiting case at zero against the original size-matched writes. If zero is selected, evaluate the prespecified gain 0.5 as a diagnostic and keep the calibrated result at zero.

Freeze the natural assessment as both translations of pairs 13–24 in the stored order from each of the four held-out works. These 96 directions are distinct from the breadth meaning panel. The broad writer already emitted its unassisted answers. Apply the fixed comparison to all 960 free book translations and 118 role families. On the second panel run both masked candidate-order assessments, with and without an identified reference, including the text teacher and an unrelated foil. Require a positive lower paired work-bootstrap bound on meaning gain under both readings, at least 110 complete role families and independently reproduced selected pixels to claim a useful decoder. Report entropy, calibration set size, derivative of dev risk at zero, raw generation time, and failure modes. The Gibbs normalization is a mathematical correspondence with equilibrium weights. The device implements numerical inference. It makes no claim about a biological substrate or the electrical energy required.

In vision, divisive normalization accounts for some observed changes in neuronal responses when stimuli compete. The language test uses this as inspiration for a background comparison. The directly relevant algorithmic precedent is context-aware decoding. Here the background has the same answer history and approximately the same source length, with a supervised gain and an explicit cap on one glyph's odds change.
