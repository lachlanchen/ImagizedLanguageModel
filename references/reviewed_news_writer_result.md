# Can a trained visual writer translate an unfamiliar printed shape?

Two independent English-writing models each received 1,368 professionally reviewed Chinese–English translations and matched rehearsal on one RTX 4090 D. We evaluated 192 external WMT 2017 news requests from 66 documents. Both fitted writers received **0/96** complete-meaning credits on sources containing an unfamiliar printed shape and **2/96** on sources containing only familiar shapes. The two credited answers were already credited before training. Showing the trained writer the actual unfamiliar strokes changed 93 of 96 answer bitmaps. Neither input coverage nor sensitivity to a change in pixels establishes transfer of meaning. ## How the comparison was made

The reader takes a 16×16 field of measured ink densities for each 32×32 source cell. Previously unknown bitmaps get temporary request-local addresses and pass through the same neural encoder. A frozen bilingual phrase dictionary supplies hints from the contiguous source runs it can read. Holding every input position and hint fixed, the hidden treatment supplies the mean Chinese-inventory observation at every unrecognised shape. The output model emits a sequence of physical shapes from its existing English inventory and makes its own stop decision. Sharp glyph outlines come from this finite motor. The marker has a separate 227,843 frozen parameters. Deployed dependencies include the phrase tables and optical features.

From the professional 2021, 2022, 2023 and TED evaluation sources we retained one error-free translation per selected source, with all available raters agreeing. Of the 1,368 training translations, 232 had at least two raters. Their 351 documents are disjoint from the internal held documents. Only 28 held sentences contained shapes outside the original Chinese inventory, below the initial requirement of 30. We preserved that failed selection, kept all training rows unchanged and reserved a different entire year for external evaluation. Of 2,001 WMT 2017 sentences, 479 passed the textual, length and inventory screens. We reserved 96 from each input group before fitting. One evaluation source and a different English reference had normalized surface matches somewhere in the earlier million-row OPUS training corpus. No aligned evaluation pair matched. These checks cover the identified corpora. The length and English-output screens select relatively short sentences. [Initial split](evidence/open_news_training_v1/selection.json) · [External split](evidence/open_news_external_v1/selection.json) · [Overlap trace](evidence/open_news_external_v1/earlier-overlap.json) · [Original WMT task](https://www.statmt.org/wmt17/translation-task.html)

Each arm updated 1,817,441 parameters for eight matched passes, 688 updates and 1,173,391 supervised target positions, including rehearsal. The physical and hidden fits took 11.1 and 14.3 seconds inside their respective update loops, with peak allocated tensor memory of 0.423 and 0.421 GiB rounded upwards. This costing excludes preparation, parent training, calibration, evaluation, runtime reservations and external teachers. These results cannot support a matched-quality 1,000-fold efficiency claim. ## What the writer learned

On unfamiliar sources, a repeated English word trigram occurred in 58 parent answers, 39 hidden-fit answers, 38 physical-fit answers, 42 erased-input replays of the physical fit and one supplied reference. The training corpus contained 288 occurrences of 147 source shapes outside the old inventory. Every unfamiliar shape had appeared in training for 30 held sentences; none had appeared for 51; 15 mixed the two. Only ten sentences contained exclusively new-to-the-original-inventory shapes seen at least five times during fitting. All three exposure groups received zero complete-meaning credits. [Original output audit](evidence/open_news_external_v1/writing-audit.json)

Given the correct preceding reference cells on unfamiliar-source requests, the physical fit chose 4,330/7,462 next cells correctly, versus 3,457 after exchanging the source and deriving the corresponding phrase hints. The true source raised the complete-reference mean log probability on 92/96 requests. Yet the writer assigned higher mean stopped log probability to its own answer than to the reference on every one of its 94 stopped paths. The earlier parent chose 4,384/7,462 reference cells with the true source. This is evidence for source conditioning and a measured failure to turn it into reliable autonomous writing. Multiple valid translations and a single reference spelling limit the interpretation of the score margins. [Supplied histories, exchange scores and emitted paths](evidence/open_news_external_v1/conditioning.json)

For two possible complete readings with outcome counts (a,b) and (c,d) in two visibly distinguishable classes, the best finite-sample accuracy gain attributable to keeping the classes separate is exactly min(a−b,d−c) when their majorities favour opposite readings. It vanishes when the same reading is a majority in both. For any finite list of shape classes, merging them cannot improve the best attainable count. The Lean proof bounds the available information under fixed nonvisual context. A trained network can fall short of the optimum. [Checked Lean 4 derivation](../formal/span_list/UnfamiliarInkEvidence.lean)

The next model comparison should test the target-side language prior directly. Pretrain a compact independent visual decoder on uninterrupted English prose from document-disjoint books, with a matched control receiving the same glyphs and boundary lengths in shuffled word order. Keep bilingual adaptation, generation and the source evidence fixed. Test whether intact word order improves freely generated complete meanings under source changes, and check retention of English syntax and controlled roles. Repetition and conditional rankings are secondary measures.
