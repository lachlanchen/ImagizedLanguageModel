# Which parts of a conversation can a glyph writer learn?

A 2,447,719-parameter reader and writer learned to select, order and spell word images from 73,714 real human conversation turns on 4,476 CoQA stories. It receives a pictured passage, question and preceding human exchange. The reply consists of actual printed glyphs. A second model with 2,510,170 parameters explicitly predicts a continuous image representation of the next word. Each trained for 8,000 updates on the same batches of 32 turns. Both inherited optical weights and a passage reader previously fitted on SQuAD. Entries count stopped answers matching the normalized first human answer. On development, the direct writer produced 1,221 verbatim answer images and stopped on 5,557 turns. Across four original human references per turn it matched at least one on 2,221 turns and obtained 46.7% official F1. The word-code writer matched 2,200 and obtained 46.2%. These are subset scores from complete printable English stories of up to 320 words. Swapping the question also swaps its supplied dialogue history. In the registered secondary year substitution, the direct writer returned both years correctly on 26/47 reserved and 31/50 development pairs. The word-code writer did so on 26/47 and 30/50. [Four-reference scores](evidence/dialogue_ink_v1/additional_references.json) · [Date substitutions](evidence/dialogue_ink_v1/plain/dates.json)

The primary composition test required previously unseen multiword answer wording absent from the pictured inputs. The direct writer obtained 14/586 and 18/697 normalized matches, with zero physically exact answers. The word-code writer obtained 16/586 and 19/697 normalized matches, with zero and one physical match. The single exact new phrase was *he was only a cat*. The writer assembled it by pointing to five words. Under the stronger normalized-phrase test it was the only success across both splits (0/478 and 1/570). On 83 development turns all four references required absent multiword phrasing. Neither writer exactly matched any of the four. Word F1 was 23.4% and 25.3%. The measured language is largely extractive and short. The prescribed composition test fails. [Held decisions](evidence/dialogue_ink_v1/word_code/audit.json) · [Conditional results](evidence/dialogue_ink_v1/word_code/conditional.json)

With the correct preceding words and spelling route supplied, the second writer generated only 7/573 and 5/599 required words. Supplying the actual target word's visual features raised this to 306/573 and 299/599. The next bottleneck lies in grounding the word choice in the passage and question. Source addressing also fails often. Forcing a correct route or providing the intended word image is diagnostic supervision and cannot enter the released inference path.

The direct dialogue fit took 132.6 s and 15.04 kJ on an RTX 4090 D. The word-code fit took 192.1 s and 20.51 kJ, plus 20.5 s and 2.20 kJ for 960,000 word-image presentations. Accounting for inherited training is necessary for an end-to-end compute comparison. Single-face ASCII experiments do not establish transfer to handwritten or historical forms.
