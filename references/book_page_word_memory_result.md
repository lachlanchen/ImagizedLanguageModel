# Can an optical decoder use the earlier page?

On 3,949 eligible book passages, the frozen optical model wrote the exact final word **109 times** with the whole passage available, **56 times** with 96 cells and **zero times** after exchanging the earlier ink. A frequency-based retrieval rule recovered **662** on the complete pages. The model gained access to relevant evidence but often selected the wrong word from it. All 109 exact answers had occurred on the original page. [Original output images](../publication/ilm-nature/evidence/book_page_words.zip) · [Editable figure](../publication/ilm-nature/figures/Page_word_memory.pptx) · [Standalone image-input code](../ilm/visual_lm/page_word_memory.py)

The visual decoder was fitted on 96 PG-19 books in the earlier word-order comparison. The page-memory experiment changed inference and froze every weight. The score compares the trained likelihood of a complete proposed word followed by a space under the final 32 pictured cells and under a single neutral space, per scored cell. Proposals include all distinct visible alphabetic runs of 1–23 cells and the model's first complete free word when representable. Shape boundaries come from the existing English print repertoire. The response ends after one selected word by design. Multiplication of local passage frequency by inverse book document frequency provided the stronger comparator. The neural writer's deficit against that comparator was 14.0 percentage points (paired 95% interval 12.7–15.3). The evaluation used a separate LAMBADA validation edition. From its 4,869 records we excluded nine passages overlapping the earlier test under normalization and 911 with an unsupported glyph in the final local query. The complete and clipped page contained an exact target candidate for 3,239 and 562 eligible passages, respectively. The full-page writer gained 92 formerly incorrect answers and lost 39 formerly correct answers. It gave zero correct new words among the 710 cases with no target candidate on the page. The corpus-frequency rule gave 152 exact words from 96 cells. Independent recount checked all 19,745 archived model and control outputs. Twelve single-image CPU replays reproduced the original answers. Excluding alphabetic fragments cut by the crop edges changed 317 clipped predictions and 355 exchanged-page predictions. The resulting counts were 57 and zero, with all 109 full-page successes unchanged. The full view gained 92 and lost 40 against this clipped control. [Boundary audit](evidence/book_page_memory_v1/crop.json)

A subsequent learning comparison should use document-disjoint natural book endings to fit the probability of selecting each earlier pictured word, starting from the measured corpus-frequency baseline. Compare with a ranker using the same candidates and shuffled distant context. Require a lower paired confidence bound above the salience baseline on fresh free words, report words absent from the page, preserve generation of novel answers and retest bilingual meaning before making a broader language claim.
