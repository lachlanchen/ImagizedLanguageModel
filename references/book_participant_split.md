# A work-disjoint test of participant questions in novels

When names were exchanged consistently in pictured questions, passages and the two candidate clauses, the aligned writer still preferred the supported clause in all four conditions on only one of 24 new-story comparisons. The next test asks a simpler question on genuine fiction. Given two different human questions and two distinct participant expressions from the same passage, can a visual reader assign both expressions correctly?

The prepared split has 394 training passages from four works and 226 reserved passages from the Harry Potter and Three-Body collections. Each passage has two human questions in Chinese and English, yielding 788 training and 452 reserved questions per language. Selection requires a Chinese interrogative for a person, an English question beginning with “who” or “whom”, and a short annotated answer present on the page. The two candidate answers must be distinct, and neither may occur inside the other. These rules can admit titles and descriptions as well as personal names. The source questions and excerpts stay in the local data directory. [Frozen source counts and hashes](evidence/book_participants_20261124/sources.json)

The split withholds both entire works from a fresh reader; earlier writers in this repository have seen material from those works. It deliberately uses official training and validation pages of the two held works only for reserved evaluation. The other four works contribute only official training pages. The distribution is uneven, with 355 of 394 training pages from one work. Exact duplicates are removed across the two sets.

Train the reader from fresh weights. Compare it with a matched reader in which the question-to-person link is cancelled on the same pages. Present both candidates as source images in both orders. Require both questions to be correct under either presentation order and report results by held work and request language. Candidate images are supplied in this test. Free answer generation and production of novel glyph shapes require further measurements. The split is ready for training; it has no model score yet.

Prepare the images from the installed human-annotated BiPaR corpus with `PYTHONPATH=. python -m scripts.prepare_book_participant_questions`. This writes passage and annotation manifests to `artifacts/book_participants_v1/`. Pixel rendering belongs to the reader's input pipeline.
