# Answering an English question about one of two Chinese clauses

The latest source reader can write different readings for a single glyph, but its question classifier has just two meanings. This experiment changes the information the question must carry. A two-line page contains two authentic classical clauses with different actions and different objects. A pictured English request names one action. The independent model must identify its line and write the English reading of that action's object. Supplying only a generic verb/object role cannot resolve the question.

Train a dual image encoder on the original training works using visual question images and the contextual states of their Chinese predicates. Group positives by the registered action gloss and use other actions in the batch as negatives. Keep the released clause reader, person adapter and English speller fixed. At inference, derive the verb and object positions separately on each printed line, select the line from the question, then use the frozen speller at its predicted object. Compare a matched fit that exchanges the pictured action labels across examples and a nonparametric training-count baseline that matches the English action to the predicted source glyph. Give that last control the English action identity. Also report line selection at supplied predicate positions and writing at supplied object positions. Reject pairs in which an action gloss equals either object gloss. This exact-string rule permits inflectional and substring overlap.

Reserve evaluation before fitting. Select eligible held clauses with single English verb glosses that occur at least three times in training. Pair different verbs and objects deterministically, at most once per clause, within each held work. Require both answers under both line orders and two new English question frames. Use a separate evaluation of pairs sharing the identical printed Chinese predicate with different action readings. Report these whether or not they succeed. Report loss of exact interpretations in the scoring lists. Texts are genuine clauses juxtaposed for this test; whole-page continuous book reading remains a separate experiment. The new relation component is limited to 300 seconds and 6 GiB per arm on one card. Preserve the full result when the controls do as well as the learned component.

Add a pixel-only baseline that writes the predicted verb for each line and searches for that contiguous ink strip in the question. It requires neither a transcript nor a list of action names. The prespecified combined reader takes a unique physical match and otherwise uses the learned similarity, with first-row tie breaks tested by reversing the page. This baseline capitalizes on the action being explicitly named in the evaluation, and may match a substring in the frame itself. Compare it separately with the neural-only reader. At training, sample extra contrasts from alternative glosses of the same printed head where available. Check ordered-line equivariance for all unequal scores and count every exact tie.

After the literal-name scores were recorded, reserve a wording stress test on the frozen model and existing held pairs. Replace both names by the listed editorial alternatives in both held question frames. Require that both alternatives differ, that neither repeats either literal action as a whole word, and that neither mentions a registered object as a whole word. Keep the literal-name results on exactly the same subset and count writing and row selection separately. These phrases approximate the English action in isolation. This is an additional diagnostic chosen after literal testing. Its result has no effect on the registered literal-name scores or the checkpoint.
