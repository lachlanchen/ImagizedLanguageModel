# Translating from pictured language and writing the pixels of the answer

The released compact writer reads a segmented binary image and returns another image. On 192 held literary requests its learned pixel motor reproduced every complete PNG and stop decision of the preceding repertoire writer. The two systems therefore share the same two source-only meaning credits. A second model, which reads pixels and uses a pretrained piece decoder, preserved 22 meanings. The quantized 8B text comparator preserved 160. A local bilingual model graded the answers in two presentation orders. ## Where the ink comes from

The previous writer repeatedly selected a stored 32 × 32 bitmap or stop. The new motor takes the visual code for that selection, predicts 1,024 logits and thresholds them at zero. The language network reads the pooled pixels of its own emission at the next step. Inference needs neither an output bitmap table nor a font. The finite choice repertoire still restricts the forms it can generate. The motor learned all 3,717 trained shapes with zero incorrect pixels. Its 2,266,112 parameters bring the model to 3,954,592, plus 604,480 stored visual-code scalars. The 4,000 motor updates took 4.17 seconds after loading the existing model. These numbers exclude antecedent training. [Motor fit](../models/neural_glyph_motor/fit.json) · [Registered test](neural_glyph_motor_protocol.md)

A Lean induction shows that equality of the two motors' pictures on the visited codes suffices for equality of full output and stopping under the same deterministic transition. The check on independently executed PNG inputs covers floating-point differences outside the proof. The preceding GPU repertoire writer and the new CPU pixel writer agree on all 192 outputs. An older, separate autoregressive pixel writer used 7.58 billion total parameters and gave seven exact proportional-layout replies out of 32. Figure 1 distinguishes its results from the compact systems and includes a damaged name. ## Measuring understanding through a frozen decoder

Each new optical reader saw the same 28,342 bilingual passages in the same order for four passes. Matched regression and prediction training each took 2,364 updates per direction. Both fitted contextual vectors and log attention mass. The prediction arm also matched the pretrained translation distribution at each genuine target prefix, with 32 explicit events and all other pieces contributing to a single residual event. Full-vocabulary development divergence fell from 0.671 to 0.435 nats into English and from 0.933 to 0.612 into Chinese. All four fits used less than 2.1 GiB of allocated memory. The two prediction fits took 71 and 66 seconds, excluding target preparation, inherited training, generation and judging. The grouped loss and its full-normalizer gradients were independently checked with vocabularies up to 65,001 entries. The source-only difference between prediction and continued regression was +12/192. The work-level contrasts were +3, +8, −1 and +2. An exhaustive four-work paired bootstrap gave a 95% interval of **0 to 13.54 percentage points**, failing the strictly positive lower-bound criterion. The initial floating-point calculation rounded its zero lower endpoint to about 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁸. Recounting the same sealed votes with rational contrasts corrected the decision without changing any outputs or votes. Reference-informed credits were 16 versus seven. In a separate smaller set of answer alternatives, the prediction reader received 33 credits against two for within-book, length-matched source exchange. Assessment varies with the comparison set. The independent writer translated a pictured Chinese source as “Very true, he said.” Its first English answer on the panel repeated “hand” and lost the concealment of the speaker's dislike. The gain in the hybrid system is largest into English. A direct transfer test can now hold the learned motor and output choices fixed and compare independent language decoders supplied with the regression-trained and prediction-trained optical fields. Whole meanings on new book passages and counterfactual source changes remain the primary outcomes. The current evidence provides no matched-quality compute advantage or general-purpose translation result.
