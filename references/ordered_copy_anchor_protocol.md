# Learning to preserve and rewrite several parts of a pictured sentence

The single-span study found an exact-reference ceiling of 60/1,024 with three source cells and unrestricted target length. A sentence can require expansion in several places, deletion elsewhere and copying of unfamiliar names. Train a source-conditioned writer to identify ordered reusable ink and fill the gaps between successive copied regions. Rewriting may insert images between adjacent source cells or before the first and after the last cell.

The preparation uses all 55,872 training pairs from 1,200 chapters. Each possible alignment point is a pair of physically identical source and target cells. An increasing chain of points preserves their order on both sides. For each point we compute the length of the longest chain ending there and beginning there. The point belongs to some maximum chain exactly when the two lengths sum to one more than the maximum length. Every maximum chain contains one point of each forward rank. A rank with a single eligible point fixes that correspondence across all maximum alignments. Multiple feasible points remain uncertain. Some uniquely aligned pictures, including punctuation, can serve as separators without representing the same semantic unit in both sentences.

The 1,186,800 source cells and 1,762,187 target cells yield 787,478 forced correspondences. Of these, 609,351 fall outside the preparation's listed punctuation and space inventory. Across the corpus there are 356,806 intervening regions with different source and target ink, including 144,418 pure insertions and 14,895 pure deletions. In 12,523 rows some copy correspondence is ambiguous. The saved arrays preserve the complete source, target, offsets and ordered physical pairs. Equality uses canonicalized bitmap bytes from the shared raster bank. No held-out sentence was read for this preparation. The correspondence algorithm was checked against enumeration of every optimal alignment on 961 pairs of short binary strings, including repeated marks and empty sequences. A future matched writer comparison must use only the source and its own output at deployment. Measure whether a source-only planner can preserve names, numbers and other visible details while generating every intervening gap, including insertions with an empty source span. Keep the existing composer, literal copy and a matched version with the copying decision untrained. Supply true alignments only in a separately identified diagnostic, quantify gains and losses from replacing them with predicted positions, and score independent complete translations on fresh chapters. Agree on held-out cases and hardware ceilings before the first fit. A high alignment score alone cannot establish translation. The training archive records physical supervision and contains no predictions from such a model.

## Frozen replication cohort

We reserved 512 real passages from 390 chapters, after excluding 5,629 previously encountered chapters and screening against 839,461 earlier rendered strings. Each of four source-length ranges (3–12, 13–32, 33–64 and 65–96 glyphs) contributes 128 passages. No chapter contributes more than four. The complete-meaning review roster contains six passages per length range and 24 distinct chapters. Both the literal copy and unchanged phrase composer have already emitted their complete control images. The source-only planner and gap writer have yet to be fitted or evaluated on this roster.
