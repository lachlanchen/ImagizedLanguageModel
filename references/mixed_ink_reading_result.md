## What the observation can identify

Adding the ink in successive glyph layers destroys order. In particular, the sequences AB and BA give the same sum even with noiseless measurement. If they occur equally often and require two different outputs, the best decision from that sum alone succeeds half the time. More generally, if a representation maps inputs to a common state z, its best exact-answer accuracy on a finite distribution is the sum over states of the largest joint probability P(z,y). Any deterministic downstream network must make the same decision for every input sharing the state. A spatial grid with an explicit reading order or a sequence of visual tiles preserves order. This inverse-problem argument concerns what information survives the measurement. It says nothing by itself about the number of updates needed to learn grammar.

There is a second ambiguity at the output. Under squared pixel error the optimal predicted image is the conditional mean. If two equally plausible characters put strokes in different places, that mean can contain partial strokes from both. A recurrent writer can instead commit to a whole configuration before emitting pixels. Pixel-based autoregressive language generation has already been demonstrated in PIXAR. The question tested here is whether one small model can act differently on the same pictured passage and write the requested answer on a consumer GPU. [Tai et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.874/)

## What was trained

A new 61,972-parameter visual question reader selects either a contiguous source span or a frozen 2,115,971-parameter recurrent writer. The switch operates once per answer. The copy path accepts every passage length represented in the roster. The writer uses its own previously emitted ink and can emit up to eight new cells. The reader scores a start, then scores each legal end using the question and a learned length bias. It receives the entire passage, without an answer-dependent crop. The original generator reads the central gap window. Exact bitmap equality and pixel summaries are allowed input features. A tile has no learned vocabulary entry. This design adapts the copy/generate choice to physical ink. [Pointer-generator networks](https://aclanthology.org/P17-1099/) · [Protocol and architecture](mixed_ink_reading_protocol.md)

Twelve passes over 7,235 real training questions and three requests on 2,048 distinct pictured windows took 42.84 seconds on one RTX 4090 D. Peak PyTorch allocation for the recorded fit and primary assessment was 190.34 MiB. The time excludes prior writer training, rendering, corpus preparation and later evaluation. Full-corpus compute at matched language quality against Qwen remains unmeasured. The earlier 17,686-window run retained 17,497 historical writer streams under default matrix precision, below its 99% threshold. With the original high matrix-multiplication precision, the entire saved output archive, including lengths and stops, reproduced byte for byte. The two runs and their decisions remain available. [Fit and original scores](evidence/mixed_ink_20261118/fit.json) · [Precision replay](evidence/mixed_ink_20261118/replay.json)

The subsequent 17,600 windows came from 2,635 additional source groups. Among the 16,089 windows with four visible cells on either side of the gap, unfamiliar instructions elicited the correct left quote in 16,005 cases and the correct right quote in 16,086. Only 398 windows had both correct quotes and a correct missing expression. The historical-plate executable reproduced every intact and relocated historical stream and every new modern gap stream in its compatibility check. The separate historical pathway selected the requested glyphs in 9,565 of 9,600 intact cases. It reads a supplied plate. ## Why scaling this model alone cannot solve book continuation

Let P be the pictured source and Y the required complete output. The possible outputs lie inside the union of the contiguous intervals of P and all strings of at most eight images. This is a generous upper bound, since it grants perfect selection and an arbitrarily capable short writer. Of 53,711 adjacent segment pairs in the two previously held-out books, 53,585 could be rendered. In **47,944 of those pairs (89.5%)**, the reference was longer than eight glyphs and did not occur as a contiguous source span. In 47,943 of those cases at least one needed glyph was absent from the preceding segment. Optimizing this architecture longer cannot produce those exact book references. Other valid continuations can exist. [Book-specific counts and source digest](evidence/mixed_ink_20261118/book_support.json)

A useful next comparison is a recurrent decision at each output glyph. After each emitted bitmap, the next action can copy a visible tile, draw new ink or stop. The context state retains the question and emitted reading order. When identical source tiles occur in multiple positions, their probabilities can be summed during training. The same physical target can receive probability through both drawing and copying. This construction removes the measured contiguous-span restriction. Learning the correct continuation still requires evidence. Compare it with the frozen system on complete source answers, new expressions and longer mixed-origin strings before claiming useful book generation.
