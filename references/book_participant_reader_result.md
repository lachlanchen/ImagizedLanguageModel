# What transferred to two withheld novels?

A 64,666-parameter visual reader learned to use Chinese questions to distinguish between two people on unfamiliar pages. Across 226 passages from the Harry Potter and Three-Body collections, it assigned both people correctly on 62 passages. A reader trained with the same two names independent of the question scored ten. A literal glyph-matching rule scored 38. The difference from literal matching was 10.6 percentage points, with a paired 95% interval of 4.0 to 17.3 points. Selecting a name without being told the two candidates remained much harder. One *Three-Body* passage asked who led Wang Miao to a corner of the observatory and who devised a system of cosmic rites. The autonomous visual reader returned 墨子 and 孔子 respectively. This was the first complete Chinese success in the reserved order. Figure 112 also shows the unselected first example from each work with the original output pixels. In English, when the reader changed its choice with the question, 27 assignments were correct and 36 reversed. It exceeded both weak controls on the registered complete-page metric but showed no general preference for the correct English assignment. [Original image outputs and native figure](../publication/ilm-nature/figures/Figure_112_book_participant_reader.png)

The question reader scored the start and end of each possible short expression, then summed the probability of all occurrences that produce identical ink. Both fresh fits saw the same 394 pages, including 355 from one work. Both reached 2,000 updates. The aligned fit recovered every training expression, yet its unrestricted English output recovered only seven on the held works. The larger, previously trained clause writer produced no exact name-only replies on the same test. This strict comparison can penalize a valid name embedded in a longer response. A 35,952-parameter detector trained on 24,000 DuIE snippets proposed distinct names without seeing the question. Its 16 proposals contained 222 of the 452 reference answers; both references were available on 59 pages. Its first choice alone was correct 49 times. The resulting gain over reading all substrings did not meet the required comparison against the independent-label reader. The intervention reused the diagnostic pages after the first reader had been scored. [Detector and coverage](evidence/book_participants_20261124/person_reserved.json) · [Intervention design](book_person_proposals_protocol.md)

On one RTX 4090 D, optimizing the aligned question reader took 19.9 s and 2.43 kJ of device energy. Optimizing the detector took 27.0 s and 2.84 kJ. Across 904 individual questions the all-strip reader used 484 J, with 5.6 ms median latency. Qwen3-8B used 23.2 kJ, with 193 ms median latency. Rendering and model initialization were excluded. At these settings the readers differed markedly in accuracy. The two released files reproduce all 132 sampled GPU responses on CPU.
