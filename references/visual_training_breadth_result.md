# What six times as many distinct translation pairs teach a visual writer

A 1.82-million-parameter independent writer learned from 147,456 pictured sentence pairs on one RTX 4090 D. It earned **6/96** complete-meaning credits on four new books. The same model trained for the same number of glyph decisions on 24,576 repeated pairs earned **4/96**. Their starting model earned **5/96**. Both paired intervals across works include zero. The broad fit preserved the controlled role and polarity criteria, but its improvement on unseen prose failed the prespecified adoption rule. ## Pictured sentences and what the models wrote

Both fits read **我理解你的痛苦。** and wrote **I understand your pain.** The starting writer already gave the same answer. Both also translated “But the answer is yes.” as **但答案是肯定的。** The broader fit wrote **然而，每一个都是全新的。** for “Yet each was entirely new.” The repeated fit wrote **然而每一个都有新的东西**, which says that each has something new. The second additional broad credit, **所以我甩了一点点。** for “So I shook it up a bit.”, is a literal physical interpretation. The book's Chinese text refers to changing the approach. That automated credit is questionable. On a request about continuing to meet a difficulty with ingenuity, the broad writer produced a sentence about a contract and control. The paper shows individual physical outputs with the sources that elicited them. [Original image comparison](../publication/ilm-nature/figures/Visual_training_breadth.png)

## Identical training budgets

We started both arms from the same fitted visual draft editor. Its shared phrase tables already used all 497,775 aligned training pairs. We excluded from the intervention the 32,768 natural indices previously used to fit the editor, held controlled source images, malformed script and length combinations, and mismatched written decimal sequences. Of 436,852 eligible bilingual rows, we drew six disjoint, equal-length cohorts. Every natural pair was new to the present editor comparison apart from the deliberately repeated first cohort. This accounting does not establish absence from all antecedent training stages. [Reservation, edition hashes and corpus binding](evidence/visual_training_breadth_v1/selection.json)

A pass consisted of 24,576 natural pairs and the same 6,240 controlled training pairs in both arms. For every slot, both source and target lengths in both directions were identical across all six cohorts. Each arm took **2,088 AdamW updates and 11,300,604 supervised decisions**, including stops. Both fits updated 1,817,441 numerical parameters with the same learning rates. The repeated fit read 10,251,570 draft cells and the broad fit 10,253,890. Both read source pixels through shared trained optical features, generated from their own previous glyphs and could copy from a source-derived draft. Their output surface uses a finite compiled print repertoire. [Protocol](visual_training_breadth_protocol.md) · [Broad weights and fit](../models/visual_training_breadth/broad/fit.json) · [Repeated weights and fit](../models/visual_training_breadth/narrow/fit.json)

Training and per-pass development evaluation took 57.96 s for the broad fit and 57.88 s for the repeated fit. Peak allocated device memory was 1.26 GiB in each case. Preparing both directions of the training drafts took 185.47 s with six CPU workers. Setup, data preparation, preceding fits and grading are outside the fit times. The neural weights occupy 7.27 MB per fit, which are part of an editor requiring about 25.37 MB of numerical assets. The aim of training at 1,000-fold less compute than a comparable language model remains untested at matched quality. [Draft preparation and hashes](evidence/visual_training_breadth_v1/drafts.json)

The first pass used identical pairs in identical order. GPU updates were numerically close and gave slightly different development outputs. Development free glyph F2 started at 0.3943 into English and 0.1526 into Chinese. The final broad scores were 0.3931 and 0.1706. The final repeated scores were 0.3945 and 0.1683. Repeated-data training NLL fell from 1.253 to 1.137, versus 1.219 at the end of broader training. That lower rehearsal loss gave no corresponding book advantage. Two clear meaning errors occurred in a seeded inspection of 32 training pairs. ## The decoder responds to the ink

Holding the correct reference history and the original phrase draft fixed, restoring the correct source in place of a different book's passage raised average reference log probability on 94/96 broad-arm requests. It repaired 696 wrong next-glyph decisions and spoiled 114 among 3,246 tested glyphs and stops. Restoring the draft alone while holding the correct source fixed repaired 71 and spoiled 19. Intact broad top-1 prediction was correct at 2,064 positions, versus 2,036 for the repeated fit and 2,035 for the starting writer. Yet the broad model ranked its free output above the reference on **92/96** requests, favoured the reference once, and tied three times within 0.000001 nat per glyph. The source changes the distribution. Most of those changes do not recover a complete new translation.

For each input exchange, take the log-score margin between reference and actual free answer, normalized by each answer's length. Restoring the source and draft in either order decomposes the change in margin. Mean source and draft contributions to that change were −0.327 and −0.389 nats per glyph. The Lean proof gives the unique efficient allocation that divides the interaction equally, and the exact condition for reversing the preference. This calculation concerns two candidate strings. Valid alternative translations can lower reference agreement. ## How much can the same pixels support with inherited language weights?

We tested the existing pretrained optical reader on the same 96 raw source PNGs, with original OPUS-MT source and target weights and a decoder that writes visual spans. A separate candidate comparison credited **18/96** pictured path outputs, **41/96** original greedy OPUS text outputs, **5/96** independent broad outputs, **81/96** saved Qwen translations, **56/96** supplied translations and zero foils. The change in the independent score between the two grading sets is observable grader variability. The optical reader stopped on 90 requests and the native model on all 96. All eight sampled optical outputs reproduced their pixels, stops and span boundaries on CPU. For example, the pictured source 所以我稍微改变了一下做法。 elicited “So I changed my approach a little bit.” from the pretrained reader. These inherited weights support a stronger visual translation route and add considerable antecedent language training. [Pretrained visual PNGs](../publication/ilm-nature/evidence/breadth_pretrained_images.zip) · [Paired scores](evidence/visual_training_breadth_v1/transfer.json) · [OPUS-MT](https://aclanthology.org/2020.eamt-1.61/)

A shortest-path calculation using the actual initial and continuation span tables found 81/96 native text outputs exactly expressible in at most 128 visual cells and actions. Of 26 requests credited for native text and rejected for the pictured writer, **18** admitted the native wording. The procedure recovered a legal path for every naturally stopped pictured answer. This establishes reachability of the canonical native spelling. It does not show that a greedy model selects that path or that the native translation is the only valid one. ## An exact address can still produce a worse sentence

The pretrained writer normally averages native embeddings associated with a visible output span. We checked its actual stopped paths. Of 1,064 emitted spans, 195 mapped to multiple embedding addresses when initial and later positions were pooled, affecting 81 of 90 stopped answers. Within the observed phase, every one of the 1,064 had a single address in the fitted tables. For any observation space, exact readback is possible on a support precisely when the intended address is constant among cases with identical observations. Including phase resolves this measured ambiguity. The proof makes no claim that an entire visual sequence follows the native model's trajectory. [Lean proof](../formal/span_list/PhaseGlyphReadback.lean) · [Span counts](evidence/visual_training_breadth_v1/surface-coverage.json)

We changed the feedback after an emitted span to its phase-specific training mean. Initial aliases preserve physical ink and expose the relevant phase to the readback. The English and Chinese writers added 297 and 580 aliases, respectively, or 608,256 and 1,187,840 bytes of feedback vectors. With the old pooled values restored, the alias-only control exactly reproduced all 96 original output images, stops and span boundaries. With the new values, 31 of 96 images changed. The new writer stopped on 89 requests versus 90 for the parent. It gained one meaning and lost four in a new two-order comparison: **17/96 versus 20/96** (paired four-work difference −3.13 points, interval −9.38 to +2.08). Native text earned 45, the independent broad fit six, the Qwen comparator 83 and the references 52 in that assessment. Eight of eight phase-conditioned GPU outputs, boundaries and stops reproduced on CPU. This exploratory test used the same books that revealed the ambiguity. Its outcome does not qualify the variant for promotion. ## Research decision

The independent writer responds to its source yet often gives a broken sentence higher probability than a faithful one. Sixfold broader exposure alone did little to change this ordering. The inherited language system can express substantially more complete meanings on the same images. Local correction of its feedback address did not close the gap to native text. Retain the original optical and independent writers as controls. The next independent training comparison should transfer a teacher's **change in target preference when the source proposition changes**, using the same two candidate answers under both sources. This cancels a source-independent preference for a common answer. Use semantically verified paired interventions on natural passages, teacher agreement in both presentation orders, a matched ordinary positive-target fit, and held sources where the reader has to write from a blank answer. Measure coverage and the teacher's paired competence first. Passing a discrimination test alone cannot qualify free writing.
