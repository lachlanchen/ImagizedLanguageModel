# Probability of the first visible mark

The trained writer first chooses a route, then chooses a glyph within that route. Identical source and exemplar pixels can receive probability by either route. Before any decoder change, measure whether the correct first mark is accessible in the trained distribution. Use the final teacher-supplemented checkpoint, all 1,024 saved unseen inputs, the original first answer, and both eligible recorded targets. Change no weights and generate no new sentences.

Normalize route, source-position and exemplar probabilities separately. Aggregate copy and exemplar mass by exact physical glyph, including duplicate source positions. Treat stopping as a distinct outcome. Omit the open drawing route from the lower bound; its entire route probability is a valid upper bound on the unassigned mass for any ink. A strictly larger lead over every competitor than this unassigned mass certifies the winner. Count agreements, certified changes, gains and losses against both targets, CPU versus GPU replay disagreements and unmatched pixels. If the certified rule improves first-mark accuracy against both targets, test whole autonomous answers separately. Otherwise prioritize better conditional learning, leaving the deployed decoder intact. One CPU worker, at most eight minutes.
