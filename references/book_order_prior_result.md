# What does a visual language model learn from intact word order?

Reading English prose in its original order improved the prediction of unseen printed glyphs. It gave no improvement on an external Chinese news translation test. The two trained writers received the same words, punctuation sequence, paragraph ends and training schedule. On 125,289 held glyphs, the intact fit required 1.973 bits per glyph compared with 2.166 for scrambled words. Their respective top-choice accuracies were 58.1% and 54.5%. Both models produced repeating phrases when left to write freely. ## What the images show

With the prefix “They rushed across the plateau, ” the intact prior wrote “and the street of the street of ”. The shuffled prior repeated “the”. Four cases from each of 16 held books were fixed by window order. All 256 CPU replays from decoded source pixels reproduced the two book-trained models' output PNGs and stopping behaviour before and after bilingual adaptation. All 64 book continuations reached the 32-glyph cap for each of those four checkpoints. The supplied-history loss is averaged over the full held sample. The intact-order saving over scrambling was 0.193 bits/glyph, with a 95% book-bootstrap interval of 0.185–0.201. A five-gram model using Witten–Bell interpolation on the same intact windows reached 2.070 bits/glyph. A unigram model gave 4.352 bits in both training orders. The visual neural model inherited bilingual training. This descriptive comparison measures prediction on the same suffixes. A compute advantage at equal language capability remains unmeasured. The book-trained writer answered 你知道她的名字吗？ with “Do you know her name?” and 你知道你的名字吗？ with “Do you know your name?” Both are exact original output images from an earlier OPUS training partition. In the first credited family, the assessor accepted “I'm not hurt” for a source expressing inability or future protection from harm. On a new news item the writer retained “three days” while losing the names and events. All three arms received their one news credit on the same sentence and stopped on all 128 news requests. The assessor accepted 107/128 supplied references and zero displaced references. The paired edit-family interval includes zero (0.0 to 10.2 percentage points). ## Where the contextual signal ends

In each of 88 source-edit families we supplied the shared correct prefix up to the first divergent reference glyph. Holding each source's phrase draft fixed in turn, the intact fit shifted the branch odds in the correct direction under both drafts on 74 families, identical to the control. It preferred both actual next glyphs on 34 families. The shuffled fit scored 75 and 36. Lean verifies that a source-independent shift in branch odds cancels from this interaction and gives the exact feasibility condition for strict opposite decisions under a common integer-score bias. Lean also verifies equality of additive unigram costs under permutation and cancellation of word-internal transition costs in an adjacent exchange. [Measured source interactions](evidence/book_order_prior_v1/source_interaction.json) · [Lean formalization](../formal/span_list/BookOrderPrior.lean)

An exploratory external grammar test used all 67 BLiMP paradigms. We summed conditional log probabilities over the visible glyphs and excluded the paragraph stop. Thirteen pairs fell outside the input bounds. The intact-order fit scored 50.98%, compared with 52.18% for shuffled training. The paired paradigm-bootstrap interval for the difference was −3.68 to 1.11 percentage points. For the 9,070 instances whose two alternatives have identical glyph counts, the difference was −11.55 points (interval −19.35 to −4.64). The same improvement in next-glyph compression failed to improve this grammatical comparison. [BLiMP authors' dataset](https://github.com/alexwarstadt/blimp) · [Scores by paradigm](evidence/book_order_prior_v1/grammar.json) · [Every pair's two likelihoods](evidence/book_order_prior_v1/grammar_scores.npz)

We also paired two 32-cell contexts from the same held work ending in identical eight-glyph suffixes. Their next words differed. A five-gram model must score each word identically under the two prefixes. On 256 disjoint window pairs the intact neural fit ordered the context interactions correctly 137 times, compared with 128 for the shuffled fit and 146 before book training. Its greedy completions recovered 22/512 attested next words and zero complete pairs. An attested continuation can have plausible alternatives. ## Data, fitting and reproduction

The 96 training books supplied 48,664 windows. The 16 reserved books supplied 1,536. Each arm took 1,143 book updates on 16,448,169 scheduled positions and 688 matched bilingual updates on 1,173,391 positions. The 1.82-million-parameter core shares the print motor, marker and phrase infrastructure with its parent. The book phase updated 541,120 parameters with zero bilingual context. The bilingual phase updated all core parameters. The 4090 D fit receipts record 0.31 GiB peak allocation during book fitting and 0.42 GiB during adaptation. These are allocator measurements for these phases. Initial training, frozen dependencies, data preparation and grading have separate costs. [Protocol](book_order_prior_protocol.md) · [Book versions and split checksums](evidence/book_order_prior_v1/data.json) · [Individual training receipts](../models/book_order_prior/) · [Data source](https://github.com/google-deepmind/pg19)

An offline Qwen3-8B assessment, given the two proposed next words in both orders, singled out both attested words on 52/256 pairs using the 32-cell prefixes. Providing up to 96 actual preceding cells raised that count to 81/256. The longer prefixes had a median length of 69. The assessor saw no following text. These judgments measure how often a separate model can use the available clue. [Blind assessments](evidence/book_order_prior_v1/context_clues.json)

On a separate test of book discourse, 5,004 of 5,153 LAMBADA passages qualified after typography normalization. The three priors each recovered zero exact final words from either 32 or 96 preceding cells. The intact writer began 1,712 of the 96-cell continuations with “the”. The actual final word already occurred as a whole word, ignoring case, in 3,980 full prefixes, 913 of the 96-cell suffixes and 25 of the 32-cell suffixes. The original median prefix contained 306 cells. This is a test with clipped context. Even a perfect selector could copy only a fraction of the answers from those clipped images. The immediate design question is whether a trained visual writer can select relevant earlier ink when it receives the complete passage, while retaining ordinary generation for words absent from the page.
