# Writing across separated image spans

The preceding writer returned only one of two requested historical forms even when it answered both individual questions correctly. We test a recurrent decoder on frozen, continuous visual representations of the entire pictured question and passage. At every step it may copy a source interval of up to 64 cells, emit a learned training bitmap or stop. A previous copy feeds its two contextual endpoints back into the decoder. Written glyphs have learned feedback vectors. The model receives the original question once and predicts all decisions at inference. No task type, stage label, reference split or target length is supplied.

Training uses 6,144 questions about 768 genuine two-entry Hanziyuan plates, 3,072 ordered pairs of pictured human CMRC questions and 2,565 pictured DuReader answers. Historical targets retain the original ink. Page and stage order vary. For supervision, longest matching source intervals are chosen greedily with first occurrence breaking ties. Remaining glyphs enter a training-only writing memory. Every aligned sequence must reconstruct its exact target. The encoder was trained previously; its cost and provenance belong to the whole system. The additional writer uses 12 complete passes, seed 89641 and batches of at most 48. The single GPU fit has ceilings of 20 minutes and 12 GiB allocated. Only the final state is evaluated.

Evaluate the previous deployed responder, the inherited single-span scores and the new writer on identical inputs. Count complete stopped images and both requested stage orders on the same card. Test 24 family-disjoint historical cards with two source orders and changed wording, 128 passage-question pairs in both orders, all 571 open DuReader cases, 64 existing role questions, and the previously reported 言/征 card in both orders. Compound CMRC examples are selected within the 512-cell context budget. Inspect prior and actual two-form outputs without replacing failed answers.

A historical gain requires more complete two-form answers than both controls on original and changed wording, including correct reversals on at least half the held-out card/name/layout combinations in each wording. A natural compound-reading gain requires more both-order successes than both controls. Broad replacement additionally requires preserving the prior single-question result and improving open writing on source-absent content. A gain limited to historical layouts retains an experimental writer and leaves general writing open. Failure on ordered pairs calls for checking the first selection and the first decision following a correct copy.
