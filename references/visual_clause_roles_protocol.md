# Visual selection of a verb and its object in either order

The released reader located both words across four pictured classroom questions on none of 14 object-first passages in the two most recently tested books. In the 19,067-clause gloss training set only 212 objects preceded their verb. Requiring an English word gloss had also removed many pronoun objects.

Use visual source-location supervision from all eligible root-verb and direct-object annotations in the same six training works. Fit a joint distribution on two distinct source cells and a question classifier on the 40 original training prompt pairs. The reader outputs the endpoint requested by the imaged question. At inference neither the clause order nor a role identifier is supplied. Keep the page encoder and English writer, including the previous person residual, fixed. Compare uniform training, an equal-size schedule with one quarter fronted clauses, and the balanced schedule with the two endpoints exchanged randomly across presentations within each physical verb/object glyph group. The original released reader and a context-free, training-only glyph-frequency baseline provide further controls. Balance exposure across arms by update count. Fit twelve passes, with a 6 GiB and 300 second ceiling per arm on one card.

The primary test pairs passages with the very same verb and object in opposite orders. Nineteen editorial pairs from four independent primary texts appear in the frozen TSV. Check verbatim source membership, retain prior contextual gloss lists and exclude a pair from the primary endpoint if either passage or either target writing window overlaps model fitting. Report all excluded pairs separately. Use the independent English and Chinese classroom stems in the earlier protocol. Every pair must locate both words in both passages for all four pictured questions. Score both stopped English images separately, then jointly with location. Report actual question classifications, word-order strata, ordinary held-treebank passages, duplicate target glyphs and prior 539/29 retention. Compare the same fixed aligned model against both training controls and the lexical baseline. Matching exposures or a high marked-location result alone cannot establish efficient general language induction. If source selection improves but answer pairs fail, identify the missing source or writing evidence in the actual outputs.

The passages are literal excerpts selected from the local ZhJpBook editions of the Mozi, Zuo zhuan, Guoyu and Huainanzi. The two editor-selected clauses in each pair can be drawn from different chapters. The pair probes a fixed-order shortcut, while a reader that recognizes the individual glyph identities may still pass.
