# Learning a correction and translating an unfamiliar passage

A visual writer learned to repair many sentences that it had previously mistranslated. On a sample of 96 repeatedly contrasted training items it earned **65 complete-meaning credits**, compared with 61 from matched likelihood training and zero before either fit. On 96 other ordinarily rehearsed training items the scores were eight, seven and six. On 96 requests from four unseen books the preference writer and the original writer each earned one credit, for the same figure label. The matched writer earned zero. This is evidence of learning specific corrections from glyph images. Broad transfer to unseen passages remains unresolved. ## What changed during training

We reserved four books before mining. Twelve passage pairs per work were evaluated in both directions. Both arms began with the same independent 1.82-million-parameter optical editor. Its source marker, print palette, draft tables, physical input and output repertoire, and incremental decoder were shared. It can generate a canonical glyph or copy a glyph from its tentative translation. Its inference path uses no external language model. [Frozen comparison](visual_sequence_preference_protocol.md) · [Source hashes and mining sample](evidence/visual_sequence_preference_v1/selection.json)

The original writer generated complete translations for 2,048 training pairs in both directions. Two local 8B models judged the 4,096 outputs and references in opposite orders. Both admitted 400 comparisons under the rule that the reference should express the source and the free answer should contain a material error. A further single-pair pass admitted 352, with 203 English and 149 Chinese targets. This extra screen was added before fitting after an inspection found a missing name. The same family of graders still accepted the reference omitting “Kyle”. A later model reading of twelve retained comparisons per direction flagged two more context-dependent cases among 24. No human adjudication was performed. Among 50 textually identical reference/output pairs, one grouped rater gave five inconsistent judgments, the other zero. These findings limit the interpretation of individual labels. Let s(y) be the sum of teacher-forced glyph log probabilities along a complete target, divided by its number of physical cells including stop. Both writers learned from the same ordinary 39,008 bilingual pairs and the verified positives. The preference arm also minimized one half of softplus(4[0.20 − s(reference) + s(error)])/4, averaged over verified pairs. The matched arm executed the same positive and negative evaluations with the preference loss weight set to zero. This compares a pairwise objective against supervised likelihood while holding model size, ordinary sources, additional positive-target weighting and training schedule fixed. Sequence-level training has earlier applications in neural machine translation. [Implementation](../scripts/train_visual_sequence_preference.py) · [Shen and colleagues, 2016](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1159/)

Each fit took 944 updates over 4,885,382 ordinary target positions, 1,600,973 additional positive positions and 1,702,786 negative positions. The two schedules contain the same 45,312 presentations of the 352 training preferences. In the second pass the treatment scored the positive above the free error on all 22,656 presentations, while the control did so on 21,941. Mean training gaps were 1.420 and 0.810 nats per cell. These are running training-batch measurements on repeated items. On one RTX 4090 D the two fits took 48.3 and 50.8 s respectively, each with 1.49 GiB peak allocation. The board energy counter recorded 6.48 and 6.65 kJ. The initial generation took 7.6 s, grouped labeling 430.9 s and the additional check 58.1 s. Fitting costs exclude those upstream operations and earlier training of all dependencies. ## Why better training margins did not yield better translations

On the fresh books, no supplied reference outscored its writer’s own answer by mean per-cell log probability in any of the three neural conditions. Mean reference-minus-free margins were −1.129 nats per cell for the parent, −1.268 for likelihood and −1.269 for preference. With the reference history supplied, the three writers selected the correct next glyph on 2,280, 2,221 and 2,224 of 3,554 decisions. At the initial position alone the preference writer diverged from the reference in 39 cases. All 288 measured first deviations under supplied history agreed with the raw autonomous output. Alternative valid wordings are possible. [Probabilities on every path](evidence/visual_sequence_preference_v1/paths.json) · [First-divergence checks](evidence/visual_sequence_preference_v1/audit.json)

A whole-target ranking and a sequence of local choices impose different constraints. Lean verifies that correct top choices at every visited reference prefix suffice to reconstruct the reference by incremental decoding. An explicit probability tree shows that the globally highest-probability complete path can nevertheless lose at its first local choice. For unequal lengths, length normalization also assigns a shared-prefix coefficient of 1/L₊ − 1/L₋ to the preference gap. Across the 352 retained training comparisons this term discounted a completely correct prefix in 76 cases, but averaged only 1.53% of the absolute direct path coefficients. This is an algebraic decomposition of the score, not a measurement of neural parameter-gradient size. [Lean proof](../formal/span_list/SequenceMarginGreedy.lean) · [Measured prefix coefficients](evidence/visual_sequence_preference_v1/prefix-weights.json)

A separate diagnostic tested whether the bilingual training corpus contains useful evidence where the parent chooses the wrong cell along a supplied reference history. For each pictured source, we retrieved 32 of 497,775 training passages by visual-strip TF–IDF cosine similarity, using only the pictured source to choose passages. Among 1,274 erroneous decisions, votes after three true preceding target cells supplied the correct next glyph 187 times (14.7%); source-length-matched random passages did so 54 times (4.2%). The correct cell was available in 321 versus 131 cases. The related targets contained 789 of 1,325 distinct content-bearing reference strips, compared with 381 for random targets. Neither set contained an exact complete reference. This exceeds the prespecified five-percentage-point advantage in conditional voting. The next test should condition on the writer's own output and compare related and random local memories on other books.
