# Learning a change of role from a pictured passage

On 128 held-out DuIE credits the 87.1-million-parameter visual writer correctly read both versions of a passage in both question orders on 165 of 256 passage–language pairs. The equal-exposure control did so on five. On twelve independently worded stories both writers scored zero of 24 pairs. The result establishes learning of factual assignments in the credit domain. Transfer to narrative corrections and changes of plan remains poor. [Protocol](role_change_writer_protocol.md) · [PowerPoint figure](../publication/ilm-nature/figures/Role_change_writer.pptx)

On the song-credit example the writer emitted “作词人是易家扬，演唱者是陈奕迅。” After the two names exchanged places in the passage it emitted “作词人是陈奕迅，演唱者是易家扬。” These describe the two supplied accounts. Both questions were unchanged. The two strips are selected from the first held-out credit where all eight requests matched their complete targets. On the first registered letter-writing story, however, the answers assigned both actions to the same person. All 96 story replies stopped. Of these, 37 mentioned only one of the two names, 26 reversed their order, eight included both with repetitions, and 25 put both names in order exactly once. A correct ordering still permits an incorrect action. The two fits used identical ordered inputs, answer lengths and complete answers within each scene, question order and language. The aligned fit associated each visual edit with its supported answer. Both fits saw 2,788 paired training scenes, genuine book requests and historical-glyph replay. Each performed 2,496 updates on 49,340 presentations and 853,600 target positions. The aligned optimization took 100.0 s with 4.47 GiB peak allocated GPU memory on one RTX 4090 D. This is fine-tuning of 28.6 million parameters in a writer with a pretrained backbone. It excludes the parent model, teacher, data preparation and inference costs. The teacher proposed action phrases during dataset preparation. The released writer requires no teacher at inference. The paired credit contrast gained 62.5 percentage points, with a 95% bootstrap interval of 54.3–70.3 across the 128 works. Lean verifies the exact relation between the aligned and independent-label training objectives at fixed predictions. Any predictor insensitive to the edit has equal aggregate risk under the two assignments. A lower aggregate loss alone can coexist with the wrong assignment on one page. The empirical probability check supplied each of the two possible answers in turn and included the stop probability. The aligned writer preferred the supported assignment on 998 of 1,024 credits and 49 of 96 story requests. All answers were writable. In the arm-hidden reading the local text Qwen3-8B gave 39 supported statements out of 48. Semantic credit allowed supported paraphrases and consistent repetitions. One reader assessed the statements with prior knowledge of the study. Recasting only the story page in the training style raised the count of correctly ordered names from 25 to 49 of 96; recasting only the questions gave 23. Recasting both gave 55 and four complete contrasts, all in Chinese. The English versions still yielded zero complete contrasts. The rewrites used the answer annotations and cannot act as a general preprocessing method. The independently supervised model passed no complete contrast in these conditions. The new writer preserved all 192 familiar historical requests and all four two-stage requests on a shared 言/征 card (two orders per character), with zero of 192 rephrased historical requests. Keep the earlier general-purpose research checkpoint available. These trials have not established arbitrary glyph synthesis, open-domain proficiency, or a matched-quality compute advantage over Qwen. The next comparison should locate the binding error on simple statements before adding further narrative training. Hold the page fixed and ask about each action separately. Independently exchange the two action pictures or the two participants and score the writer's preference for each named participant. Preserve both languages and both versions. Correct single-action choices alongside failed ordered replies would motivate a change in multi-clause writing. Failure on a single action would motivate learning the link between the pictured question and the pictured predicate. Reserve new stories before any subsequent fit.
