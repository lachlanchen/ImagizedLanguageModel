## Data and comparison

We retained 4,640 English and 43,087 Chinese training questions with wholly visible, nonoverlapping annotated facts; 21,989 had multiple parts. MultiSpanQA supplies English answer positions. DuReader entity sets give Chinese strings that can have more than one occurrence on the shown page. Their first aligned occurrences provide weak training labels. Excerpts are fixed before consulting the answers. English questions receive up to 1,200 cells of one passage; Chinese questions receive up to 300 cells of each of the first four retrieved documents within 1,200 cells. Some annotated sets exceed the excerpt or the 16-part decoder cap. All reserved questions remain in the primary denominators. Official development pages were screened against training questions, normalized excerpts and sampled 72-character fragments. Public-data pretraining overlap in the Qwen comparison is unknown. [MultiSpanQA](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.90/) · [DuReader](https://aclanthology.org/W18-2605/)

Each fit saw 72,000 question presentations in identical order, six examples per language per update, on one RTX 4090 D. Replaying the sampler showed that all 4,640 English questions and 24,476 of 43,087 Chinese questions were actually presented. The latter included 9,775 distinct multi-part questions across 14,280 multi-part presentations. This limits conclusions about the value of further Chinese training. [Sampling counts](evidence/multiple_ink_runs_v1/sampling.json) The same initial encoder, physical ink, optimizer and learned count reward were used in both fits. The first treatment used weighted categorical classification at each glyph. The second trained separate logits for opening a run and for continuing one already open. A closing run may be followed immediately by another. The path log likelihood is normalized by valid page length during training. A second theorem proves the finite maximization recurrence with an arbitrary terminal count reward. The deployed decoder rounds local log utilities and the count reward to thousandths and maximizes the integer path score. The added count reward changes path ranks; the unweighted normalization theorem concerns the underlying local process. ## What determines the answer?

On the original English questions the revised reader answered nine correctly with the intended questions and ten with unrelated questions of similar length. Seven were correct under both. On fresh English questions three were correct in either condition, including one in common. On the original altered-ink test the reader preserved both complete versions on 2/97 English and 0/48 Chinese cases. The two English questions asked about the same song and the same two names. No complete edit pair succeeded on the fresh 61 English or 35 Chinese cases. These findings constrain how much question understanding can be inferred from the few exact answers.

A separate reference-assisted calculation restricted the decoder to the actual first-reference count on cases where every part occurred literally in the excerpt and the count was within range. Among 116 Chinese cases the ordinary reader wrote a single part every time; the correct count ranged from two to twelve. Giving the true count recovered four complete answers. On 233 English cases the corresponding scores were 12 without and 16 with the true count. Examined on its own, the Chinese count head predicted a singleton on 102/116, a plural on 14/116 and the correct count on only 2/116. Enforcing these predicted counts still recovered zero complete answers. In English its count was exactly correct on 126/233, yielding 12 complete answers. The joint CPU decode reproduced the recorded GPU count on all 349 cases. The Chinese failure thus arises in both count estimation and the identity of the selected facts. [Count-head measurements](evidence/multiple_ink_runs_v1/count-head.json)

The released English sentence writer was run on all 256 English multi-part questions with the selected physical runs joined by the fixed pictured conjunction “ and ”. It stopped on 170, with all annotated first-reference strings present in nine. With the corresponding human facts supplied it stopped on 151, with all parts present in 98. This substring measure accepts repetitive or ungrammatical sentences. The repeated final word in the song example is present in the emitted image. The English writer was trained previously on single-fact statements. No human semantic scoring was performed for this new sentence comparison.

On the matched 32-question benchmark, both optical list readers solved 0/16 in each language. Local quantized Qwen3-VL-8B (Q4_K_M) receiving the identical two PNGs solved 1/16 English and 0/16 Chinese. Giving the *same baseline weights* the page and question strings produced 6/16 English and 1/16 Chinese. The benchmark is small and uses tightly packed glyph grids. It supplies no matched-quality efficiency advantage. [Per-question records and fitted checkpoints' metadata](evidence/multiple_ink_runs_v1)

## Decision

Keep the bilingual reading and historical-form tests open. The reference count rescued only four Chinese cases. A meaningful next comparison must test why the model selects the first salient entity from Chinese pages and misses the remainder. Compare complete reference sets with plausible proper subsets and equally sized wrong sets drawn from the same page. Use matched initialization, training presentations and input fields. Reserve fresh pages, natural singleton questions and witnesses whose correct answer changes when a question or source fact changes. Reward recovery on independent questions and preservation of unaffected facts. Retain the current conditional reader as a fixed control. A longer fit is justified by held-out improvement per unit of compute on this task; the present evidence does not establish an open-ended book reader.
