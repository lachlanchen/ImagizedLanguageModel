# What happens when a reader is trained against incomplete visual answers?

Two readers started from identical weights and saw the same 114,912 training presentations each, covering all 43,087 Chinese and 4,640 English aligned training examples. One added competition against subsets, replacements and extraneous runs. Both read pictured questions and pages, with up to sixteen disjoint output runs. On fresh pages both wrote three complete English plural answers out of 64 and zero Chinese plural answers out of 96. On 64 Chinese singleton questions the control wrote eleven correct answers and the contrast reader fourteen. The complete-answer criterion failed.

With the first human reference held fixed, the control wrote 96 parts, of which 23 matched; contrast wrote 536, of which 62 matched. Each was compared against the same 996 annotated parts. The higher recall came with 474 unmatched predictions versus 73. In 48 Chinese cases with the first annotated list visible and within capacity, supplying its length and retrospectively choosing the best of the page's documents admitted only four complete outputs in either arm. Count prediction and document choice cannot explain the remaining errors. A local Qwen meaning assessor also accepted five deletion controls and four insertion controls in at least one presentation order. It failed its registered calibration and supplies no validated semantic accuracy.

The complete model wrote the two English names Gaby Moreno and Vincent Jones. Its separate statement writer misspelled the final word of the sentence. Its Chinese poem output recovered several verses and also selected unrelated commentary. The released strips preserve every selected cell. On the historical-form selection task neither fit wrote a correct target on any of the 128 scored questions in either language. On the same 32 plural page images the contrast reader and Qwen3-VL-8B Q4 each solved one English question and zero Chinese questions. The image reader used 7.29 J for its warm measured pass versus 17.14 kJ for the local VLM. These tiny, low-accuracy comparisons cannot establish efficient useful language modelling. Training and setup costs are additional.
