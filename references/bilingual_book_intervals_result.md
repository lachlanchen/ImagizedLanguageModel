# What transfers from pictured translations to book answers?

Two questions about different facts on the same page expose three decisions. Can the model recognize the same question across scripts, locate its answer on a page, and write the answer it found? We measured all three on the same regular-print glyph representation.

All three interval readers completed zero of the 23 new passages under the four-request criterion. The matched reader had 31 exact excerpts in 92 requests. Feeding each selected interval to the independent causal writer with the original question yielded 19 exact stopped replies. The writer lost 12 correct selections and repaired zero errors. With the full original page it also gave 19 exact replies. Text-input Qwen3-8B Q8 gave 85/92 and completed 18 passages. Keep the original writer as the released model. The independent question encoder used 848,961 trainable parameters and fresh initialization. Training on the glyph pixels of 11,438 human question pairs took 16.1 seconds of optimization on one RTX 4090 D across eight passes, with 0.57 GiB peak allocated GPU memory. Pairing translations within a passage succeeded on 364/374 BiPaR passages (97.3%, per-passage Wilson 95% interval 95.1–98.5%). The same fit with English questions exchanged within training pages gave 248/374. A length-only assignment gave 257/374. On XQuAD the aligned encoder matched 198/227. Across the five other works it matched 20/23, with 18/23 for length alone. In the much harder search against all 748 Chinese BiPaR questions, exact translation retrieval reached 89 top-one and 288 top-five. These are clustered literary data; passages from a work may be correlated. All scores use regular print. The finite-mass proof explains how repeated intervals can make a spelling most probable even when another location has the highest single score. A separate proof shows that row and column score offsets cancel in the two-question comparison. Neither theorem implies that optimization finds linguistic representations. The held-out pairing, erasure, source-excerpt and stopped-writer measurements test different consequences. The page reader inherits a Chinese QA transformer originally trained on text. Its reported 35.3 seconds cover one adaptation pass on 22,876 requests and exclude pretraining and corpus preparation. The separate small question encoder learns its own optical and language features solely from paired question images. The next experiment tests whether this representation supplies information about answer endpoints beyond the frozen reader.
