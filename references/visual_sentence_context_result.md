# Recognizing a pictured answer and writing it freely

On 192 unseen English questions the retained visual reader–writer generated 12 stopped answers matching a human reference. Two newly trained writers generated six and five. On 192 Chinese questions the counts were 50, 40 and 38. All three received the same predicted fact. The retained writer sees the question and fact strip. The other two also see the passage. Only the comparison between the two new fits isolates the auxiliary training objective. The retained system remains the deployment baseline. ## Which aspect of language was learned?

Two human questions share each test page. We supplied both full references for a discrimination test and held the visual evidence constant while scoring each assignment. The context writer favored the human assignment on 95/96 English and 63/96 Chinese pairs. Reversing the question glyph order reduced these to 50/96 and 52/96. Duplicating a question produced 96 ties in each language. In English it separately preferred each correct answer on 74/96 pages. Counting shared glyphs with untrained multiset Dice similarity also favored the correct English assignment on 95/96. It and the context writer failed on different pages. Three-glyph overlap gave 93/96. For Chinese the respective untrained counts were 59/96 with 13 ties and 11/96 with 77 ties. The English aggregate is achievable using shared glyphs alone. The order intervention shows that the neural score responds to order, but these two findings together leave semantic understanding unestablished. ## What prevents the earlier writer from giving the right answer?

On a separate previously used development cohort, we kept four live output histories and saved the probability of every visited stop using the frozen baseline. A discarded prefix bounds the probability of any longer reply extending it. If the best stopped reply dominates all such prefixes, it is globally maximal within 256 decisions. The Lean theorem applies under complete coverage of visited and discarded paths and nonincreasing extension scores. The numerical comparison uses a positive margin of 10⁻⁵ and double-precision path accumulation on floating-point neural probabilities. A small exhaustive CPU enumeration checks the implemented bound and all visited scores. All 384 selected scores were separately replayed through the teacher-forced loss (largest discrepancy 5.63 × 10⁻⁶). A GPU repeat after correcting CPU stop-score storage returned identical output hashes, bounds decisions and counts. Floating-point scores varied by up to 3.01 × 10⁻⁷. The wider search recovered one extra English answer (5 to 6/192), and kept Chinese at 43/192. Accepted references occurred among explored completions on only 9 and 47 questions. The bound certified a global mode on 167 English and 192 Chinese questions. Of those modes, 161 and 149 missed every accepted bitmap. Stratification matters. With exactly the annotated fact, 9/13 English modes still missed the reference, whereas all 39/39 Chinese modes matched. One English model output changes who did what, writing “Terre'Blanche beat Terre'Blanche” where the reference says he beat Terre'Blanche. Another writes “died on Sunday” where the reference says “died Sunday.” Both miss exact matching, but the latter retains the fact. An independent development experiment induced 28,765 distinct reusable edits from 81,551 training questions. The required precision and family support retained 567 boundary patterns. They applied to 0/192 English and 94/192 Chinese questions on the earlier cohort and yielded zero new correct answers. The learned rewrite table remains an archived experiment. The next fit should improve the reader and the writer under separately measured conditions. Training on generated grammatical-role errors requires identifying incorrect propositions without penalizing faithful paraphrases. ## A fresh test of the surface cue

We reserved a further 229 natural pages (458 questions) after excluding earlier training and evaluation images and source families. We first sought pairs for which neither shared single glyphs nor shared glyph triples favors the annotated pairing. The remaining eligible English material yielded no such pair. From 85 fresh English pages, each overlap scorer solved 84 pairings, and the retained neural writer solved 83. The writer generated six of 170 accepted answer images independently and 43 given the annotated fact. On Chinese, the overlap condition furnished 48 pages. The retained writer assigned the human references correctly on 20/48, gave 19/96 exact free replies, and reached 94/96 when supplied the fact. Neither question was paired with an alternative human answer during free generation. On another 96 Chinese pages the respective exact free and supplied totals were 36 and 187 of 192. The challenge conditions on the references and the two overlap scores. It measures behavior on the selected population. [Reservation](evidence/visual_lexical_controls_v1/reservation.json) · [Pair-by-pair results](evidence/visual_lexical_controls_v1/test.json) · [Original images](../models/fact_span_ranking/examples/lexical-control-1)

The held examples expose a concrete error in reading a page. Asked which company ASE acquired, the model retrieved and wrote the name of a person. On a second question about the origin of ASE's own name it selected the person specified in the human answer. Both are stopped and legible. Before extending training, distinguish getting the right relation, locating its value, and writing the answer. Give the writer an equivalent paraphrase as a positive example during scoring when human judgment supports it. Measure role-sensitive contrasts on independent English materials, since the QAMR–QA2D paired statements in this reservation retained the surface cue. Rendered text has precedents in PIXEL. Sequence-level feedback on model-generated alternatives has precedents in supervised neural transduction. The present results identify where a pictured reader–writer would need such feedback and still leave its advantages over text models open.
