# What a visual writer can recover from a bilingual draft

An independent editor preserved both polarities in 225 of 236 paired event prompts, compared with 222 for matched training without a draft and 195 before training. It preserved who did what to whom across all four variants in 107 of 118 sets. On translations from four unseen books it conveyed the entire meaning once in 96 requests. Its sole credited answer exactly reproduced the phrase draft. General book writing remains open. ## Matched bilingual training

A single RTX 4090 D trained two writers from the same marked optical model. Each consumed 32,768 real parallel sentence pairs sampled from the optical corpus and 6,240 earlier controlled training pairs. We reserved twelve additional passage pairs per edition from a Sahara cultural history, *The Nature of Space and Time*, *The Grand Design* and *Brief Answers to the Big Questions*. Their source and target images were excluded from the training corpus and previous panels according to the selection manifest. The phrase table had already seen the real training pairs used to generate drafts. Training drafts were produced from source images alone. Six CPU workers generated all 78,016 drafts in 43.7 seconds of decoding. [Protocol](glyph_draft_editor_protocol.md) · [Selection](evidence/glyph_draft_editor_v1/selection.json) · [Draft hashes](evidence/glyph_draft_editor_v1/drafts.json)

The encoder reads overlapping windows of four physical glyphs at stride one and three inferred event fields. At step t, the decoder produces a distribution over target glyphs and stop. A learned gate interpolates it with attention to positions in the draft. Attention mass at repeated instances of the same physical glyph is added. Thus an editor can copy the correct form from several draft positions or generate a glyph absent from the draft. The matched arm sees the same source with the copy channel disabled. Both learn from the reference history during fitting and subsequently consume their own generated glyphs. The architecture adapts the pointer-generator mechanism to a second visual input. The Lean result shows that a deterministic draft cannot expand the attainable panel scores of unrestricted source rules. Its potential benefit lies in computation, finite capacity and learning. A draft-only reader must fail at least one of two inputs that collapse to one draft and require incompatible answers. [Model](../ilm/visual_lm/glyph_draft_editor.py) · [Checked theorem](../formal/span_list/DraftInformation.lean) · [See, Liu and Manning, 2017](https://aclanthology.org/P17-1099/)

Each arm processed 9,770,764 target positions including stops in 1,888 updates. The schedules match in every epoch. The editor took 57.9 s of additional fitting, peaked at 1.11 GiB allocated, and reached mean final training loss 1.246. The matched arm took 41.8 s, used 0.89 GiB and reached 1.316. The editor has 1,817,441 parameters, plus a separately trained 227,843-parameter source marker and fixed optical tables. Its model weights, marker, palette, inventories, strip and lexical tables, bilingual phrase tables and calibration total 25,366,083 bytes on disk, excluding runtime libraries, integrity metadata and optional text-rendering fonts. These are incremental training costs over previously estimated weights and tables. All five visual conditions stopped on all 568 requests. Every generated PNG was hashed, all 96 held phrase drafts replayed on CPU, and both neural arms reproduced all 24 sampled GPU answers and stops through CPU image inference. The content-bearing reference strips recovered by the original writer, phrase draft, matched writer, editor and exchanged editor were respectively 338, 627, 336, 381 and 276 out of 1,507. Exchanging drafts changed 87 of 96 editor images. Repeated triples of any strips occurred in 27 editor answers and one phrase draft. A paired bootstrap over the four works gives a 95% difference interval of 0 to 3.125 percentage points for the editor over matched training. The original writer tied the editor in complete meanings; the registered improvement criterion failed. A bilingual model graded complete meaning in two candidate orders with duplicates merged. Its rejection of 24 supplied references and its relationship to the Qwen comparator limit interpretation of the scores. ## Why recognizing a translation is easier than writing it

With the same unrelated draft held fixed for two sources, the editor assigned the larger joint reference probability to the correct translation pairing on all 48 adjacent book pairs and all 236 controlled agent–patient swaps. When both candidates received one identical source, the distinct drafts alone identified the correct pairing in 47/48 book pairs and 168/236 controlled role pairs. This test supplies both complete translations as alternatives. It measures conditional discrimination between two candidates on these samples. [Protocol](glyph_draft_source_probe.md) · [All log scores](evidence/glyph_draft_editor_v1/source-probe.json)

The comparison changes when the rival answer is the model’s own free output. With the source and correct draft fixed, the editor assigned the reference a greater complete-sequence log probability on 2/96 requests, and greater mean log probability per glyph including stop on only 1/96. The median reference deficit per glyph was 0.87 nats. Only four requests had any credited candidate among all five visual conditions. Reranking those five by total or mean editor score earned one credit. An occasional reference may be less suitable than a paraphrase, or may contain information from adjacent book text. The automated grading and the score comparison nevertheless identify a failure that persists with genuine sources and unrestricted glyph output. [All emitted and reference sequence scores](evidence/glyph_draft_editor_v1/path-comparison.json)

The next comparison should learn to rank a full, source-faithful sentence above the writer’s own materially wrong answers. Mine negatives on training works, verify substantive errors before using them as negatives, and preserve adequate paraphrases. Compare against continued likelihood training on the identical sources and target exposure. Test both the reference-versus-own-output score gap and free complete meanings on different works. Retain controlled role accuracy and target-copy tests. A score improvement without new freely generated meanings would yield evidence about ranking alone.
