# Train the probability of the complete answer against a plausible wrong answer

On the four literary works, 94 of the ordinary writer's 104 wrong stopped answers had higher probability than every accepted answer to their question. This establishes a concrete limitation of search. Even a decoder returning a global maximum of that distribution must miss these 94 exact answer sets. For the ten cases with a higher-scoring reference, the comparison alone cannot tell whether another wrong answer scores higher still. The local text baseline completed all four requests on 21/34 passages. Use the released bilingual writer as the initial model. Mine its freely stopped replies on **training pages only**. Keep every accepted human span for each question. A different spelling, a shorter equivalent answer or an annotation omission can give a false negative. Record the original question, source pixels, accepted alternatives, generated rival, and evidence that the rival answers a different question or contradicts the passage. Exclude uncertain negatives. A text or vision teacher may adjudicate training candidates offline. Training and inference of the new writer still consume glyph images. Report rejection rates and inspect a fixed sample of decisions before fitting.

For prompt $x$, accepted answer $a$ and verified wrong reply $b$, define $s(x,y)=\sum_{t=1}^{|y|+1}\log p(y_t\mid x,y_{<t})$, including the final stop. Add $\log(1+\exp(s(x,b)-s(x,a)))$ to the ordinary physical answer loss. This loss directly penalizes an observed complete wrong reply that the model prefers to an accepted reply. Its derivative with respect to the score difference approaches one for strong reversed preferences. Shared prefix factors cancel analytically. Record reply lengths and the length-conditioned result because absolute sequence probabilities are sensitive to the number of actions. Keep the existing replay and free decoder. Compare with positive-only replay on the same prompts, same initial state, same gold answers and a matched forward/backward budget.

Treat the 34 literary passages examined here as development from this point onward. Reserve new works, two distinct questions per passage and their alternative answer strings before inspecting new model outputs. Require higher complete four-request passage accuracy than both the ordinary release and matched replay there, and preserve the prior historical and regular-print minimums. Report first errors, stopping, the source-absent test and runtime independently. If training fixes the observed score gaps without improving freely answered pages, measure coverage of new unseen high-scoring wrong answers instead of promoting the fit. This objective has no trained result yet.

For each old stopped reply scoring above every accepted answer, let $g$ be its log-score advantage over the best accepted answer. Let $m$ be the maximum, across accepted alternatives, of the combined remaining lengths of the two replies after their shared prefix, counting both stops. Reversing any one of these pairwise decisions at fixed inputs requires at least one action log probability to change by $g/m$ or more. Changes to shared-prefix factors cancel. Lean verifies the inequality in common integer score units; the empirical calculation uses the observed floating-point log scores. Over the 94 strict losses, the median whole-reply gap was 2.924 nats and the median lower bound on the largest necessary action change was 0.375 nats. These bounds describe score changes along these prefixes, not model-parameter distances.
