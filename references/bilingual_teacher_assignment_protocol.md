# Can a small bilingual teacher tell which complete translation belongs to which source?

A role location can identify a verb while leaving the choice of verb open. For a prospective sentence-level training signal, test whether an independently pretrained translation model distinguishes two complete propositions in both directions. The OPUS-MT English–Chinese and Chinese–English models are used for offline scoring. Their parameters and piece inventories have no role in the deployed optical writer.

Given aligned pairs (x,y) and (u,v), score all four complete target sequences including their stops. The assignment margin is log P(y|x) + log P(v|u) − log P(y|u) − log P(v|x). Any additive source-only or target-only preference cancels. Compare each teacher's margins with the frozen 1.69-million-parameter optical writer's margins on the identical four requests. Use sums of log probabilities, include stop, and count ties as ties. Absolute margin sizes across different alphabets do not have common calibration. The log likelihood ratio is dimensionless within each model. This complete-assignment measurement has a different interpretation from generating a correct translation without candidates.

Test polarity inversion and subject–object exchange on all 118 existing controlled families. Include active and passive source wordings, both language directions, and retain the two distinct members of a comparison only once for each operation and voice. For natural language, take up to 64 disjoint pairs of bilingual passages per training work in hash order, choosing the most similar unused English source among 128 randomly ordered available candidates by character-bigram Jaccard similarity. Preserve counts, full sentence endpoints and underlying visual handles. Exclude any pair with an identical source or target. The natural reference pairing is inherited from the local interlinear corpus. The four new role-assessment books and all development works are excluded from the natural training candidates.

Cache all four teacher scores and all four optical scores before making a training decision. Record per-family success and natural assignment fractions, measured time and memory, and disagreement between the optical and teacher preferences. For a candidate training signal require at least 90% correct teacher assignments separately for role exchange and polarity in both language directions and at least 90% natural assignments in both directions. Preserve the result if a threshold fails. A qualified score is an offline training signal subject to independent free-output testing. The calibration cannot establish free language quality or the cost of training the original teachers.
