# Teaching a glyph writer where to copy and stop

A Chinese reader supplied probabilities over possible answer spans while a smaller writer learned from pictured questions and passages. Removing the reader after training leaves an image-in, image-out model with 86.6 million parameters. On 196 new questions the distilled writer gave nine more exact answers than ordinary replay. Both models passed 11 of 98 passages when required to answer two different questions about each one. The earlier mixed writer remains the default under the prespecified decision rule. Each question pair used the same pictured passage and disjoint accepted answers. An answer had to stop and match a full accepted reference in pixels. Relative to replay, the distilled model gained four complete new passages and lost four. The paired bootstrap 95% interval for the difference was −6.1 to +6.1 percentage points. Replacing the question with a blank cell yielded one exact individual answer and zero complete pairs. On earlier questions where the parent missed an answer the span reader found, distillation recovered 28/98 and replay recovered 17/98. Where the parent alone had been right, distillation retained 10/21 and replay retained 16/21. No additional DRCD pairs survived screening. All new paired passages came from CMRC development. **What the writer actually returned.** Asked 三个重要的显微镜学的分支分别是什么？ the distilled writer returned 光学，电子，和扫描探针显微镜, an accepted answer. For a question on Hong Kong's exchange-rate index it returned legible financial prose but omitted the nominal effective exchange-rate weighted average specified in the reference. Among 124 wrong replies, 66 started with the wrong glyph. Of the 58 that started correctly, 20 continued beyond an accepted reply and 15 stopped at a proper prefix. **Historical forms and open answers.** The distilled writer gave 192/192 correct replies on historical cards with familiar prompts and 56/192 with rephrased prompts. It copied the oracle and bronze forms on the known 言 card in both orders. On separate, two-paragraph date boards, it passed every request on 20/48 boards with learned paragraph selection, compared with 23 for replay and 28 with supervised alignment. On 571 DuReader questions it reproduced 10/177 contiguous references, 0/94 references requiring distributed ink and 0/300 requiring an absent content glyph. Exact canonical scoring can miss other valid phrasings. These outcomes leave autonomous prose composition and the invention of new historical shapes open. [Task scores](evidence/interval_teacher_20261124/result.json)

**What was learned from the teacher.** At each annotated answer prefix the writer learned a distribution over stopping or copying a specific next position. Conditioning preserves the weight of all compatible occurrences, including repeated glyph sequences. The proofs establish the finite mass identities. On 496 real examples, direct and sequential computations differed by at most 0.00000764 in log probability. On all 18,333 natural training rows, the teacher's highest-weight interval reproduced the annotation on 9,524 rows and the mean probability assigned to the complete annotation was 0.503. A verified training target can still teach an incorrect answer. [Lean derivation](visual_interval_derivation.md) · [Numerical audit](evidence/interval_teacher_20261124/teacher_targets.json)

**Single-GPU costs.** The three writers inherited the same text-pretrained Chinese transformer and the same visual adaptations. Their identical one-epoch fits used 26,381 training examples, 1,326 updates and 346,780 answer positions. Each fit updated 28.6 million parameters and took 90.5–91.9 seconds on one RTX 4090 D, with peak allocated GPU memory below 4 GiB. Preparing teacher logits over 5,808,339 source positions took another 72.3 seconds. These times exclude inherited pretraining and data collection. In warm, sequential, batch-one serving on 196 questions, the distilled writer took 9.11 seconds on prepared glyphs, with another 1.79 seconds to render all inputs. Qwen3-8B Q8 took 51.59 seconds from text and loaded all 37 layers on the same GPU. All three writers returned identical pixels and stops in batch-one and batched evaluation. Qwen gave twice as many exact answers as the distilled writer. Equal-quality efficiency and a 1,000-fold advantage remain unestablished. **Change of typeface.** On bold versions of the same 196 questions, the four writers gave 25, 36, 38 and 39 exact replies respectively when each cell could match either source-style or canonical ink. They passed two, four, six and seven complete passages. With Kai script all four writers gave zero exact replies on 192 questions after two passage pairs were excluded for missing font coverage. The independent release includes the complete distilled writer, a human question and both orders of the known inscription example. All three offline CPU runs matched the original GPU output pixels and stopped. The archive occupies 366,650,456 bytes. [Typeface scores](evidence/interval_teacher_20261124/typefaces.json) · [CPU release check](evidence/interval_teacher_20261124/release.json)

Restoring one typeface at a time isolates part of that failure. On the same 192 questions the distilled writer gave 72 exact answers with regular print in both fields. A regular page with a Kai question gave two correct answers, only one shared with the original 72. A Kai page with a regular question gave zero. In each crossed condition it failed every complete passage. Either input field can therefore disrupt an answer on this cohort. This experiment does not locate the fault within a particular layer. The next matched training comparison should align both visual inputs to their regular-print states while preserving access to the source bitmap for inscription copying. Measure transfer to a font family hidden throughout training as well as familiar print, question erasure, open answers and original historical forms.
