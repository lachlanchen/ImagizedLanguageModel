# Can the context choose a different phrase?

In a 192-passage test, broadening supervision reduced glyph edits but left the visual writer at zero faithful translations in eighteen readings. A preserved astronomical number acquired an erroneous unit of age. The current comparison changes the loss on the same writer. One arm rehearses correct visual phrases; the other also learns to assign a lower score to the phrase selected for a different source.

## Data and outcomes

Starting from the released broad checkpoint, replay every one of the 55,872 original pictured book pairs exactly once. Use 39 training pairs selected and edited from a deterministic 96-pair sample of the common-answer-prefix inventory. The positive phrases describe distinguishable events, states or referents recoverable from their respective sources. Each update sees 64 book examples and four paired examples, drawn in shuffled cycles. Both arms see the same order and the same alternatives. Keep the full-output checkpoint at update 873, one seed, AdamW learning rate 0.0001, gradient clipping at 1, peak allocation below 10 GiB and wall time below 30 minutes per fit.

For a source image stream \(x\), pictured target prefix \(h\) and continuation \(y\), average the log softmax of the existing exemplar-head similarities over the cells of \(y\). At each step supply the observed history and advance the source pointer when the observed cell equals its next planned anchor. This yields a conditional **phrase score** \(s(x,h,y)\). The comparison measures compatibility under supplied history. The generative writer retains its separate close, copy, retrieve and draw decisions. The score bank includes the original exemplars and training phrase cells. Use a common bank extended with any new candidate cells for the paired test.

Both arms minimise the original book loss plus half the mean negative score of the correct continuations. The crossed arm adds half the mean of \(\log(1+\exp(s_{wrong}-s_{right}))\), computed independently for each source. Each loss uses both directions. The four scores of a pair allow two tests. A positive sum of the diagonal scores minus the off-diagonal scores measures an interaction. Requiring both strict choices also rejects a fixed preference for one of the answers.

The 192 new archive sources come from 177 further chapters. Eighteen whole readings were selected before fitting. Six natural source pairs and nine authored pairs probe roles, polarity and quantities. For the authored quantity problems, distinguish accurate translation of the given facts from explicit computation of a result. Credit a complete faithful rendering for the former and score the requested detail separately. Record the complete generated images for every source and use a method-masked reader to assess whole translations and paired details. A model must improve both strict paired choices and **faithful autonomous answers on the eighteen new readings** relative to rehearsal before the comparison supports improved semantic transfer. Retain the prior writer, a literal-copy control and a phrase composer. Report cases where source overlap makes a forced choice easy. No gain on the complete outputs means the conditional score remains a diagnostic.
