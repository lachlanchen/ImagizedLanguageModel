# Reading two people’s roles from images of classical Chinese

The strongest visual reader in this comparison read **38 of 56 Chinese-request answers** from 14 reserved excerpts of the *Discourses of the States* (Guoyu). Each excerpt supported two questions about different people. We asked both questions again after exchanging the names. The reader answered all four correctly on four excerpts. Its frozen parent completed three, the equal-label control two, and text Qwen3-8B eight. English questions yielded 16 exact visual answers and zero complete excerpts. The image reader consumes a stream of raster cells, projects their visual features into a contextual encoder, and scores contiguous spans wherever they appear on the page. The four-layer attention update and boundary head contain 50,690 trainable parameters. The deployed model has 86,000,354 parameters plus 11,799,814 buffer elements, occupying 391,196,600 bytes of tensor state. The aligned fit presented 19,200 classical questions and 9,600 modern questions in 1,200 updates on one RTX 4090 D. It took 24.62 s and 6.78 kJ of measured device energy with 1.33 GiB peak PyTorch allocation. These figures exclude the inherited model's pretraining, visual fitting, preparation and evaluation. The runtime uses neither Qwen nor a text tokenizer. The output is selected source ink. Open writing, new glyph synthesis, English role understanding and matched-quality compute savings require separate evidence. ## What the smaller readers learned

A 64,666-parameter student trained on 2,277 natural bilingual question pairs and 349 constructed pairs from modern novels gave 34/112 Chinese and 26/112 English answers on 28 excerpts from *A New Account*. The same training without the bilingual matching term gave 34 and 22. Neither model completed a four-answer contrast. Lean verifies that distinguishable translation scores are compatible with a constant answer decoder. [Bilingual protocol](role_question_binding_protocol.md) · [Audit of 5,280 outputs](evidence/role_question_binding_20261124/audit.json) · [Lean proof](../formal/span_list/ParallelQuestionMargin.lean)

Using the Kyoto Classical Chinese Dependency Treebank, we extracted 3,960 eligible source relations covering 49 verbs, retaining 194 original dual-role clauses and constructing 1,979 additional pairs of clauses. After 347,680 pictured training questions, the small aligned reader completed one of 16 Zuo excerpts in Chinese (23/64 individual answers), versus zero complete for the original and equal-label readers. English yielded 15/64 and zero complete; on modern novels exact answers dropped to 33/452 and 26/452. The original bilingual generative writer also completed zero classical contrasts across these first two works. The archived fit remains available for comparison. ## What would distinguish reading the action from tracking a position?

The two name images exchanged places in these experiments. A model that finds the correct position without interpreting the verb can still succeed. A second verified result gives a stricter diagnostic. Keep both people at fixed locations, exchange which action each performs, and ask the *same* question on both pages. Any reader invariant to that edit must miss at least one answer when the source images differ. The theorem specifies behaviour distinguishable by an experiment; learning that behaviour is an empirical question. A subsequent comparison should blind the fixed-order passage pairs and score all four original outputs before adding a generative answer writer.
