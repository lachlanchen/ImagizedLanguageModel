# Learning from a writer’s first wrong glyph

Given the pictured classical sentence 今魏之辞倨而币薄。, a supplied insertion boundary and the modern prefix 现在魏, the preference-trained writer drew 国的 and stopped. Its parent drew 的. In another held-out sentence the target was 所以 and the updated writer produced 一个. Both model outputs can be read. Only one restores the recorded wording. Both corrections learned from 241,256 insertion sites, with eight epochs and identical example order. A separate set of 13,440 sites selected their final scale by full first-image correctness. The preference writer recovered one of 7,504 previously unseen insertion instances and stopped on 21,630 of 24,048 generations. A single study-involved model reader rated the anonymously ordered phrases before matching them to their writers. All 48 recorded insertions were judged acceptable. [Training record](evidence/first_emission_20261118/fit.json) · [Reading record](evidence/first_emission_20261118/reading_result.json)

On the reserved 837 sites from 73 contributing chapters, the preference update improved whole phrases from 26 to 33. The 95% paired chapter-bootstrap interval for that gain was 0.23 to 1.59 percentage points. It recovered fewer phrases than the count control (59), with a difference of −4.87 to −1.25 points. On these same chapters the first-image count fell from 134 to 131. The preference objective does not consistently improve first-image accuracy in new chapters. [Reserved chapters](evidence/first_emission_20261118/reserved.json)

Across all sites, 678 previous first-image errors became correct, 508 previous successes became errors and 7,214 wrong first images changed into different wrong drawings. Of 19,128 parent errors, only 1,089 now assigned a higher probability to the reference signature than to the old mistake. In 336 of those cases a different generated competitor still outranked the reference. The score concerns 48 measured stroke pixels. Image correctness compares all 1,024. The objectives, independent pixel recount, public CPU examples and raw source examples are available. The model runs locally with visual inputs, draws its own continuation and uses its own pixels as feedback. The new correction contains 131,456 parameters. Mining the previous images, then preparing states, fitting and calibrating the two corrections took 32.1 and 63.4 seconds on an RTX 4090 D. The latter phase peaked at 1.02 GiB allocated device memory. Those figures exclude the parent model, corpus preparation and construction of 76.5 MB of resident count arrays. These experiments establish limited insertion learning with help from the supplied position and modern prefix. Autonomous book writing, open-ended etymology and efficiency at comparable language quality are open. ## Writing with no supplied boundary or prefix

Given only the classical source and pictured translation instruction, both models reproduced **0/84** complete translations. The preference writer reached its phrase limit on 62 sentences and stopped on 22. Repetition explains many of those failures. Of the capped outputs, 52 ended with at least three consecutive copies of the same one- to twelve-glyph block. None of their references did. The writer required 5,532 glyph edits over 5,994 reference glyphs, compared with 5,576 for its parent and 4,578 for direct copying. The complete output for 少阴，少阳。 was 年轻，阴，少阳。. It missed the particular military interpretation supplied by the source corpus. [Full autonomous scores and displayed images](evidence/first_emission_20261118/sentences.json) · [Repetition counts](evidence/first_emission_20261118/cycles.json)

On a separate 32-pair calibration diagnostic, the offline Qwen3 4B teacher identified 24/64 insertions under both option orders with authentic sources and 5/64 after a source swap. A response-format sensitivity test that resolved every reply yielded 35/64 and 8/64. The visual preference writer's corresponding strict candidate likelihood choices were 33/64 and 31/64. The text teacher was sensitive to source identity, but it missed the preregistered 40/64 criterion. The teacher supplied no generated words to the visual writer, and these calibration results do not estimate performance in new books. ### Reproduce the comparisons

The phrase experiment builds on the chapter roster and checkpoints of the matched sentence experiment. With those local data artifacts present, mine the first images with `scripts/prepare_first_emission_training.py` if the preparation is absent, fit with `scripts/train_first_emission.py`, generate with `scripts/evaluate_first_emission.py` and independently recount with `scripts/report_first_emission.py`. Run `scripts/evaluate_first_emission_sentences.py` for the source-only check. The public inference example needs only the versioned weights, count tables and pictured inputs in this repository. The scorer and training scripts reserve GPU 0 through the existing project lock. Frozen run directories preserve original measurements.

Pairing complete translations from the existing calibration roster would give misleadingly strong results. Matching bags of identical pictured glyphs alone assigns 6,714 of 6,720 full sentence pairs correctly. Only four distinct pairs evade both unigram and adjacent-pair overlap. The next reading comparison needs genuinely confusable, independently judged alternatives.
