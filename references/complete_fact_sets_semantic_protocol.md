# Calibrating a semantic reading of list answers

Several Chinese answers contain a longer but meaningful place name. Test whether strict normalized equality hides complete paraphrases. This secondary analysis was designed after inspecting the primary counts. Choose twelve questions per language by a new salted hash, requiring the entire first two-to-six-part reference literally visible on the page. Present the question, excerpt and expected meaning to a local Qwen3-8B Q4 text assessor. Present five unlabeled replies in a reproducibly shuffled order and again in reverse. The replies are the two fitted readers, a complete annotation, that annotation missing its last part, and the annotation with one unrelated extra part from the same language. Keep all assessed replies and decisions. Treat synonymous wording and reordered items as equivalent; an incomplete list or a substantive unsupported addition cannot be fully correct.

Before interpreting adjusted model scores require at least 22/24 complete anchors credited on both orders, at most two omissions or additions credited on either order in each family, and at least 90% binary complete-status agreement across all entries. Report all controls. If calibration fails, make no semantic accuracy estimate from the assessor. Even a calibrated panel is small, machine-reviewed and limited to these visible references.
