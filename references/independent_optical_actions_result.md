# Recognising a translation and proposing its content

Two independent visual language bodies learned on one RTX 4090 D. Both read an ordered image grid and return an image. On four unseen books they could assign two supplied translations to their sources on 95/96 and 93/96 pairs. They could rarely produce an adequate translation without those alternatives. The strip writer correctly translated “She is my wife.” into “她是我的妻子。” It was its only credited book answer. Each normalized complete side had zero exact matches among the 268,342 training pairs after punctuation and spacing were removed. **Free book translation.** The comparison reserved 24 paired passages from each of four previously unused works. Each source was rendered to PNG and read back as pixels for inference. An 8B Qwen3-VL judge assessed complete meaning with candidate identities hidden and the order reversed. The strip writer had zero English and one Chinese credit. The single-glyph and exchanged-source controls had zero. The paired gain was 0.52 percentage points with a four-work bootstrap interval of 0.00–1.56. The registered requirement for a strictly positive lower bound failed. Qwen3-8B Q4 received ordinary text and earned 166/192 (89 English and 77 Chinese). The judge credited 133/192 provided translations and zero unrelated foils, so model-based scoring is an imperfect proxy for bilingual readers. A second, reference-informed assessment scored 1, 0 and 119 for strip, single glyph and Qwen. The strip writer chose to stop on 189 requests and the single-glyph writer on all 192. CPU replay reproduced the archived pixels, length and stop on 574/576 generated answers. The strip and exchange discrepancies involve the same underlying pictured source. GPU originals remain preserved. **Reading with supplied alternatives.** The book assignment compares both correct conditional log likelihoods with both exchanged likelihoods, including the final stop. Predicting solely from the relative source and target lengths scored 57/96 with half credit for ties. The controlled test reverses the agent and patient of each bilingual sentence, keeping exact glyph multisets fixed on both sides. Eight name pairs, six transitive predicates and two polarities produced 96 possible pairs. Twelve pairs were outside the output repertoire, leaving 84 pairs per direction. The single-glyph writer correctly assigned 84/84 into English and 83/84 into Chinese. The strip writer scored 82/84 in each direction. Both writers saw the candidate answer histories during these tests. This is evidence of sensitivity to role order within these constructions. **Where free writing lost content.** On the same controlled sources, the strip writer omitted both prescribed names on 258 of 336 requests and emitted exactly one on 78. The single-glyph writer omitted both on 212, emitted one on 117 and both on seven. Names were assessed by matching the emitted bitmaps to the finite print repertoire, allowing case changes and requiring English word boundaries. Alternative transliterations and paraphrases can evade this measure. In the first fixed book case, a man throws himself into a raging fire. Both writers produce English words while losing the event. Figure 1 retains the first preselected case from each of two books alongside the separately labelled credited example. The editable deck includes the other two works. **Model, scale and cost.** Both bodies have 3,196,224 allocated trainable parameters and share the numerical initialization, batch order, optimizer schedule and 11,592 updates. The control uses only the first cell of the output compositor. The strip condition can activate several positions. OPUS-100 contributed 240,000 pairs and the parallel book corpus contributed 28,342. Each body saw 2,146,736 directed examples. The network computes output keys from pictured glyphs or strips and feeds the chosen pictured form into its next step. The neural drawing motor supplies 2,266,112 frozen scalar parameters and 604,480 fixed glyph-code values. Compilation checks every emitted bitmap against the training palette. Inference loads no text language model. Both output inventories are discrete and finite.

Including stops, the four passes require 59,311,744 decisions for single cells and 25,750,104 for strips. Padding and output repertoire widths raise evaluated head scores from 79.94 to 164.86 billion. End-to-end epoch timing is 14.3% longer for strips, before adding data preparation. The exact break-even condition for a linear score-equivalent cost is proved in Lean 4.12.0. For these observed unpadded counts the shared cost would need to exceed 1,045.10 score-equivalents per removed decision. This approximation omits memory traffic, padding and source encoding. The measured timing governs the hardware comparison. The experiment supplies no matched-quality efficiency advantage over a text LLM. **Research decision.** The next comparison targets content selection before fluent writing. Teach an independent body to predict which target-side pictured spans are supported by a source passage, then condition free decoding on that prediction. Learn the span evidence on parallel training images. Hold the drawing motor, examples, training budget and test rules fixed. Measure participant and predicate survival, intact complete answers, how many unsupported names enter, and transfer to new books. Oracle target spans can provide a diagnostic ceiling only. This trial resolves the writing-unit comparison and leaves general free generation open.
