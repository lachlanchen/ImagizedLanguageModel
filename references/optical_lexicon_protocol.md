# Inducing a bilingual lexicon from paired glyph images

Both full-source writers earned one complete book meaning in 96 attempts. One translated bedroom as surgery, despite recognizing the bedroom in the reverse direction. Test direct distributional grounding of repeated visual spans in parallel sentences. Use the previously fitted seven-cell English and Chinese strip inventories. Segment both sides deterministically by longest matching physical span. Deduplicate complete pairs from the book-rehearsal corpus and the 268,342-pair OPUS/library release. Fit a sparse-alignment IBM Model 1 distribution in both directions with five EM passes, a null source position and unigram pseudocount smoothing. Training inputs are only arrays of glyph bitmaps. A four-work follow-up panel uses the next 12 admissible pairs per work, disjoint on each pictured side from the earlier panel and training. Development examples, including each pictured side, must be excluded from lexicon training.

For each document, marginalize a stationary mixture of target strips over the source spans. At each generated glyph, sum over every compatible latent strip boundary. Use the log conditional probability ratio between this source-dependent visual expert and its target-only unigram version to adjust the frozen neural writer's next-glyph distribution. Preserve its stop probability. Choose correction strength from 0, 0.25, 0.5 and 1 on development next-image log loss. Report the untouched writer, the selected correction, and an exchanged-source lexical control on the fresh book images. If zero wins, keep the original writer as the selected method and label strength 0.5 exploratory. Judge complete translations in both directions and retain original images. First measure whether the induced conditional assigns higher probability to the paired human target than to the same target under another source, independent of neural scoring.
