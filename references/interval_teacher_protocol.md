# Teaching a causal image writer where a reply begins and ends

The mixed writer missed 177 of 256 natural answers on the last paired reading test. Ninety-four errors started with unaccepted ink. Another 83 began with a correct glyph. A visual span reader recovered 98 answers that the writer missed, and missed 21 answers that the writer recovered. Test whether transferring its distribution over pictured source intervals improves an independently deployed writer.

Start each arm at the released mixed writer. Replay its complete 20,271-example mixture and add up to 2,048 eligible paired training passages per script, with two distinct human answers on each page. Use one epoch, identical seeded batches, all final weights, a maximum of 20 GiB allocated and 20 minutes of optimization per arm. Use three conditions. Replay retains the original whole-answer and first-copy losses. Alignment adds supervised source positions and stopping for complete contiguous human answers, marginalizing over duplicate occurrences. Distillation uses the same added weight, half supervised alignment and half prefix-conditional next-position and stop probabilities from a frozen span reader at unit temperature. All three receive the same actual question images, targets and exposure. Deployment uses the same 86.6M causal image network in every case.

For each answer prefix, partition the teacher's matching intervals by whether they end there or continue at a source position. Prove the mass accounting in Lean and test the numerical implementation by enumerating intervals. Teacher scores come exclusively from training pictures. Freeze the model and evaluate the earlier 128 paired sources and up to 128 further eligible pairs per script from development data, excluding earlier pages and protected DRCD material. Report exact stopped images, complete pairs, question erasure, previous reader-only and writer-only cases, broad human answers, dates with competing evidence, selective quotation and historical ink under both instructions. Compare equal training exposure and report the incremental compute, including teacher preparation. Promote a checkpoint for general use only if it beats replay on complete new pairs, increases broad natural exact answers over replay and retains both orders of 言. Record any loss on other tasks. If simple alignment or replay wins, keep those results and diagnose the remaining gap.
