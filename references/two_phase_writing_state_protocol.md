# Refreshing the writing state after two glyphs

For 182 of the 196 single-state failures the emitted physical path outranks the canonical path. Among 87 failures on rare expressions, 82 favor the emitted path. Test whether conditioning the single frozen recurrent writer a second time can recover the rare forms. For every one of the same 3,845 observed training expressions, initialize two bounded 192-dimensional vectors from the original passage centroid. The first supplies the initial recurrent state and conditions all patterns in the first two glyphs. The second conditions every subsequent pattern and stop, including at lengths beyond four. Preserve the actual recurrent hidden state and emitted-pixel feedback at the transition. Keep the physical renderer, all network weights, the normal beam and stopping rule. Only the continuous states are optimized, jointly, for the original 100 Adam steps and physical trajectory objective. This doubles the supplied control coordinates per expression. Use exactly the original centroids, images, initial/final beam, counts and thresholds, including all nine canonically unreachable forms. Verify duplicate-state decoding and likelihood agree with the ordinary implementation. Report original outcomes gained and lost, accuracy by frequency and reachability. Score at least 90% overall and in the one-to-five group before testing a reader that predicts these controls from a passage independently. The paired states receive reference supervision; their own scores measure writing capacity only. Apply the original compute and memory limits.
