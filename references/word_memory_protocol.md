# Retrieving previously seen answer images

In the preceding masked-word experiment the visual writer recovered none of 327 answer words absent from the displayed inputs, even with the other answer words supplied. A fitting-only inventory contained many of those word images. This experiment estimates how much memory retrieval helps on a fresh story cohort.

Reserve positions 400 through 546 in the existing salted ordering of eligible CoQA training-source stories. The first 400 belong to two earlier experiments. Fit only on the original training turns and the earlier MultiSpanQA training additions. Save and compare ordered presentation hashes for both input representations.

Freeze the previously released masked writers. For each representation add a learned gate and a query against 2,048 previously observed answer-word images, ranked by training-answer frequency with first occurrence breaking ties. Fit the probability of the observed word under the mixture of existing copy/spelling and memory distributions. Train for 3,200 updates of 32 turns using the prior four-way sampler, half full masks and half random masking, seed 812641. Limit each fit to 20 minutes and 12 GiB allocated on GPU 0. Record training and preparation energy separately. Compare with the unchanged masked writer on both images and bytes. Preserve the original length predictor and four-pass schedule. At inference merge copy and memory mass for identical word images and retain a greedy spelling candidate.

Primary measurements are stopped normalized exact answers that lose credit under both input exchanges, and word F1 on turns requiring absent multiword phrasing. Claim an autonomous composition improvement only with a net gain in those exchange-sensitive answers and a positive lower endpoint on the paired story-bootstrap difficult F1 difference. Report diagnostic single-hidden-word recovery separately for copyable, source-absent in memory, and source-absent outside memory. Compare original and exchanged inputs on all held turns. A recovery gain alone supports a word-emission result. If whole-answer composition still fails, use the comparison to distinguish word availability from contextual selection.
