# When can a smaller visual writer express more?

A model asked to carry an unfamiliar mark through a sentence can learn where the mark belongs and take its exact pixels from the page. A model asked to translate *what the mark means* must retain its visual distinctions. We implemented both reading paths and measured their different effects on original Mandarin and English sentences.

## Language from image streams

Two 1,688,480-parameter encoder–decoders trained independently of a text teacher on 31,387 Tatoeba bilingual pairs in both directions. They share initialization, examples, updates, four-cell receptive windows and the same learned optical writing head. Each saw 753,288 directed examples across twelve passes. The lowest development loss occurred at pass eight in both arms. Inference reads aligned 32 × 32 cells and emits a stopped stream of bitmaps. A 16 × 16 array of local ink densities supplies each cell's optical features. The ordinary writing head selects from 78 English and 3,700 Mandarin training shapes. No BPE model, OCR transcription or language-model service runs in either student. Language direction is supplied.

On the same ordinary examples local Qwen3-8B Q4 earned 31/32 and 30/32 from text. It preserved all 16 additional relation, time, number and negation contrasts. Neither student passed any. ## Physical support and substitution

A trained address branch adds 77,121 parameters. Each training target receives the sum of its spelling probability and its probability across every matching position on the source page. We replaced aligned personal names with authentic historical specimens in human translations. The names supply controlled positions. The specimens' historical meanings play no part in those replacements. Historical or phonetic interpretation of a mark would need different supervision. The open branch emitted the new shape exactly once and stopped in 106/140 requests. The closed branch in precisely the same trained network did so in 0/140. Neither reproduced a complete reference in these 140 requests.

For carrying opaque names, a second execution mode preserves the exact payload and assigns distinct unknown images numbered slots in order of first appearance. Each slot uses the same optical feature, averaged over 1,024 training specimens. A neural sentence plan refers to slots, and the writer retrieves the original 32 × 32 image for each such action. Modern training shapes retain their optical features. The two modes use exactly the same 1,765,601 weights. Across 35 test sentences in both directions, the number of swap pairs that copied both specimens once, stopped and preserved every surrounding glyph rose from 5/70 to 56/70. On 40 additional development sentences the counts were 3/80 and 66/80. The latter sentences previously influenced the base checkpoint through ordinary development likelihood. This was their first substitution comparison.

The canonical mode gave two exact complete references out of the 140 test requests, both assignments of the same sentence. The corresponding validation result was zero of 160. The human reference for the successful sentence conveys looking for work while omitting the source adjective “good”. In the first failing example the model loses the occupation of dolphin trainer. Fourteen test pairs kept a stable plan without copying exactly once. This is a visible gap between applying a valid symmetry and learning the right sentence plan.

With a bounded in-memory cache, 140 test requests needed 66 actual neural generations in 1.86 seconds, versus 140 in 3.93 seconds with caching disabled. All output bytes and stop flags matched. The development comparison used 71 rather than 160 calls. These two-thread CPU timings measure paired, same-context requests. A stream of unique sentences offers no such cache reuse. ## What the mathematics gives the model

1. **Representing the input.** Let $P$ arrange four consecutive optical cells into a block, with explicit absent padding. A left inverse recovers each original cell. Consequently every decision on the original stream can be implemented after this rearrangement with identical pointwise loss. In particular, for finite random variables, $H(Y\mid P(X))=H(Y\mid X)$. Only the inverse and risk identity are formalized. Pooling actual pixels and projecting a block to a finite-width hidden vector are later operations. For a deterministic projected feature $Z$, the unrestricted optimal log-loss penalty is $I(Y;X\mid Z)$, by the entropy chain rule. For binary decisions, if a collision merges positive and negative classes of weights $p,n$, the smallest attainable error mass on that class is $\min(p,n)$. Lean proves the last statement. Ordered packing alone guarantees neither an injective learned projection nor an efficient universal decoder.
2. **Representing the output.** A fixed visual palette can emit only its stored glyphs. Taking a visible address adds the page to the output support. The increase in representable shapes holds for any unseen bitmap on that page with no new parameter per specimen. Predicting the correct address, timing and stopping remains learned. A text architecture offered the same raw-pixel addressing operation could share this advantage.
3. **Learning across renderings.** Let a plan contain ordinary glyph actions and references to local image slots. When the raw images for distinct slots are distinct and lie outside the ordinary palette, rendering admits a left inverse. Lean proves that the rendered prediction equals the rendered reference **if and only if** the plans agree. It also proves substitution commutes with rendering. For an arbitrary noiseless binary decision on $M$ contexts and $K$ payloads, exact identification of the entire decision table in the worst case needs a labelled point at every one of the $MK$ pairs. When the decision truly depends only on context, one label per context is necessary and sufficient. A missing context admits two indistinguishable training hypotheses giving opposite answers there. This is the finite identification problem formalized in Lean. It neither gives a sample bound for SGD on this network nor says that every question has the symmetry. In particular, to distinguish an oracle and a bronze form *by appearance*, both cannot be treated as the same anonymous payload.
4. **When compression saves work.** At source length $4n$ and target length $m$, global source interactions fall from $16n^2$ to $n^2$ per head, and source–target interactions from $4mn$ to $mn$. Unequal final blocks use $\lceil N/4\rceil$. These counts and the coverage bound are checked in Lean. With the same-width learned projectors, measured translation deteriorated in both directions. The default ordinary translator therefore retains stride one.

If a fixed-font image is rendered deterministically from transcript $T$, then $I(Y;X\mid T)=0$. The rendering can still change which regularities a small learner can exploit. Two original attestations with the same transcript can instead retain visually meaningful evidence about date or style. Its value requires a measured prediction on those original images.

The loss invariance statement concerns the existence of a mathematical decision after packing. Carrying a specimen uses a different guarantee that holds for the actual composed action sequence. An application can benefit from preserving the physical source and sharing decisions over irrelevant variations while continuing to train on those visual variations that change the requested meaning. ## Does training on editorial prose help?

Two continuations began at the selected stride-one checkpoint. Each saw 400,000 directed sentences. One reused conversation translations. The other mixed 25,000 conversational and 25,000 news pairs per pass, covering 61,448 distinct editorial pairs in four passes. News was longer, yielding 12,543,465 target positions at 6.41 kJ, compared with 8,481,406 at 5.91 kJ for replay. The corpus screening retained 78,251 eligible training and 954 held pairs. It excluded 256,297 over-length rows, 26,293 outside the fixed inventories and 2,007 empty or overlapping rows. Each direction was assessed on 16 new conversational families and 16 separate news article groups. Both directions from a family are dependent.

| Meaning accepted in both grading orders | Base | Replay | News mix | Qwen 8B Q4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Conversation, both directions | 4/32 | 2/32 | 5/32 | 29/32 |
| News, both directions | 0/32 | 0/32 | 0/32 | 26/32 |

All 64 human references were accepted twice and all 64 foils rejected twice. Replay stopped on 26/32 news requests and the mixed model on 32/32. Mean news chrF2 rose from 9.55 to 13.25. The criterion for a semantic gain in both domains failed. Further training should directly evaluate the contrasting argument roles and relations that these whole-sentence losses missed.
