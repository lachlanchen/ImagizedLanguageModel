# From a pictured fact to a complete proposition

The dynamic copy-run writer improved glyph likelihood when given the right output history, but gave no exact source-absent answer in 192 fresh cases. Removing its whole-span route made the English writer stop on only 14/96 questions. Several semantically correct replies were source fragments. Test a different supervision signal: the human declarative sentences in QA2D. Distinguish reading the fact from expressing it. At deployment an independent optical interval reader first writes a short answer from the complete pictured question and passage. A second optical writer reads the question and that emitted fact and writes a declarative image one or more glyphs at a time. No human answer, text parser, candidate list or teacher participates at inference. Keep the original single-page model available for bilingual open writing.

Reserve 128 distinct QA2D development questions with complete human targets absent from both the question and answer, including up to 32 from other source datasets. Suppress overlapping question and SQuAD passage identities in the sentence writer's training split. Compare the same physical writer before and after 6,400 updates of 24 examples from the QA2D training release. Freeze the optical reading stage. Test the sentence writer with the correct intermediate separately from the full picture-to-picture pipeline. The QA2D SQuAD development sources come from the original SQuAD training release and can have been seen by the upstream reader. Therefore also reserve 96 fresh official SQuAD development passage families distinct from the previous paired studies. Use only these for new-passage claims, score the factual content and inspect masked outputs. A complete match given a correct answer measures expression, not retrieval. On full-system failures compare actual readback with the human fact. Retain the bilingual regressions of the independent optical model and make the English sentence mode explicit. Charge the full dependency chain, and do not infer general intelligence, historical reading or a speed advantage from this experiment.
