# Does removing name preference recover action assignment?

The aligned clause writer favours the correct canonical answer on 53 of 96 new-story questions and passes one of 24 complete assignment comparisons when given both candidate clauses. Test the hypothesis that it knows the roles but prefers the written form of one name. Use the fixed earlier and newly reserved stories, both languages and both page versions. Keep all fitted weights frozen.

Simultaneously exchange every complete occurrence of the two annotated participant names in the page, question and both candidate clauses. The page edit that changes who performed the action is a separate experimental axis. Average the original supported-minus-rival log score with the consistently renamed score. A fixed name preference cancels in this sum. Count ties as failures. Require improvement in the number of complete comparisons on both story sets against the unmodified aligned writer, and report the common parent and independent-label controls. This is a diagnostic with supplied names and two complete candidate replies. Its result is ineligible as free-generation accuracy. No new model fit is authorized by a score improvement alone. Use one GPU, under 20 GiB allocated and five minutes of computation.

The aligned writer improved from zero to two complete comparisons on the earlier stories, but remained at one on the new stories. Individual decisions on the new stories fell from 53 to 48 of 96. The condition for advancing this approach failed. The common parent remained at two complete new-story comparisons and the independent-label control improved from zero to one. The three frozen writers took 6.25 seconds together, with 0.62 GiB peak allocated GPU memory. A stable preference for a written name explains only part of the problem.
