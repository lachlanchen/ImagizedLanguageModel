# Can a pictured question change the beginning of a written answer?

The previous Chinese writer deviated from 228 of 300 input-absent references before it reached the first missing glyph. This comparison asks whether the pairing between a pictured question and its human answer teaches the writer to choose a better opening and complete the answer from a blank page. [Observed failures](bitmap_history_writing_result.md)

Use the original human questions, answers and documents from the screened DuReader training split. Retrieve an excerpt of at most 192 characters by matching the question alone against the first four documents and their first five paragraphs. Screen the selected window against the reserved evaluation text and require a printable bitmap for every cell. The resulting training corpus contains 191,007 of 192,230 screened records and 15,718,278 evidence positions. There were 715 excluded for passage overlap, 245 with an unrenderable character and 263 without a matching window. The retrieved excerpt may omit information the human answer requires. All corpus content and derived weights remain local for noncommercial research or teaching.

Fit two writers from the same random initialization. Give one the correct question. In the second, use a fixed random permutation with no question record in its original row. Keep the evidence window, answer, optimizer, ordering of target examples, bitmap inventory and number of updates matched. Train one full pass through the new corpus followed by six passes through the established 2,565 training questions. Use 96 records per batch, AdamW, a gradient-norm limit of one and learning rates of 0.001 and 0.00025. Warm up over 80 steps. Limit each arm to one hour and 12 GiB of allocated memory on GPU 0. Only the final trained state of each arm is eligible for evaluation.

The encoder maps physical 32-pixel square images through shared layers and reads the passage alongside the pictured question. A recurrent network reads the preceding output glyph and assigns probability to stopping, writing a familiar shape, or copying a visible cell. Add the probabilities of every way to emit the same bitmap before taking the logarithm during training or choosing a glyph during decoding. A white source cell has its own positive address and remains distinguishable from stopping. The model receives the original image addresses solely for indexing and testing exact pixel equality. The output dictionary contains training-answer images. New shapes can be copied from an input; drawing an independently invented shape requires a separate test.

Keep the 571 original development questions and all three answer groups fixed. Evaluate each writer with its original questions and again with questions rotated by one record, retaining each passage and reference. Generate from an empty answer through a learned stop or 64 output cells. Archive the actual bitmaps, addresses, stop decisions, edit distances, first-cell and first-four matches. Report reachable supplied-history negative log likelihood and unreachable target count separately. Report results in all four conditions. The question-blind first-sentence baseline gives 66 complete matches; the previous autonomous writer gives 25, all contiguous.

The primary test for composition requires at least 15 of 300 complete stopped input-absent references, more than either broken-pairing control, and more than 66 complete references overall. Inspect responsiveness and factual support on the previously designated cases before any positive language claim. An opening-choice benefit requires a positive paired 95% bootstrap lower bound for first-four accuracy against both the deranged training control and the test-time rotation. Resample whole cases (10,000 draws, seed 19427); multiple references, argument paraphrases and external adjudication remain limitations. If the writer misses the full-answer test, retain the released passage reader and inspect autonomous repetition, opening choice, stopping and evidence coverage before another training change.
