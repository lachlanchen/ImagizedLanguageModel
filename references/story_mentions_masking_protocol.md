# Learning the position of a character independently of their name

The unrestricted optical reader selected a correct visible answer on 4/188 Chinese development queries and 51/177 English ones. Supplying annotated name intervals left its total accuracy below nearest-name selection in both languages. The new comparison factorizes name discovery and attachment. Preserve the original scores.

Collapse each occurrence of a candidate name to the same learned marker. Retain its source interval and equality to other name occurrences outside the network. Score occurrences from their surroundings and the marked pronoun. Aggregate occurrences that would write identical answer pixels. Copy the winning source strip or abstain. Evaluate with a cast of possible spellings and with candidates proposed automatically by the original locator. The cast condition receives the distinct earlier annotated spellings, without locations or cluster identifiers. Its cost and information differ from the unassisted reader. Report the fraction of wanted names present after matching in each condition and the nearest candidate under that identical information.

Train on all retained training questions using candidate spellings from the annotations and the frozen locator, mixed equally per batch. Use one fixed 1,200-update schedule, 32 cases, balanced training languages, 12-minute and 4-GiB limit per fit. Compare optical and byte readers plus a learned control receiving only geometry and name recurrence. A fixed recency bias represents the useful nearest-name prior. Compare both targets of natural query switches, especially when the same name is nearest. The published development and test results of the earlier model informed this decision. The new scores on those works are follow-up results. Test on additional independent material where possible. Keep automatic and supplied-cast inference available regardless of which improves.
