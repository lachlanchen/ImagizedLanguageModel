# Recovering the print face restores the existing answer

Four held books supplied 96 new paired passages and 192 English or Chinese translation requests. Changing the source typeface changed all 192 free translations. Passing the serif images through the frozen glyph classifier restored all 6,432 canonical source cells and all 192 canonical outputs. Each of the 960 original model answers reproduced on CPU. These observations refer to known cell boundaries and the writer's finite drawing repertoire. The source-only Qwen3-VL judge credited 161 text-comparator answers and 137 corpus references, and rejected all cross-book foils. We grouped both directions and all requests within each book for uncertainty. The adapted-minus-direct serif increase was 2.60 percentage points (four-book bootstrap interval 1.04–4.17). The entire credited set is pictured in Figure 3. A close reading finds that request 0027 drops the explicit destination and 0028 drops the force of “only”. The primary scores retain both credits. The guided writer's 187 uncredited canonical answers survived exact optical restoration. Since identical answers must receive the same primary credit, the highest count possible under the observed answer-equality constraint is five. This is a conditional upper bound for this frozen writer and these data. The proof constructs the most favorable feasible scores for the answers allowed to change. [Lean theorem](../formal/span_list/PrintRecovery.lean)

Compiling the two directional glyph motors eliminated repeated neural drawing and feedback calculations across requests. It cost 97 ms and 8.30 MiB to tabulate the outputs, feedback and prediction keys. The direct and compiled implementations both matched all 192 archived canonical answers, including stops. Five paired warm traversals of 192 inputs yielded a median speedup of 1.18× on one RTX 4090 D (range 1.02–1.43×). Median compiled time including the one-off compilation was 0.484 s. Measurements exclude model loading, input transfer and PNG encoding. The time ratio compares two implementations of one ILM. The Lean equivalence theorem assumes equal table values; the device replay checks actual arithmetic on these inputs. The remaining failure is semantic even when the source cells, motor shapes and feedback are recovered exactly. The next model experiment should change the language decision from a cell to a learned visual span, emit its boundaries autonomously, and retain the present guided writer, matched passage controls and bitmapped answers. The four books used here must be excluded from future fresh panels. The released PIXEL and PixelGPT implementations provide masked and causal patch objectives for future matched training comparisons.
