# What a compact glyph writer learned from pictured facts

A 1,368,010-parameter writer learned to describe a printed restaurant record in English. A 223,200-parameter listener learned to read fact sets from its proposed answer images. Each network was trained from scratch on one RTX 4090 D. The source was 33,525 human descriptions of 12,568 distinct records from the public cleaned E2E corpus. The writer's sampler touched 33,524 descriptions. Generated answers used a finite alphabet of 73 distinct printed glyphs and a learned stop. Chinese, English and interleaved input fields used identical cell geometry; proper names retained their original spelling. Historical exemplars belong to a separate retrieval experiment. [Dataset](https://github.com/tuetschek/e2e-cleaning) · [Inference and training](../models/semantic_page/README.md)

## A change in one pictured fact

The first held card with an editable family policy names Blue Spice, pub, Chinese food, city centre, near Rainbow Vegetarian Café. The returned sentence for a negative policy reads “Blue Spice is a pub located near the Rainbow Vegetarian Café in the city centre. It serves Chinese food and is not family friendly.” Replacing only the policy produces “Blue Spice is a family friendly pub located near the Rainbow Vegetarian Café in the city centre. It offers Chinese food.” Both complete sentence images were reproduced through the CPU PNG entry point. The test includes 1,291 such interventions. Neither an external language model nor gold record fields are required by the reader at inference. ## How often was the sentence faithful?

Each input language uses the same 1,847 test meanings, all absent from fitting. Both scores require complete recovered fact sets. The literal source-blind pattern reader frequently records both a numeric and a qualitative rating for a single phrase. The second score merges price and rating aliases. It can ignore a loss of precision. Neither test checks English grammar, and unrecognized additions can escape both. The rule and its performance on the original human sentences are available for inspection. ## Why predicting ink alone did not settle the problem

When multiple incompatible bitmaps could follow a prefix, a square-error optimum averages their intensities. That can put the prediction between printable marks. Sampling or selecting a complete physical glyph gives the writer a sharp output prior. The remaining challenge is to assign the correct probability to whole sentences under a change of source. Next-glyph likelihood trains on actual human order, but a single most probable local choice can lose a rare place name or extend a completed answer with an unsupported price. The separate listener supervises the inverse relation from human sentence images to their facts. Its scores choose among original complete outputs. It can reject a fluent error only when a better completion exists and its reading discriminates them.

Let a faithful completion be any sentence expressing the requested facts, and let a candidate pool contain the eight completed beams. A faithful winner is guaranteed if one faithful candidate has an ideal score exceeding every unfaithful score by more than twice the maximum error of the learned scorer on that pool. The checked Lean statements also show that irreversible prefix failures telescope to total failure and that disjoint answers to opposite sources require distinguishable conditional output distributions. These are conditional statements about finite decisions; the margin and a uniform error bound have not been shown for this model. The empirical ceiling can be measured directly. Among 1,847 Chinese cards, 1,668 had a merged-faithful candidate. Of 241 final failures, 179 lacked any passing candidate and 62 had one the selector missed. With identical candidates and length normalization the listener recovered 179 earlier misses and lost 26 earlier successes. Improving selection alone leaves most errors intact. ## Related work, cost and next test

PIXAR previously demonstrated autoregressive text generation in pixel space. Work on pixel encoders found that rendering choices affect learned representations. Semantic reranking of sentences also predates this experiment. Our contribution here is a small reproducible visual writer, an optical reverse reader, a matched byte control and a controlled test of what happens to factual content under interventions on the input image. [Tai et al., 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.874/) · [Lotz et al., 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.628/) · [E2E semantic reranking](https://ufal.mff.cuni.cz/~odusek/E2E/final_papers/E2E-ZHAW.pdf)

The image writer consumed 11.51 kJ in 113.0 s, the matched byte writer 11.72 kJ in 115.2 s and the shared listener 1.49 kJ in 12.0 s. The released visual system contains 1,591,210 parameters. Training peaks reported by PyTorch were 186.3 MiB and 116.9 MiB for the image writer and listener. These are allocated tensors, not full device or system memory. Beam and readback generation for 3,138 Chinese cards consumed 4.66 kJ. Training and decoding energy, corpus preparation and downstream reading quality must be compared at equal quality on future tasks. Current evidence does not demonstrate an advantage over the matched byte reader or an 8B general language model. [Writer receipt](evidence/semantic_page_v1/ink-fit.json) · [Listener receipt](evidence/semantic_page_v1/listener-fit.json)

The largest measured loss now comes from the candidate pool. A subsequent comparison should let the writer copy individual observed name glyphs while retaining free composition, use held-out names and contradictory context, and score the complete answer. An independent natural-language Chinese source with human Chinese responses is needed to test Chinese prose generation. Each claim about a new script, font or domain needs its own held data.
