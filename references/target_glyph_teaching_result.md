# Can supervision on the next visible mark improve a translation?

A compact visual writer translated the pictured sentence “Certainly not.” as 当然不是。 on a held passage. It also changed “Of all this, no more need be said.” into an instruction to stop talking. Both are intact outputs from the same checkpoint. Across 192 requests the teacher-guided writer conveyed five whole meanings. The matched writer conveyed four. The difference was too uncertain to establish a gain. ## Experiment

The training data comprised 28,342 parallel passages from 21 works, used in both directions. Two writers started from the same prediction-field checkpoint. They had identical training order, human targets, optimizer schedule and final checkpoint rule. One also learned the minimum probability its text teacher assigned to each observable next glyph or stop at true piece boundaries. The teacher and the reference answer were absent during free generation. We reserved 24 new passage pairs each from *The Great Gatsby*, *Republic*, *Water Margin* and the Bible. The titles had been assessed before. The passages and their optical sides were screened against training, development and earlier assessments. The primary model-based assessment required the complete source meaning in both presentation orders. The paired four-work bootstrap interval for the guided-minus-matched difference was −1.5625 to +3.125 percentage points. The independent gain criterion failed. The judge shares a model family with the text comparator. Its acceptance of only 123 corpus references also limits interpretation of individual decisions. Giving the judge the reference credited two answers for each of the three genuine visual writers. Of the 192 guided images, 168 differ from the matched images. Every writer stopped on every request. ## What the teacher supplied

We decoded the 32 highest-probability text pieces after the true preceding pieces, used verified surface offsets, and pooled choices with the same first visible mark. The unobserved tail, nonadvancing pieces and incompatible choices left some probability unresolved. The resulting floors covered 364,385 English and 318,323 Chinese boundaries per pass, with 82.9% and 75.7% mean resolved mass. For the student probability q below its observed floor l, the loss adds l log(l/q) − l + q. For q at or above l it adds zero. On the finite probability grid, the smallest possible total variation across all admissible teacher completions is exactly the sum of positive deficits. Lean proves attainability as well as the lower bound. The generalized-KL inequality follows separately by monotonicity. Numerical gradient and exhaustive finite checks cover the implementation. [Lean proof](../formal/span_list/OpticalMassCompletion.lean) · [Loss checks](evidence/target_glyph_teaching_v1/loss_checks.json)

Each fit made 2,040 updates over 7,343,256 human glyph or stop positions. The 802,240 fitted parameters belong to a deployed 10,481,570-parameter model with 604,480 additional optical-choice scalars. Each fit took about 24 seconds after teacher preparation, with peak allocation below 4.1 GiB on one RTX 4090 D. This excludes antecedent training, preparing teacher predictions, evaluation and grading. Supplying the true preceding glyphs reduced development negative log likelihood from 1.086 to 1.076 nats per English decision and 3.688 to 3.642 per Chinese decision. Matched-quality efficiency remains unmeasured. [Matched checkpoint](../models/target_glyph_teaching/likelihood/fit.json) · [Guided checkpoint](../models/target_glyph_teaching/guided/fit.json)

Whitespace accounted for 49.7% of the English teacher probability and letters for 14.3%. For Chinese, 0.9% mapped to whitespace and 59.0% to letters or ideographs. At the first bitmap departure of each guided answer, 63/96 English and 84/96 Chinese cases were aligned boundaries. At these boundaries the observed probability certified the reference over the emission only four times in English and eleven times in Chinese. It certified the reverse ordering 22 and 17 times. Some alternative renderings or wordings preserve meaning. The partial one-piece probabilities leave many decisions unresolved. Computing a full character distribution from a pretrained text model involves summing over compatible token histories. ## Which pixels can be reproduced?

All 192 guided answers, all 192 starting-writer answers, all 192 exchanged-source answers and 191 of the 192 likelihood-only controls reproduced byte for byte on CPU, with matching lengths and stops. Control 152 switched between two almost tied choices at glyph 58. The top two GPU scores differed by 0.0002594. The CPU reversed their order and stopped at 62 cells, while the original GPU answer stopped at 79. This persisted at batch size one. The primary grade belongs to the original GPU output. ## Can a longer pictured span preserve the language state?

We fed an inherited translation decoder a vector estimated from the ink of the true preceding target span. We learned occurrence-weighted means from the training passages, with a length-based fallback. The optical source field, decoder and output head were fixed. On 240 paired development passages, we excluded the initial decision in each direction. Agreement with the original decoder's preferred next piece reached 2,866/2,951 (97.1%) in English and 2,808/2,957 (95.0%) in Chinese. Scrambling span vectors within length gave 3.6% and 10.8%. Both prespecified development thresholds passed. Most evaluated spans occurred in training, and the experiment supplied genuine preceding ink and its original segmentation. It measures recovery of a decoder decision under supplied history. [Protocol](optical_span_feedback_protocol.md) · [Scores and controls](evidence/target_glyph_teaching_v1/span_feedback.json)

The next architecture can put a learned visual span reader and writer on either side of a continuous pretrained language backbone. First measure boundary and glyph errors under true history, then give it only the source and its own output. Score the resulting stopped images on fresh passages against the published writer and a text model, and count the inherited body in full. Work on byte-level backbone adaptation and cross-tokenizer distillation motivates the separation of local spelling from global prediction. Our experiment concerns visible glyphs, and a deployed optical writer must learn how to create and delimit its own spans.
