# Matching printed questions across languages

On the reserved works the adapted reader found three exact answers to 46 English questions. Is the question itself sufficient to distinguish the requested fact across scripts? Train a shared visual question encoder directly on human Chinese–English pairs from the same 11,438 retained training questions. Give the control the same English questions exchanged between facts within each original passage. Neither encoder receives page text, answer images, character identities, word boundaries, a translation model or a language identifier. Use fresh weights shared by initialization. Train eight full passes of batches grouped by page, at most 96 examples, with a symmetric contrastive objective and temperature 0.07. Treat identical pictured questions as equivalent in the batch. Keep each final checkpoint. Cap each fit at 20 minutes and 12 GiB allocated on GPU 0.

On all 374 BiPaR development passages compare the two possible one-to-one assignments of two English questions to their Chinese counterparts. Count strict wins, ties and losses, and report per-question retrieval among the whole evaluation set. Run the same comparison on the earlier 23 classical and modern passages as development and report XQuAD separately. Treat aligned pairs as learning a cross-script relation only if the aligned encoder beats the exchanged encoder, the physical length baseline, and chance with the lower 95% Wilson bound above 0.5 on BiPaR. Reading a passage, answering it and generating glyphs are separate measurements. If the test passes, use the fixed bilingual question representation to condition a visual page reader and test on further unseen works. If it fails, measure source-domain and optical errors before attaching it.
