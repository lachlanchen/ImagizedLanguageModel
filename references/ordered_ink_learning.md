# Which information should a visual language model preserve?

Consider two instructions, 「人打虎」 and 「虎打人」. The same three glyphs occur in both. Their order determines who hits whom. If a device adds the three ink planes, or combines them with a pixelwise maximum, the two instructions produce exactly the same measurement. A learner receiving only that measurement has no observation that distinguishes the two events. Under equally likely orders its best accuracy on that choice is one half. A language prior can guess which event is common; the picture cannot tell it which event was written.

For a stream of length $n$ with physical glyph multiplicities $m_1,\ldots,m_k$, ordinary superposition preserves at most the multiset and collapses at least $n!/\prod_i m_i!$ orders. Distinguishing equally likely orders requires at least the logarithm to base two of this count in additional bits. On 834 development article titles, 832 admit more than one physical ordering; the median log count is 4.585 bits. This combinatorial quantity concerns the permutation experiment. Natural-language order has a different distribution. The existing 552 matched Mandarin sentence pairs make the distinction concrete: every pair has the same physical multiset and a different grammatical ordering. A sum-only observer must tie on every pair. A grid retaining cell positions or an ordered stream avoids this loss. There is a second ambiguity in the opposite direction. Suppose the next glyph is equally likely to be any of three distinct nonempty masks whose strokes are disjoint. Under squared error, the optimal prediction for each of those ink pixels is one third. The fitted independent Bernoulli probabilities are also one third. Thresholding the means or taking the factorized joint mode emits a blank even though there are no blank targets. This is an existence proof of a failure mechanism; identifying it in an actual mixture writer requires evidence about that writer's learned conditional density. A joint shape decision followed by stroke rendering can retain alternatives long enough to choose a coherent one. Prior pixel-language work has also reported noisy outputs under maximum likelihood. [PIXAR](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.874/)

A statement about language needs an intervention on meaning. Hold a passage fixed, change the pictured question, decode without a supplied answer, and require both complete pictures to be correct. The measured passage reader and stroke writer pass this test on seven of 3,100 distinct-answer pairs. Repainting an answer in another typeface demonstrates stroke control. It does not identify where answer content came from. Requiring an article title with a glyph missing from the entire pictured prompt blocks even a perfect source-position selector on that example. [Paired answer result](grounded_detail_writing_result.md) · [Composition protocol](visual_title_transducer_protocol.md)

A practical objective can train a source-position distribution and a physical drawing distribution with a shared history. Several occurrences can carry the same answer ink, so a selector should sum their probabilities before taking the logarithm. A learner can then preserve its uncertainty about which source occurrence to follow until the next observation. The likelihood gradient allocates credit to occurrences in proportion to that posterior. This is the same marginalization used for latent alignments in other sequence models. The physical formulation retains new bitmaps and removes any need to allocate an output label per character. The deployment still needs an accurate drawing branch when evidence is absent. These constraints identify which experiments can falsify a proposed design. None implies that a particular hardware budget buys general Chinese competence. A cost comparison with a token model requires the same task, quality, accounted training and inference costs, and independent test conditions.
