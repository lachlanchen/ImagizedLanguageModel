# What a visual writer learned from whole stories and supporting passages

A 2,462,192-parameter writer learned to answer substantially more pictured questions about English stories after bilingual training. The strongest condition presented the entire story twice. Showing a second writer the annotated span for half its English presentations improved answering when a human located the evidence at inference, but impaired independent reading. Neither model produced an exact, stopped answer on any of 116 questions with no accepted answer visible as a continuous string of glyphs. The paper prints a speaker identification that both writers got right and an event explanation both got wrong. ## Corpus and models

The same shuffled schedule presented 92,760 English turns across 6,169 CoQA training stories and 11,865 Chinese C³ questions twice. We excluded reserved stories, duplicates, overlength inputs and training stories sharing any 72-character case-folded whitespace-free window with the CoQA development or DROP validation passages. The query included the most recent human exchange where available. Each training passage contained at most 4,096 aligned cells and the query at most 320. The models started with the same bilingual weights and learned 344,879 parameters with a fixed 2,117,313-parameter optical prior. Each completed 13,080 AdamW updates on one RTX 4090 D. The whole-story fit took 412 seconds and 62.33 kJ of GPU energy, the evidence fit 376 seconds and 50.34 kJ. Peak allocated GPU memory was below 0.9 GiB in each fit. These are incremental costs for the two updates to the inherited writer. [Training records](../models/evidence_views/) · [Corpus receipt](evidence/evidence_views_v1/corpus.json) · [CoQA](https://stanfordnlp.github.io/coqa/) · [C³](https://github.com/nlpdata/c3)

Inputs and outputs use 32 × 32 monochrome cells. The writer has a canonical bank of 5,547 entries, including control values, and can transport additional input bitmaps. It scores intervals, recurrent copying and single-glyph emissions. The bank and cell alignment constrain what it can generate or read. Evaluation starts with passage and question ink and ends with the model's own output and stop. Supplied highlights, teacher-forced prefixes and supplied previous dialogue are identified separately. ## A visible name can pass a word-boundary test

The word-boundary screen retained 154 questions. A retrospective audit checked each human reference against two independent sources, the original story and the visible query, as both a normalized substring and an exact sequence of 32 × 32 bitmaps. Removing questions with any such match left 116. The audit compared the actual emitted cell sequences against those inputs. Every credited output among the 154, including all 19 credited evidence-trained replies with the marked span supplied, occurred verbatim as consecutive glyphs somewhere in the original inputs. A matching run establishes that copying was possible. It does not identify the writer's internal action path. Among the 92,760 verified English training examples, the original word-boundary rule retained 10,678 and the stronger screen retained 9,547. [Pixel audit and per-hit records](evidence/evidence_views_v1/copy-audit.json)

On 24 questions with distinct human alternatives, the local semantic judge accepted 21 human answers and rejected 24 cross-document foils in both presentation orders. The prespecified minimum was 23 of 24 for each. Its model-answer votes therefore cannot establish semantic accuracy. A human paraphrase outside the recorded reference set can still be correct. ## Where evidence helps

Among 398 first turns with the annotated span and an equally long disjoint span available in the first 600 cells, the whole-story control answered 182 from the cropped page, 227 from the marked span, 16 from the disjoint span and 49 with marked ink masked. The evidence-trained writer scored 161, 268, 19 and 49. The matched full-story control was stronger when asked to read independently. On the 154 word-boundary cases, exact with the marked span rose from 10 for the parent to 14 and 19 for the two fits. All three still scored zero on the stricter 116. [Full-story results](evidence/evidence_views_v1/whole-scores.json) · [Evidence-trained results](evidence/evidence_views_v1/highlight-scores.json)

With a fixed 96-cell budget on the 991 paired-study questions with a valid span, the evidence-trained writer answered 305 correctly through its highest-mass window, 107 through a reproducible random window and 406 through a window centred on the annotation. The three windows covered respectively 48.0%, 6.2% and 97.0% of annotated cells on average. Among the 154 multiword-screen cases those answer totals fell to 3, 0 and 7. Localizing evidence and expressing an event in fresh wording present separate difficulties. [Window diagnosis](evidence/evidence_views_v1/window.json)

At a correct output prefix let c denote the target probability under the cropped view, f its probability under the full view and d the allowed drop. If c exceeds one half plus d and c − f is at most d, then the target beats every disjoint rival under the full view. Lean checks this finite-mass result and its contrapositive. At d = 0.10 we tested 965 target positions including stop on 64 real first-turn questions in the 600-cell intervention. Whole-story and evidence training certified 707 and 690 positions respectively. Just two and one answers respectively were certified through their final stop. Greedy decoding produced the selected reference exactly on 18 and 15. A sufficient local margin can be absent even when a prediction is correct. ## Retention and local comparison

On the separate Chinese exam the two fits wrote 10 and 11 of 288 answers, below the earlier Chinese-focused writer's 23. They recovered both historical periods on zero of 64 original Chinese cards. On 256 previously used bilingual QA pages both writers answered both questions on 14. Neither produced eight matching initial continuation cells on any of 128 English or 24 Chinese book prefixes. On 64 paired-study English questions the whole-story writer fell from 22 exact with the standard sans font to 16 with serif. The evidence-trained writer fell from 21 to 13. A fixed local Q4 Qwen3-VL-8B answered 5/32 English questions on the same passage and query glyph images on which the evidence-trained model answered 8/32. Sixteen inputs came from CoQA and sixteen from DROP. The models used different inference implementations. The writer processed the requests serially, taking 2.88 s and 0.252 kJ. The local vision service received up to four simultaneous requests and used 30.32 s and 7.29 kJ. Both scores are low and the sample contains only 16 passages. Earlier bilingual image comparisons favored Qwen. These comparisons do not establish equivalent capability or matched-quality training cost. ## Decision and relation to prior work

Retain both research checkpoints, the better existing bilingual readers and the Chinese specialist. The comparison rejects extra cropped views as a sufficient intervention for independent composition. A subsequent test should teach a writer to express a proposition from the pictured evidence with independent human phrasings. Measure it on distinct documents with unchanged facts, counterfactual substitutions and answers absent from both inputs. Separate training the locator from training the writer. A concrete control keeps the same training questions, answers, image budget and initialization and removes only the proposed composition supervision. Judge natural alternatives with a qualified independent reading and publish fixed-order failures alongside successes.

A training-only preparation step grouped existing E2E English descriptions by the complete annotated restaurant record and CrossWOZ Chinese responses by domain, entity, requested field and database value. It yielded 7,290 English groups with 27,114 distinct phrasings and 846 Chinese groups with 2,462. Case, punctuation and whitespace differences alone did not qualify. With the current 64-decision reply limit, only 450 English groups have two complete answers that fit. A 256-decision budget admits 7,286. All 846 Chinese groups fit at either length. The source labels identify shared facts. Conversational implicatures and extra claims still need semantic screening. These domain-constrained examples are suitable for training and diagnosing a formulation stage, with independent book and QA families required to test transfer. [Training manifest, counts and source hashes](evidence/evidence_views_v1/training-fact-wordings.json) · [Preparation code](../scripts/prepare_grounded_phrasings.py)

Pixel-space generation has precedents. Tai and colleagues trained an autoregressive pixel language model and evaluated visual text generation. More recently Chu and colleagues report, in a preprint, that writing intermediate visual steps improved their canvas reasoning tasks. These studies motivate comparing a directly generated answer with one conditioned on a model-generated intermediate image. Our controlled contribution is the bilingual full-passage comparison, physical copy audit and measured transfer of conditional glyph confidence. Whether intermediate writing improves autonomous paraphrase on these stories remains open.
