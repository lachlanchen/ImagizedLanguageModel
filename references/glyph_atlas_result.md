# From a pictured etymology question to an attested form

A 257,577-parameter optical reader identifies the headword and requested fields from an aligned question strip. The page compositor copies the entry's recorded ink. Among 6,971 distinct indexed headwords, 6,176 belong to training and 795 to held families. A stage with several attested examples contributes one deterministically selected exemplar. The user can inspect its original identifier. Unsupported cells and visually duplicate headwords limit coverage.

On 6,360 fresh two-field questions the original optical reader completed 2,979 replies. Varying the training wording raised this to 4,268 with the same fit budget. Supplying the correct entry to the broader reader raised the total to 6,070. Complete quartets required two headwords in both field orders and passed on 942 of 1,590. The corresponding broadened byte reader completed 2,349 requests. Some correct replies reported a missing field. Among 5,056 requests with an available payload at least once, 3,368 replies were exact. The 795 held headwords were reused in the fresh wording evaluation.

Choosing only among indexed headwords raised complete replies from 3,941 to 5,676 on 6,360 separate requests. Deleting the requested entries exposed 4,770 wrong selections versus 410 under the original rule. The release retains the unconstrained reader with exact lookup. Its answer to the English question about 言 includes J04903, B02975, S01648 and L06958. For 马 the reader returned B14610 and J22129 in the requested order. An unfamiliar question about 丝 retrieved 丂, and an implicit origin question about 言 remained unresolved. This experiment establishes source-grounded access to historical writing. It does not test the invention of an explanation in prose.
