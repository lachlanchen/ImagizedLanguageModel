# Does seeing the shapes of letters buy efficiency?

The released visual writer and the subword Transformer differ in copying, architecture and inherited weights. Isolate the representation with a cold pair. Give two freshly initialized 128-wide recurrent copy-and-spell writers the identical passages, visible space boundaries, trimmed pointers, 89 training letter classes, word feedback, label budgets, rationale targets, seed, AdamW updates and 8,000 batches of 32 turns. Give the visual branch measured 32×32 ink cells and the token branch the original ASCII bytes. Reduce a cell to a normalized 256-component retinal observation or a one-hot byte observation. Apply identical seeded random projections, binding each letter to its position before pooling for a word feature. Preserve endpoint-letter features and length. Both decoders also receive their respective observed letter features when spelling. Use no pretrained network, corpus-derived word embedding or target answer during deployment. Match tree structure and parameter counts. Record independently the time and energy for preparing features and fitting. This evaluates a single-face printable task with known word boundaries. It does not model the cost of parsing an unaligned scan.

Save the 100-, 800-, 2,400- and 8,000-update weights in fixed order. Evaluate every held turn at each point. Compare both exchange controls at the final checkpoint. Report the number of normalized first answers, four-reference scores and new-wording accuracy. Calculate a whole-story paired interval for the final quality difference. Report the earliest measured energy that reaches 10%, 20% and 25% exact accuracy on both splits, or mark unreached. The energy includes feature preparation. A ratio at a common threshold concerns only these two architectures on this corpus. Keep all four checkpoints and fail rather than reschedule if either fit exceeds 50 minutes or 12 GiB of allocated VRAM. Better physical copying alone does not qualify the system as a general language generator.
