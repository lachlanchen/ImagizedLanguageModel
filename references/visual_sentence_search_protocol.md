# Does a complete high-probability glyph stream answer the question?

Two English questions can be matched to their two human reference images on 93 of 96 pages by the retained writer when both references are provided. The same writer freely answered both questions on only two of those pages. Ranking two supplied replies leaves the much larger space of wrong sentences untested.

On the previously observed fourth cohort, keep the learned complete-span reader, fact-strip input and original writer frozen. Compare its published 256-decision greedy replies with free writing under the same model using four live histories per question. Score a complete image by the sum of its conditional log probabilities, including stop. Retain the highest-probability stopped stream encountered, including a possible empty answer. Keep the four most probable live prefixes; carry the largest discarded-prefix log probability forward as an upper bound on every discarded completion. At budget exhaustion, a live prefix cannot yield an admissible stopped answer. Check the selected score against independent teacher-forced evaluation. Report the complete-image and semantic limits of sequence likelihood, the proportion of decisions with a global probability certificate and the upper bound from having access to any completion encountered. The human references are used only to evaluate the frozen outputs. Test every question from both languages, keep all failures, preserve the existing released decoder and reserve any later confirmation for fresh passages.
