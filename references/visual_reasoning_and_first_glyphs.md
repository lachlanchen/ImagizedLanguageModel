# What a glyph writer can learn from visual context

Two physical images can look equally legible while only one completes the sentence. For an ideal probabilistic reader, let Y be the recorded next glyph, L the local pictured context and S the rest of the classical source. The best possible reduction in expected log loss from access to S is the conditional mutual information I(Y;S | L) = H(Y | L) − H(Y | S,L). This follows by taking expectations of the true conditional log probabilities. A learned model's finite-sample score difference also includes approximation and estimation errors. In particular, its gain on two-choice recognition cannot be read as the information content of the language. Multiple valid modern translations can fit the same source.

Writing the energy as E(v | x) = −log p(v | x) makes a practical weakness of the current training objective visible. Reducing E for the reference relative to the old drawing leaves every third drawing free. At 1,089 held-out sites the new preference writer favored the reference signature over its previous wrong drawing. At 336 of these sites its actual new drawing still had higher probability than the reference. Extending training on the same fixed rival misses precisely those competitors. This energy formulation is an identity about probabilities; it supplies no physical law guaranteeing generalization or computational savings. PIXAR established that a causal model can write rendered text patches and that readability is sensitive to training. More recently, Chu and colleagues trained pixel-input, pixel-output models to write successive intermediate states on a canvas, including a 33-million-parameter demonstration on held-out arithmetic and visual tasks. Procedurally defined tasks admit checkable intermediate states. Real Chinese passages have multiple acceptable continuations, no unique intermediate reasoning trace and a much larger set of plausible images. These papers support testing visual computation and evaluating the answer independently of its legibility. Their results do not supply a compute estimate for classical Chinese translation. [PIXAR, ACL Findings 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.874/) · [Chu et al., November 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22820v1)

A more discriminating next test asks whether a reader can tell which of two meanings belongs to each source when their surrounding four glyphs and insertion lengths match. A capable text teacher can be evaluated on the same pairs with the options reordered. This estimates whether an offline training signal is available before spending compute on distillation. A subsequent image-only student would need to use the correct source, draw a complete acceptable answer in new chapters and beat a local count control. Neither a teacher's answer text nor a supplied intermediate translation can be present at student inference. [Teacher feasibility protocol](teacher_context_feasibility.md)
