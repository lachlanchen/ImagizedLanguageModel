# Competing visual answers and the limits of selection

Two compact selectors read the same 32 proposed evidence strips. The first attends to the three pictured states of each proposal independently. The second can attend to any proposal in the list. Both are added to the same frozen signed reader and the same glyph writer. Neither new fit meets the criterion for promotion to the general bilingual baseline. Each new selector has 57,984 trainable parameters and the complete pipeline has 1,135,306. The bank covers all 39,057 original training questions. For 32,387 the 32 locations included an annotated correct span, permitting a supervised gradient. Both heads received 259,096 supported list presentations in eight passes with identical order. Constructing the bank took 11.5 s and 1.71 kJ. The independent fit took 9.3 s and 0.94 kJ, the competing fit 8.4 s and 0.87 kJ on one 4090 D. These costs exclude earlier training, the host and evaluation. Free conversation, long-form answers, prior book continuation and historical selection remain unsolved. Switching off rival attention in the fitted competing model changes 61 selected strips across the 862 questions. On English MLQA it loses one correct selection when rivals are enabled and gains none; on Chinese examinations it gains two and loses one. The independent control is unchanged on all pages. Reversing the candidate order changes scores by at most 0.000001. The checked score-gap inequality bounds when contextual changes can alter the winner. Numerical replay checks the inequality at every changed choice and the corresponding stability certificate on every applicable held question. On 80 questions assessed for meaning with the calibrated two-order Qwen3 8B Q4 judge, the signed parent, independent, competing and Qwen3-VL 8B Q4 models received 17, 20, 17 and 55 credits. All used the same pictured content; the judge received the transcribed question, passage and human reference. Energy during timed inference was 0.70, 0.75, 0.76 and 23.98 kJ respectively. ## What could a better selector recover?

We generated the frozen writer's actual answer for every one of 14,967 distinct question/strip inputs over 476 questions. All 476 claims about the original selected output reproduced under the new batching. On Chinese exam pages, choosing the best strip with hindsight raises the exact-answer ceiling from 56 to 139 of 192. On prior English full statements it raises 15 to 38 of 192. On new story questions no strip generated a verbatim reference for even one of 92 questions. On the first 16, we also assessed all distinct stopped wordings across 105 panels. Nine questions had a reply credited in both orders; two selected replies were credited in this separate panel assessment. Fourteen human controls and fourteen foil controls passed for every panel in the respective question. Both controls passed together on 12 questions, six of which had an accepted candidate. These are automatic semantic ceilings on small samples. Some credited answers have broken grammar, and searching many replies gives the judge more opportunities for false credit. [Exact census](evidence/optical_competition_v1/writer-ceiling.json) · [Accepted wordings and calibration](evidence/optical_competition_v1/story-meaning-ceiling.json)

The resulting experiment optimizes the probability of the complete stopped human reply through the frozen writer. For selector logits, the gradient is the prior minus the evidence posterior given the answer. We prepared 7,096 human questions with 10,060 distinct wordings from 232 training stories. On 64 different training stories, all 2,848 strip/wording paths were scored, and the explicit gradient agreed with autograd within 1e-14. All 64 logit updates improved answer likelihood. Fifty-three had no literal answer candidate. An initial scalar comparison varied the encoder batch shape and exceeded the 0.001-nat tolerance (0.00224). Isolating the recurrence in the same encoded context with full-precision operations reduced maximum drift to 0.000031 nats. This checks numerical learnability independently of the generalization test. The error count has a mechanistic interpretation when the writer and candidate list are fixed. Of 136 exact errors on Chinese exams, 83 could be corrected by choosing another available strip and 53 could not. The corresponding two counts on stories are zero and 92. The partition holds for any deterministic acceptance predicate and any admissible selector. Switching from exact matching to a semantic judgment changes both counts.
