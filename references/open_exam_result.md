# What a Chinese optical writer learns from reading examinations

An open-ended reader wrote the official answer and stopped on 22 of 384 held C³ questions. Before exam training it wrote ten. Training on the same answers and pages with exchanged questions gave sixteen. All three readers answered both questions correctly on zero of 128 held pages with two different questions. The matched checkpoint misses its prespecified adoption criterion. It is available for inference and comparison as an experimental model. ## Training and evaluation

The 344,879-parameter optical reader learns to select a source region, start a copy run, spell from visual glyphs and stop. A frozen 2,117,313-parameter visual prior contributes probabilities. Training sums over paths that can produce the same pixels. The independently deployed system has 2,462,192 neural parameters, a fixed visual palette and a source-copy mechanism. Its supported inputs are aligned 32 × 32 glyph streams, up to 600 cells of passage and 160 of question. A font renders optional typed input. No external language model is called by the writer. The available output shapes come from its physical palette or its input. A generative distribution over glyph streams does not imply the ability to draw an arbitrary unseen glyph from a verbal description. [Architecture](../ilm/visual_lm/recurrent_evidence.py) · [Training code](../scripts/train_open_exam.py)

Both fits completed 2,968 optimizer updates and 47,460 question presentations. Four of the 11,869 training annotations were excluded by the target checks. The exchange is a one-to-one permutation, with 985 identical pictured questions after exchange. Training used a single RTX 4090 D. Matched fitting took 81.23 s and 9.00 kJ, and exchange fitting took 77.58 s and 8.76 kJ. These are incremental costs. They exclude the prior, corpus preparation and evaluation. The question and passage are rendered independently. The hashed, previously unused development sample comprises paired and single pages from both genres. The official test set was untouched. Development screening blocks earlier reserved documents and overlapping training text according to the preparation code. One unsupported cell used a fallback. Source material is restricted to non-commercial research. ## What the answers reveal

On 282 questions with a multi-cell reference absent from the visible passage, both fitted arms wrote the same five references exactly. Those five successes all have the same two-cell answer, 错误 (“false”). Correctly producing one common verdict provides limited evidence of composition. On separately authored counterfactual passages, the matched writer followed a destination change from Beijing to Guangzhou and a purchase change from a dictionary to an atlas. Asked what 言 means on a page defining speech and water, it wrote 河流 (“river”) before and after the gloss for speech changed. Four of twelve authored replies were exact, equalling the parent. Six held exam replies were reproduced from physical PNGs on CPU, including untouched successes and failures. [Authored pages and actual replies](../models/open_exam/examples) · [CPU replay](evidence/open_exam_v1/cpu-replay.json)

The first cell differed from the reference on 329 of 384 questions. Among 206 correct option winners, 185 free answers were wrong. To locate the difference between recognizing and writing, we supplied the common target prefix for both questions on each paired page. On 124 of 128 pairs it was empty. At the first position where the two targets differed, the matched writer preferred each intended next glyph to its sibling rival on 37 pairs, the exchanged writer on seven and the parent on 27. When every other legal glyph also competed in free decoding, the matched model reached both correct prefixes on only one page. The parent reached three and the exchanged control zero. These are post-fit diagnostics. A Lean induction checks the exact equivalence between greedy writing of a bounded target and selecting its correct next cell at every target prefix, including its final stop. The predicate uses the decoder's actual tie rule. A separate sufficient condition uses strict integer score margins. ## When a historical mark moves

On the 128 dictionary questions per language, the matched fit returned the exact requested mark and stopped 33 times in Chinese and once in English. The parent and exchange readers scored zero. All 33 Chinese targets occupied the second slot. In a post-fit intervention we independently exchanged the two period labels and the two specimens in each of 64 entries per language. The target in each edited image follows the label printed beside it. The four arrangements include the authenticated original. In Chinese, 150 of 256 second-slot trials and zero of 256 first-slot trials succeeded. In English, the corresponding counts were zero and two. In neither language did any of the 128 questions pass all four arrangements. The additional four original 言 examples all failed. Reading a period designation and producing a correct historical account remain open. ## Quantized comparison

On the same 24 pictured held questions the matched reader wrote zero exact answers, the parent and exchange controls one each, and Qwen3-VL-8B Q4_K_M three. After warm-up the optical writer read and wrote the PNGs serially in 2.46 s and 0.214 kJ. The vision model answered four concurrent image requests in 22.59 s and 5.35 kJ. Equality to a single reference is a narrow measure, particularly for Qwen's generated text. These timings do not establish efficiency at matched quality. A separate Qwen3-8B meaning judge accepted the human reference in both orderings on 18/24, accepted a distractor in at least one ordering on 3/24, and agreed across orderings on 102/120 decisions. All three calibration requirements failed. We therefore have no qualified semantic accuracy estimate from that judge. The initially discarded benchmark invocation used a case-sensitive model-name check after response collection. The recorded run fixed that check and verified GPU placement.
